# The gate read — does online adaptation beat the frozen floor over one long pass? `[engineering + new runs]` (P1.2.1)

**What this substudy is.** The second P1.2 sub-study and the one that carries leg (a)'s verdict (see the
[Phase 1 overview](index.md)). It drives a pretrained ViT-B over one full pass of BDD100K *train* — every frame new to
the encoder — and asks whether the adapted encoder ends up better than the same encoder frozen. [P1.2.0](P1.2.0.md)
supplies the readout ladder, the few-shot learning rate and the second readout task.

**Verdict: leg (a) passes, on JE only.** Against the bars fixed before any data, **JE PASSes** and **MAE FAILs**. After
one pass over ~56,000 new driving frames, JE's representation carries more linearly decodable information than the
frozen encoder, on both tasks. MAE's carries less, on every rung of both tasks. JE's gain is not uniform across the
ladder, though: it is clear on the linear probe, and on traffic light under fine-tuning, but the scene task's fine-tune
rung shows a small loss. MAE's FAIL triggers [P1.2.2](P1.2.2.md), a pre-registered diagnosis of the MAE recipe.

| Backbone | Primary rung | Scene Δ vs B5 | Cell-balanced traffic light Δ vs B5 | Verdict |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| JE | linear | +0.025 [+0.008, +0.041], p 0.002 | +0.040 [+0.022, +0.061], p 0.0002 | **PASS** |
| MAE | few-shot (*k* = 100) | −0.051 [−0.067, −0.038] | −0.016 [−0.027, −0.005] | **FAIL** (every rung's upper bound ≤ +0.007) |

## Motivation

**Why the project needs this leg.** The project's premise is that a backbone living on a stream should keep learning
from it. If a frozen backbone does as well, there is nothing for the loop to protect, Phase 1 halts, and the project
reframes to selection-for-efficiency. Prior art makes this a live question. RanDumb (NeurIPS 2024) finds random features
*beat* continually learned representations in the single-pass regime, for discriminative continual learning. Memory
Storyboard (2025) finds streaming SSL adaptation *can* beat a frozen start. The SSL case on real driving data is open.

**Why P1.1's runs cannot answer it.** Two reasons.

- **The horizon is too short.** P1.1's drive is 119 steps, about one pass over ~3,500 frames — 5% of what its well
    trained on. On the drive itself the canary did not rise (JE linear 0.629 → 0.618). That says little about whether
    adaptation helps given time.
- **The start is not init-matched.** P1.1's wells trained offline on BDD for ~20 passes before the drive. So "well vs
    frozen-pretrained" mixes offline in-domain warm-up with online adaptation, and leg (a) is about the second.

## Design

### The start: settled heads on the off-the-shelf encoder

Each backbone starts from the off-the-shelf MAE ViT-B/16 encoder with its SSL heads (MAE's decoder, SimSiam's projector
and predictor) trained **with the encoder frozen** on a small reserved slice of train. The encoder at step 0 is
therefore bit-identical to **B5**. That buys three things at once:

- **No separate B5 arm.** B5 is the step-0 reading. It is deterministic, so it carries no seed spread.
- **A settled start.** The heads have bedded in, so the drive does not open with a random head's gradients distorting
    the encoder (the [P1.2.0](P1.2.0.md) motivation). Any dip that remains is a real cost of adaptation.
- **A pure contrast.** Δ(t) = adapted − B5 measures online adaptation and nothing else.

### The drive

- **Substrate.** BDD100K **train**, the three canary scenes (`data.min_canary_count=50`), 224 px, batch 32. The regime
    walk over BDD's driving regimes, single pass, as in P1.1.
- **Reservations from train.** 5% of each regime for the head-settle slice (`stream.well_fraction=0.05`); the in-run
    canary probes, 100 + 100 per (time of day × scene) cell; a selection set, 150 + 150 per scene (only P1.2.2's lr
    check reads it). The drive streams everything else once, ~1,900 steps.
- **Recipe.** P1.1's: AdamW, constant lr, JE 3e-5, MAE 1e-4. The learning rate is pinned, not tuned here; a FAIL
    triggers the follow-up in [P1.2.2](P1.2.2.md).
- **Arms.** Per backbone: the walk × **5 seeds** (the gate arm) and its matched IID control × **3 seeds** (secondary:
    does correlation change Δ?).
- **Logging.** The full P1.1 instrument vector every 25 steps against the head-settled well as anchor, so the corpus
    also serves P1.3 as its first long-horizon healthy reference. Checkpoints at step 100 and at 25%, 50%, 75% and 100%
    of the drive (~26 GB in all).

### The gate's evaluation set: BDD val

Every frame of BDD val is unseen by every arm, so val is the gate's test set. It stays unread by anyone until the gate.

- **Split.** The ~8,750 val images of the three scenes, split by a fixed seed: 40% fit pool, 60% query (~5,250).
- **Tasks.** The scene canary and the task chosen in
    [P1.2.0](P1.2.0.md#4b-the-extended-screen-and-the-input-geometry-added-2026-10-21-before-any-of-its-data) — the two
    are **co-primary**. *Outcome (recorded before any P1.2.1 data):* the first screen admitted no full-frame presence
    task (each was mostly scene in disguise); the extended screen chose **cell-balanced traffic-light presence**
    (`presence_cell:traffic light`). Within every (scene × time-of-day) cell it keeps equal frames with and without a
    traffic light, so scene and time of day carry no information about the label. On B5 it reads 0.60 linear against a
    full-label ceiling of 0.77 — headroom +0.17, three times the scene task's +0.06 under the same protocol. At 224 px
    the lights themselves are sub-patch, so the task most likely reads intersection context — signal poles, overhead
    structure, junction layout — rather than the lamp. That is still a scene-independent visual property a backbone has
    to represent.
- **Pinned scenes.** Train and val both keep only the three canary scenes (`data.scenes`), so the drive's probes and the
    gate set share one label space.
- **Rungs.** The P1.2.0 ladder: kNN, linear, few-shot fine-tune at *k* = 10 and 100 (three paired label draws each, the
    learning rate fixed on B5 in P1.2.0). Read at every saved checkpoint; the gate reads the end of the drive.

### Statistics

For a task and rung, Δ = mean over walk seeds of [balanced accuracy(adapted) − balanced accuracy(B5)], with few-shot
accuracies first averaged over their paired draws. Its uncertainty comes from a **hierarchical bootstrap** (4,000
resamples): seeds with replacement, and query images with replacement within each class. The image level matters here in
a way it did not in P1.1. There, the two arms were near-identical models whose per-image errors mostly coincide. Here,
B5 and the adapted encoder can disagree on many images, and the claim is about BDD at large, not about one probe set.
The one-sided p is the share of resamples with Δ ≤ 0.

### Pass bars (fixed before any data is read)

Per backbone, at the end of the walk:

- **Primary rung.** Linear probe for **JE** (its standard evaluation). Few-shot fine-tune at *k* = 100 for **MAE**
    (MAE's standard evaluation; frozen probes are known to understate it). The other rungs are reported; a frozen-probe
    loss on MAE is stated prominently, not buried.
- **PASS** — on at least one of the two tasks, the primary rung's one-sided p ≤ 0.025 (Holm over the two tasks at a
    one-sided 0.05; equivalently, that task's two-sided 95% interval excludes zero).
- **FAIL** — on both tasks and every rung, the upper 97.5% bound of Δ is below **+0.02**. Every reading rules out a
    2-point gain. (0.02 sits at the canary's power floor; P1.1.0's minimum detectable gap was 0.02–0.045.)
- **INCONCLUSIVE** — anything else.
- *Superseded amendment.* After the first screen, the gate was briefly set to the scene task alone at p ≤ 0.025. The
    extended screen restored a second task, so the two-task rule above applies as originally written.

**Leg (a)** passes if either backbone passes. Which backbone passes says which route stays open for the exit gate: MAE
keeps the primary (forgetting) route, JE the secondary (collapse) route. A pass on JE alone is a finding for P1.3's
plan, and is raised before P1.3 starts.

**What a No-Go looks like.** Both backbones FAIL after the lr check: over a full pass of new driving frames, the frozen
off-the-shelf encoder matches or beats adaptation, on both tasks and every rung, robustly to the learning rate. That is
RanDumb's result carried over to streaming SSL on real data, and it would be recorded plainly.

### Secondary reads (reported, not gating)

- **Δ(t)** across the saved checkpoints: the depth and step of any early loss, and when (if ever) Δ crosses zero.
- **Where the gain or loss sits.** Δ per time-of-day stratum, and, at each checkpoint, Δ split by the regime of the
    query image: the regime the walk is in (does it adapt to what it sees?), regimes it has left (does it forget?), and
    regimes it has not reached (does it transfer?).
- **Correlation's share.** Walk vs IID Δ, 5 vs 3 seeds — descriptive.

### Follow-ups (P1.2.2)

Run only if the gate does not PASS, and only after a pause for the user. On FAIL, a recipe diagnosis; on INCONCLUSIVE,
five more seeds. Both were fixed here before any P1.2.1 data was read; the diagnosis was amended once, also before the
data, to widen it from an lr check to four suspects. Its design now lives in [P1.2.2](P1.2.2.md).

### Cost

Measured at launch: a 1,748-step drive takes ~21 minutes, so 8 drives per card plus the gate ladder come to about 4.5
hours wall on both cards (the design estimate was 10–12 h). Decoded frames are held as uint8 (`data.pixel_dtype`), ~9 GB
per process, and reused across runs from a decode cache (`data.cache_dir`). Checkpoints: ~26 GB.

## Results

All reads on BDD val (5,252 scene query images; 2,958 for the cell-balanced traffic-light task), end of the walk, 5
seeds, Δ = adapted − B5 with its 95% seed × image interval. B5 reads identically in both backbones' ladders (scene
linear 0.635, traffic light 0.606), as it must: both settled-head wells hold the same untouched encoder.

### The gate, every rung

| Task · rung | B5 | JE Δ | MAE Δ |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| scene · kNN | 0.567 | −0.013 [−0.032, +0.006] | −0.056 [−0.072, −0.041] |
| scene · **linear** | 0.635 | **+0.025 [+0.008, +0.041]** | −0.043 [−0.060, −0.026] |
| scene · few-shot *k* = 10 | 0.498 | −0.009 [−0.022, +0.004] | −0.048 [−0.061, −0.036] |
| scene · **few-shot *k* = 100** | 0.662 | −0.012 [−0.023, −0.002] | **−0.051 [−0.067, −0.038]** |
| traffic light · kNN | 0.578 | +0.006 [−0.015, +0.027] | −0.008 [−0.022, +0.005] |
| traffic light · **linear** | 0.606 | **+0.040 [+0.022, +0.061]** | −0.011 [−0.030, +0.007] |

### Over the drive

Δ against B5 at the saved checkpoints (walk, 5 seeds):

| Read | Step 100 | 437 | 874 | 1,311 | End (1,748) |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| JE · scene linear | −0.009 | +0.013 | +0.028 | +0.027 | +0.025 |
| JE · scene kNN | +0.013 | +0.029 | +0.030 | +0.009 | −0.013 |
| JE · traffic light linear | +0.010 | +0.025 | +0.031 | +0.037 | +0.040 |
| MAE · scene linear | −0.021 | −0.039 | −0.044 | −0.044 | −0.043 |
| MAE · scene kNN | −0.032 | −0.040 | −0.048 | −0.055 | −0.056 |
| MAE · traffic light linear | −0.003 | −0.008 | −0.008 | −0.017 | −0.011 |

- **JE builds its gain over the first half of the drive and keeps it on the linear probe.** Its kNN gain peaks at
    mid-drive and is gone by the end.
- **MAE loses fast and then keeps sliding.** It is already 2–3 scene points down by step 100, with settled heads. The
    loss deepens slowly after that and never turns around.

### Correlation and time of day (secondary)

- **The walk vs its IID control.** On the linear and fine-tune rungs the two agree (JE scene linear: walk − IID −0.003
    [−0.016, +0.009]; IID − B5 +0.028 [+0.011, +0.044]). So JE's gain comes from adaptation, not from the order of the
    stream. The one rung where correlation costs is **JE's kNN**: the walk ends 0.030 [0.017, 0.043] below IID on scene.
- **That kNN cost sits at night.** JE's walk − B5 on scene kNN is −0.045 [−0.077, −0.011] on night frames and −0.012
    (n.s.) in daytime. Its traffic-light linear gain is largest at night (+0.051 [+0.025, +0.080]).
- **MAE's loss is everywhere.** Every time-of-day stratum, both orders (IID − B5 on scene few-shot: −0.041).
- **Regime by regime, the read is too coarse.** Split by whether a query frame's regime is behind the walk, current, or
    ahead, the current-regime subsets are small and their intervals span ±0.05–0.10. Nothing separates adapting to the
    current regime from forgetting earlier ones at this power. The full split is in `adaptation_report.json`
    (`secondary.by_regime`).

## Interpretation

### Is JE's pass beyond noise?

**Statistically, yes.** The intervals resample both seeds and query images, so they cover what would change with another
five seeds or another draw of val frames. Both primary-rung intervals clear zero (lower bounds +0.008 and +0.022), and
the test was fixed in advance with a correction for two tasks. The size is modest. Against the headroom P1.2.0 measured
(B5's full-label ceiling minus its linear read, on the train-side pool), it is about 45% of the scene task's small
headroom (+0.025 of +0.056) and a quarter of the traffic-light task's (+0.040 of +0.167).

**As evidence for the premise, it is real but not uniform.** What the pass establishes is narrow: after one pass over
new driving data, JE's frozen features are a better input for a trained linear head than the off-the-shelf features. Two
things it does not establish:

- **A better starting point for fine-tuning, on every task.** On scene, a fine-tuned head starts slightly worse from the
    adapted encoder (−0.012, an interval that excludes zero). On traffic light, it starts better (+0.019).
- **Dominance across the ladder.** kNN is flat on both tasks overall, and correlation costs it at night.

Three caveats bound it further: one recipe and learning rate; one pass; and the 224 × 224 squash, which only the adapted
arm can learn to undo ([P1.2.0](P1.2.0.md#the-readout-task-cell-balanced-traffic-light-presence)).

### kNN or linear: what should the gate measure?

**The primary rungs follow each backbone's standard evaluation, not the kNN.** JE's primary is the linear probe, the
standard read for joint-embedding methods. MAE's is the few-shot fine-tune, because frozen probes understate MAE, which
is meant to be fine-tuned. kNN is primary for neither.

**What the two frozen rungs read differs.** A kNN probe reads the representation's local *geometry*: are same-class
frames each other's nearest neighbours by cosine distance? A linear probe reads *information*: is the class decodable
along some direction, whatever the geometry around it? JE's walk keeps the information (linear holds) while reshaping
the geometry at night (kNN falls). That is the same pattern P1.1.0 found at short horizon, where correlation compressed
JE's projector at no canary cost, and that P1.1.1 called specialization rather than collapse.

**So the kNN loss does not undo the pass, but it matters for the loop.** The pass bar was fixed as written, and it
stands. The project's own selection filter, though, consumes embedding *distances*: novelty and dedup scoring are
nearest-neighbour operations. A backbone whose geometry degrades under correlation feeds the filter worse distances,
even while a trained head would not notice. For P1.3 onward, read both rungs. Linear says whether information survives;
kNN says whether the geometry the filter relies on does.

## Insights

- **The frozen floor is strong, and only one of two backbones clears it.** A pretrained ViT-B at 224 px is hard to beat
    on BDD in one pass. JE clears it modestly. MAE, with this recipe, loses everywhere.
- **MAE's loss is fast, which points at the recipe.** Two to three points within 100 steps, with settled heads, is the
    signature of an optimizer jolt more than of slow drift from the data. P1.2.2 tests that directly. *(P1.2.2 found the
    recipe at fault, but through the decoder, not the optimizer: a 64-d × 2 decoder is too small for ViT-B. Fixed, MAE
    matches B5 but still does not beat it — [P1.2.2](P1.2.2.md).)*
- **Correlation acts on JE's geometry, not its information.** The walk costs JE kNN accuracy at night while the linear
    gain holds. That is a concrete, label-confirmed forgetting lead for P1.3, and it lives on JE, not MAE.

## How to run

The whole substudy is [`artifacts/P1.2.1/run_p121.sh`](artifacts/P1.2.1/run_p121.sh) (two workers, ~4.5 h on two Gaudi2
cards), then the analysis on CPU:

```bash
bash docs/experiments/phase1/artifacts/P1.2.1/run_p121.sh
uv run python scripts/analyze_adaptation.py mode=p121 root=outputs/p121 threads=8
```

Per backbone, the launcher settles the heads
(`scripts/warm_well.py ... warmup.freeze_encoder=true stream.well_fraction=0.05`), drives the walk and the IID control
from that well (`scripts/run_deploy_harness.py ... checkpoint_legs=steps 'checkpoint_steps=[100,0.25,0.5,0.75]'`), and
reads the ladder on val (`scripts/read_adaptation.py ... pool.split=val 'tasks=[scene,"presence_cell:traffic light"]'`).
It expects the decode caches; without them the first run of each split decodes BDD (~35 min for train).
