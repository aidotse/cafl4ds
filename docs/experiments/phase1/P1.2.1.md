# The gate read — does online adaptation beat the frozen floor over one long pass? `[engineering + new runs]` (P1.2.1)

**What this substudy is.** The second P1.2 sub-study and the one that carries leg (a)'s verdict (see the
[Phase 1 overview](index.md)). It drives a pretrained ViT-B over one full pass of BDD100K *train* — every frame new to
the encoder — and asks whether the adapted encoder ends up better than the same encoder frozen. [P1.2.0](P1.2.0.md)
supplies the readout ladder, the few-shot learning rate and the second readout task.

## Motivation

**Why the project needs this leg.** The project's premise is that a backbone living on a stream should keep learning
from it. If a frozen backbone does as well, there is nothing for the loop to protect, Phase 1 halts, and the project
reframes to selection-for-efficiency. Prior art makes this a live question. RanDumb (NeurIPS 2024) finds random features
*beat* continually learned representations in the single-pass regime, for discriminative continual learning. Memory
Storyboard (2025) finds streaming SSL adaptation *can* beat a frozen start. The SSL case on real driving data is open.

**Why P1.1's runs cannot answer it.** Two reasons.

- **The horizon is too short.** P1.1's drive is 119 steps, about one pass over ~3,500 frames — 5% of what its well
    trained on. On the drive itself the canary did not rise (JE linear 0.629 → 0.618). That says little about whether
    adaptation helps given time.
- **The start is not init-matched.** P1.1's wells trained offline on BDD for ~20 passes before the drive. So "well vs
    frozen-pretrained" mixes offline in-domain warm-up with online adaptation, and leg (a) is about the second.

## Design

### The start: settled heads on the off-the-shelf encoder

Each backbone starts from the off-the-shelf MAE ViT-B/16 encoder with its SSL heads (MAE's decoder, SimSiam's projector
and predictor) trained **with the encoder frozen** on a small reserved slice of train. The encoder at step 0 is
therefore bit-identical to **B5**. That buys three things at once:

- **No separate B5 arm.** B5 is the step-0 reading. It is deterministic, so it carries no seed spread.
- **A settled start.** The heads have bedded in, so the drive does not open with a random head's gradients distorting
    the encoder (the [P1.2.0](P1.2.0.md) motivation). Any dip that remains is a real cost of adaptation.
- **A pure contrast.** Δ(t) = adapted − B5 measures online adaptation and nothing else.

### The drive

- **Substrate.** BDD100K **train**, the three canary scenes (`data.min_canary_count=50`), 224 px, batch 32. The regime
    walk over BDD's driving regimes, single pass, as in P1.1.
- **Reservations from train.** 5% of each regime for the head-settle slice (`stream.well_fraction=0.05`); the in-run
    canary probes, 100 + 100 per (time of day × scene) cell; a selection set, 150 + 150 per scene (only P1.2.2's lr
    check reads it). The drive streams everything else once, ~1,900 steps.
- **Recipe.** P1.1's: AdamW, constant lr, JE 3e-5, MAE 1e-4. The learning rate is pinned, not tuned here; a FAIL
    triggers the lr check in [P1.2.2](#follow-ups-p122).
- **Arms.** Per backbone: the walk × **5 seeds** (the gate arm) and its matched IID control × **3 seeds** (secondary:
    does correlation change Δ?).
- **Logging.** The full P1.1 instrument vector every 25 steps against the head-settled well as anchor, so the corpus
    also serves P1.3 as its first long-horizon healthy reference. Checkpoints at step 100 and at 25%, 50%, 75% and 100%
    of the drive (~26 GB in all).

### The gate's evaluation set: BDD val

Every frame of BDD val is unseen by every arm, so val is the gate's test set. It stays unread by anyone until the gate.

- **Split.** The ~8,750 val images of the three scenes, split by a fixed seed: 40% fit pool, 60% query (~5,250).
- **Tasks.** The scene canary and the presence task chosen in [P1.2.0](P1.2.0.md#4-the-secondary-readout-task) — the two
    are **co-primary**. *Outcome of the screen (recorded before any P1.2.1 data):* no candidate passed the overlap rule
    — scene and time of day predict each one at 0.65–0.71 balanced accuracy — so the gate runs on the **scene task
    alone**, as pre-registered. Traffic light, the candidate with the most headroom (+0.13), is read beside it as a
    **non-gating** secondary.
- **Rungs.** The P1.2.0 ladder: kNN, linear, few-shot fine-tune at *k* = 10 and 100 (three paired label draws each, the
    learning rate fixed on B5 in P1.2.0). Read at every saved checkpoint; the gate reads the end of the drive.

### Statistics

For a task and rung, Δ = mean over walk seeds of [balanced accuracy(adapted) − balanced accuracy(B5)], with few-shot
accuracies first averaged over their paired draws. Its uncertainty comes from a **hierarchical bootstrap** (4,000
resamples): seeds with replacement, and query images with replacement within each class. The image level matters here in
a way it did not in P1.1. There, the two arms were near-identical models whose per-image errors mostly coincide. Here,
B5 and the adapted encoder can disagree on many images, and the claim is about BDD at large, not about one probe set.
The one-sided p is the share of resamples with Δ ≤ 0.

### Pass bars (fixed before any data is read)

Per backbone, at the end of the walk:

- **Primary rung.** Linear probe for **JE** (its standard evaluation). Few-shot fine-tune at *k* = 100 for **MAE**
    (MAE's standard evaluation; frozen probes are known to understate it). The other rungs are reported; a frozen-probe
    loss on MAE is stated prominently, not buried.
- **PASS** — on at least one of the two tasks, the primary rung's one-sided p ≤ 0.025 (Holm over the two tasks at a
    one-sided 0.05; equivalently, that task's two-sided 95% interval excludes zero).
- **FAIL** — on both tasks and every rung, the upper 97.5% bound of Δ is below **+0.02**. Every reading rules out a
    2-point gain. (0.02 sits at the canary's power floor; P1.1.0's minimum detectable gap was 0.02–0.045.)
- **INCONCLUSIVE** — anything else.
- **With the scene task alone** (the screen's outcome), PASS keeps the **p ≤ 0.025** threshold — the two-sided 95%
    interval excludes zero — rather than the 0.05 a one-task Holm would allow. Fixed before any data, in the
    conservative direction.

**Leg (a)** passes if either backbone passes. Which backbone passes says which route stays open for the exit gate: MAE
keeps the primary (forgetting) route, JE the secondary (collapse) route. A pass on JE alone is a finding for P1.3's
plan, and is raised before P1.3 starts.

**What a No-Go looks like.** Both backbones FAIL after the lr check: over a full pass of new driving frames, the frozen
off-the-shelf encoder matches or beats adaptation, on both tasks and every rung, robustly to the learning rate. That is
RanDumb's result carried over to streaming SSL on real data, and it would be recorded plainly.

### Secondary reads (reported, not gating)

- **Δ(t)** across the saved checkpoints: the depth and step of any early loss, and when (if ever) Δ crosses zero.
- **Where the gain or loss sits.** Δ per time-of-day stratum, and, at each checkpoint, Δ split by the regime of the
    query image: the regime the walk is in (does it adapt to what it sees?), regimes it has left (does it forget?), and
    regimes it has not reached (does it transfer?).
- **Correlation's share.** Walk vs IID Δ, 5 vs 3 seeds — descriptive.

### Follow-ups (P1.2.2)

Run only if the gate does not PASS, and only after a pause for the user.

- **On FAIL — the lr check.** The walk at ⅓× and 3× the pinned learning rate, 3 seeds each. The learning rate is chosen
    on the **selection set** (train-side, end of drive, the primary rung), never on val. A No-Go for that backbone is
    recorded only if the chosen learning rate's walk also FAILs on val.
- **On INCONCLUSIVE — more seeds.** Five more walk seeds at the pinned learning rate, read once at 10 seeds. Still
    inconclusive → recorded as "unresolved at the canary's power".

### Cost

About 10–12 hours wall on both cards: the head-settled wells, 16 drives of ~1,900 steps with the instrument vector every
25 steps, and the ladder at 5 checkpoints. BDD train at 224 px is ~42 GB as float32, so the source must store decoded
frames as uint8 (~10.5 GB per process).
