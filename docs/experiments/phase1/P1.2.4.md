# Is the gain from the stream, and can it grow? Attribution and widening of the adaptation gain `[engineering + new runs]` (P1.2.4)

*Design doc — a stand-in for the detail doc until the runs land. Bars fixed 2026-10-22, before any of this substudy's
data.*

**What this substudy is.** A P1.2 sub-study run beside [P1.2.2](P1.2.2.md) (see the [Phase 1 overview](index.md)).
[P1.2.1](P1.2.1.md) found that JE, adapted over one pass of BDD, beats the frozen off-the-shelf encoder (**B5**) on the
linear probe: scene +0.025, cell-balanced traffic light +0.040. P1.2.4 asks two questions the project's premise depends
on. Is that gain really from the stream, or would the same training on any images produce it? And can a better recipe
make it larger?

## Motivation

**What rides on the size of the gain.** Leg (a) is an existence gate, and a modest gain clears it. But the project's
value proposition is relative. The loop — filter, health monitor, controller — exists to protect what adaptation adds.
If that is a couple of points, a deployment could reasonably skip the loop and keep the encoder frozen. So the gain must
be large enough to be worth protecting, and attributable to the stream.

**The attribution problem.** B5 is MAE-pretrained, and MAE features are known to be weak under linear probing. JE
training (SimSiam) teaches any encoder augmentation invariance, which tends to make features more linearly separable, on
any images. Three P1.2.1 readings are consistent with JE's gain being that generic effect:

- **It is largest on the linear rung,** exactly where MAE's features are weakest.
- **It vanishes or reverses under fine-tuning on scene,** which can recover separability by itself.
- **The walk and IID controls gain the same.** The stream's order does not matter (though that alone does not show the
    content does not).

If the gain is generic, the honest claim shrinks from "continuous learning beats off-the-shelf" to "SimSiam improves MAE
features". A related reviewer question: does adaptation beat a frozen backbone that was already invariance-trained?

**Not JE-specific in principle.** The attribution question applies to any backbone whose adaptation beats B5. MAE has no
gain to attribute today; if [P1.2.2](P1.2.2.md) confirms an MAE recipe that passes, that recipe joins Part A here.

**Levers left untried.** The JE recipe has had no tuning. Its learning rate came from P1.1.0's scout, which the
fresh-head artifact confounded ([P1.2.0](P1.2.0.md)). It runs a constant rate, no warmup, no weight averaging, and the
most minimal JE method. And JE's kNN gain peaks mid-drive and is gone by the end — the shape a decaying schedule or
weight averaging is meant to keep.

## Design

### Part A — Attribution

**A1. The off-domain control.** JE trained exactly as in P1.2.1's IID drive — the same settled-head start, batch 32, lr
3e-5, 1,748 steps — but on **non-driving images**, then read on BDD val beside the BDD-trained arms.

- *Data:* **Imagenette** (`/mnt/imagenette/imagenette2-320`, ~9.5k train images, full resolution). It is a subset of
    ImageNet, the domain both pretrained backbones already know, so training on it teaches the objective's invariance
    and no new domain content. At 1,748 steps it is ~6 passes. Repetition probably gains *less* than new frames would,
    which favours a "stream-specific" call; so a GENERIC verdict below is robust and a STREAM-SPECIFIC one carries that
    caveat.
- *Secondary, one seed:* **STL-10 unlabelled** (100k images, single pass, but 96 px upsampled to 224). Its blur cuts the
    other way, so the two bracket the effect.
- *Seeds:* 3, matching P1.2.1's IID control, which is the comparison arm (both are shuffled, unordered streams).

**A2. Stronger frozen floors.** Two invariance-trained backbones, read frozen on P1.2.1's val ladder beside B5:

- **DINO ViT-B/16** (`encoder=vit_b16_dino`) — a self-supervised joint-embedding backbone, the natural "best
    off-the-shelf" for this comparison.
- **ImageNet-supervised ViT-B/16** (`encoder=vit_b16_pretrained`, torchvision).

All three are read with the same mean-pooled patch embedding. DINO was trained on its `cls` token, so this pooling may
understate it slightly — a caveat in favour of adaptation.

**A3. The realistic test, if the floor moves.** If frozen DINO reads at or above adapted JE (P1.2.1) on JE's primary
rung, the deployment-relevant question becomes init-matched again: does adapting *DINO* beat frozen DINO? Then P1.2.1's
gate is re-run with DINO as the init — heads settled on frozen DINO, the walk × 5 seeds, the same val ladder and the
same bars, with frozen DINO as B5.

### Part B — Widening (JE)

All arms are the walk, from P1.2.1's settled-head JE well. They are chosen on the train-side **selection set**, never on
val.

**B1. Weight interpolation — no training.** θ(α) = (1 − α)·θ_B5 + α·θ_adapted, encoder weights only, for α ∈ {0.25, 0.5,
0.75}, on each of P1.2.1's five walk-end checkpoints. Interpolating fine-tuned and pretrained weights often beats both
endpoints (WiSE-FT, Wortsman et al. 2022). It also guards against forgetting, which suits the project's own story.

**B2. Schedule and averaging,** one seed each: a 10% linear warmup then cosine decay to zero; and an exponential moving
average of the weights (decay 0.999), read from the average.

**B3. Learning rate and fine-tuning protections,** one seed each: ⅓× and 3× the pinned 3e-5; and layer-wise lr decay
(0.75 per block) with no weight decay on norms, biases, position embeddings and the `cls` token. (Shared with P1.2.2's
arms.)

**Combination.** If two levers each help on the selection set, their combination is one more arm. B1 can stack on any
arm, post hoc.

**Choosing.** The best arm by the selection set's scene linear read (JE's primary rung) at the end of the drive, with α
chosen the same way when B1 stacks. Recorded in this doc before the confirmation.

**Confirmation.** The chosen recipe × 5 seeds, read once on val against P1.2.1's bars as written (and against the
strongest frozen floor, below).

### Pass bars (fixed before any of this substudy's data)

All on the primary rung (JE: linear), both tasks, on BDD val, with seed × image intervals. Adapted-vs-adapted contrasts
resample both arms' seeds (a two-sample bootstrap; engineering below).

**Attribution (A1).** The contrast is the stream gain over the off-domain gain, D = Δ_BDD-IID − Δ_off.

- **STREAM-SPECIFIC** — D > 0 with one-sided p ≤ 0.025 on either task (Holm over two at 0.05).
- **GENERIC** — on both tasks, D's upper 97.5% bound is below +0.01: the off-domain arm gets essentially all of the
    gain.
- **MIXED** — anything else. The share D / Δ_BDD-IID is reported either way.

**Against the strongest floor (A2, confirmation).** The floor is, per task, whichever frozen backbone reads highest on
the primary rung — chosen after reading, which only makes the test harder.

- **BEATS THE BEST FLOOR** — the confirmed recipe's Δ against it has one-sided p ≤ 0.025 on either task (Holm).
- Otherwise, **DOES NOT**; and if DINO is the floor that wins, A3 runs.

**The claim P1.2 can make** combines the two:

| Attribution | Against the best floor | Claim |
| -- | -- | -- |
| STREAM-SPECIFIC | beats it | **Strong** — continuous learning on the stream beats the best off-the-shelf backbone tried |
| STREAM-SPECIFIC | does not | **Qualified** — the stream adds value over its own init, but a better frozen start matches it (A3 then decides) |
| GENERIC or MIXED | beats it | **Qualified** — beats every frozen floor, but mostly through the objective, not the stream |
| GENERIC | does not | **Weak** |

**What a Weak claim means.** It is not by itself a No-Go: leg (a) passed as pre-registered. It is a finding the project
must face, and it is carried forward with an explicit scale caveat. One pass over ~56k frames at 224 px, against
ImageNet-scale pretraining, may simply be too little new data, or too small a domain shift, for stream-specific value to
show. The longer horizon (BDD's driving videos, in P1.3) and the larger shift (ZOD, Phase 4) are where that is tested.
It is raised with the user before P1.3 starts, beside the plan's own reframe (selection-for-efficiency).

### Engineering

Shared with P1.2.2: the lr schedule in the deploy harness; optimizer parameter groups (layer-wise decay, no-decay
group); selection-set reads in the reader (`pool.kind=selection`). New here:

- **An off-domain source.** An `AttributeSource` over an image folder (Imagenette) and over STL-10's unlabelled split,
    one regime, no canary. The resumed well's split fingerprint will not match a different source, so a deliberate
    override (`resume_check_split=false`) for this one use.
- **Weight averaging.** An EMA shadow in the drive, saved in its checkpoints.
- **An interpolated encoder in the reader:** `kind: interp` with `a`, `b` and `alpha`.
- **A two-sample bootstrap** for adapted-vs-adapted contrasts (both arms' seeds resampled, images paired).
- **Floors:** `encoder=vit_b16_dino` (added; weights at `/home/mauricio/data/weights/`, mount that directory) and
    `encoder=vit_b16_pretrained` (mount `~/.cache/torch`).

### Cost

A1: 3 + 1 drives (~1.5 h one card). A2: three frozen reads (~20 min). B1: 15 interpolated encoders through the
selection-set and val reads (~1 h). B2–B3: ~5 one-seed drives (~2 h). Confirmation: 5 seeds + the val ladder (~2.5 h).
A3 if triggered: heads + 5 seeds + ladder (~2.5 h). About 8–10 h of card time; run beside P1.2.2, one card each, ~10 h
wall for both.
