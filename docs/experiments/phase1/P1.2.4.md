# Is the gain from the stream, and can it grow? Attribution and widening of the adaptation gain `[engineering + new runs]` (P1.2.4)

**What this substudy is.** A P1.2 sub-study run beside [P1.2.2](P1.2.2.md) (see the [Phase 1 overview](index.md)).
[P1.2.1](P1.2.1.md) found that JE, adapted over one pass of BDD, beats the frozen off-the-shelf encoder (**B5**) on the
linear probe: scene +0.025, cell-balanced traffic light +0.040. P1.2.4 asks two questions the project's premise depends
on. Is that gain really from the stream, or would the same training on any images produce it? And can a better recipe
make it larger?

**Verdict: Strong — the gain comes from the driving content, and it beats the best frozen backbone tried.** Both
pre-registered reads pass.

- **Attribution: STREAM-SPECIFIC against both controls.** "Stream-specific" means specific to the stream's *content*
    (driving images), not its order: P1.2.1's walk and shuffled IID drive gain alike, and the controls are matched to
    the IID one. The same JE training on 56k non-driving images gains much less than on BDD. COCO recovers almost none
    of the gain. Places365, the strict control (other outdoor scenes), recovers 40–55% of it, so the stream-specific
    margin over it is narrower.
- **Widening: a better recipe grows the gain and broadens it.** With fine-tuning protections (layer-wise lr decay, no
    weight decay on norms and biases), JE's scene linear gain rises from +0.025 to +0.038. The kNN and few-shot rungs,
    flat or negative under P1.2.1's recipe, turn positive.
- **Against the best floor: BEATS IT on scene.** Frozen DINO is the strongest floor on both tasks. The confirmed recipe
    beats it on scene (+0.030, p 0.0005) but not on traffic light (+0.013, interval spans zero).

## Motivation

**What rides on the size of the gain.** Leg (a) is an existence gate, and a modest gain clears it. But the project's
value proposition is relative. The loop — filter, health monitor, controller — exists to protect what adaptation adds.
If that is a couple of points, a deployment could reasonably skip the loop and keep the encoder frozen. So the gain must
be large enough to be worth protecting, and attributable to the stream.

**The attribution problem.** B5 is MAE-pretrained, and MAE features are known to be weak under linear probing. JE
training (SimSiam) teaches any encoder augmentation invariance, which tends to make features more linearly separable, on
any images. Three P1.2.1 readings are consistent with JE's gain being that generic effect:

- **It is largest on the linear rung,** exactly where MAE's features are weakest.
- **It vanishes or reverses under fine-tuning on scene,** which can recover separability by itself.
- **The walk and IID controls gain the same.** The stream's order does not matter (though that alone does not show the
    content does not).

If the gain is generic, the honest claim shrinks from "continuous learning beats off-the-shelf" to "SimSiam improves MAE
features". A related reviewer question: does adaptation beat a frozen backbone that was already invariance-trained?

**Not JE-specific in principle.** The attribution question applies to any backbone whose adaptation beats B5. MAE has no
gain to attribute today; if [P1.2.2](P1.2.2.md) confirms an MAE recipe that passes, that recipe joins Part A here.

**Levers left untried.** The JE recipe has had no tuning. Its learning rate came from P1.1.0's scout, which the
fresh-head artifact confounded ([P1.2.0](P1.2.0.md)). It runs a constant rate, no warmup, no weight averaging, and the
most minimal JE method. And JE's kNN gain peaks mid-drive and is gone by the end — the shape a decaying schedule or
weight averaging is meant to keep.

## Design

### Part A — Attribution

**A1. The off-domain controls.** JE trained exactly as in P1.2.1's IID drive, but on **non-driving images**, then read
on BDD val beside the BDD-trained arms. The comparison arm is P1.2.1's IID control: both are shuffled, unordered
streams. Everything but the images' content is matched to it:

| Factor | BDD IID drive (P1.2.1) | Each off-domain arm |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Start | settled-head JE well | the same well |
| Recipe | batch 32, lr 3e-5, AdamW, constant | the same |
| Horizon | 1,748 steps, ~56k unique frames, one pass | the same: ~56k unique images, one pass |
| Resolution | full-res → 224 | full-res (COCO) or 256 px (Places) → 224 |
| Geometry | 16:9 squashed to 1:1 (1.78× horizontally) | the same net distortion (below) |
| Seeds | 3 | 3 |

Two controls, each the best available for a different skeptic. The attribution claim must survive both. (An earlier
draft used Imagenette and STL-10, which happened to be on disk; they were dropped before any data because they break the
match — Imagenette is ~9.5k images, so ~6 passes, and STL-10 is 96 px.)

- **COCO train2017 — the matched control.** 118k full-resolution, object-centric natural images. Street content is
    removed by label: any image with a `bicycle`, `car`, `motorcycle`, `bus`, `train`, `truck`, `traffic light`,
    `fire hydrant`, `stop sign` or `parking meter` annotation is excluded. 56k are drawn at random (seed 0) from the
    rest. Geometry: centre-crop each image to 16:9, then squash to 224 × 224, exactly as a BDD frame.
- **Places365-Standard — the strict skeptic.** 1.8M scene-centric images. BDD frames are whole scenes and one readout
    task *is* the scene, so the hardest version of "any images would do" is *other outdoor scenes*. Excluded categories
    (road, traffic and vehicle scenes, plus the three that correspond to BDD's scene labels): `alley`, `auto_factory`,
    `auto_showroom`, `bridge`, `bus_interior`, `bus_station/indoor`, `car_interior`, `crosswalk`, `desert_road`,
    `downtown`, `driveway`, `field_road`, `forest_road`, `garage/indoor`, `garage/outdoor`, `gas_station`, `highway`,
    `industrial_area`, `loading_dock`, `motel`, `parking_garage/indoor`, `parking_garage/outdoor`, `parking_lot`,
    `racecourse`, `railroad_track`, `residential_neighborhood`, `street`, `train_interior`, `train_station/platform`,
    `viaduct`. 56k drawn at random (seed 0) from the remaining 335 categories. Geometry: the 256 × 256 version is
    already squashed from mostly 4:3 originals, so a centre crop of 256 × 192 then a squash to 224 × 224 gives the same
    net 16:9 → 1:1 distortion. That is exact for 4:3 originals and approximate otherwise.

Not chosen: ImageNet-1k would be the purest "no new data" control (both pretrained backbones saw it), but it is gated;
OpenImages is more diverse than COCO at a much heavier download, for the same question.

*Licences:* COCO annotations are CC BY 4.0, its images under their Flickr licences; Places365 is for non-commercial
research and education. Both are used here for research only, as decode caches on the Gaudi node.

**A2. Stronger frozen floors.** Two invariance-trained backbones, read frozen on P1.2.1's val ladder beside B5:

- **DINO ViT-B/16** (`encoder=vit_b16_dino`) — a self-supervised joint-embedding backbone, the natural "best
    off-the-shelf" for this comparison.
- **ImageNet-supervised ViT-B/16** (`encoder=vit_b16_pretrained`, torchvision).

B5 and DINO are read with the same mean-pooled patch embedding. DINO was trained on its `cls` token, so this pooling may
understate it slightly — a caveat in favour of adaptation. The supervised ViT-B is read on its `cls` token, its native
readout and the only one torchvision's encoder exposes. That favours the floor. (Corrected 2026-10-22, before any floor
data: an earlier draft said all three were mean-pooled.)

**A3. The realistic test, if the floor moves.** If frozen DINO reads at or above adapted JE (P1.2.1) on JE's primary
rung, the deployment-relevant question becomes init-matched again: does adapting *DINO* beat frozen DINO? Then P1.2.1's
gate is re-run with DINO as the init — heads settled on frozen DINO, the walk × 5 seeds, the same val ladder and the
same bars, with frozen DINO as B5.

### Part B — Widening (JE)

All arms are the walk, from P1.2.1's settled-head JE well. They are chosen on the train-side **selection set**, never on
val.

**B1. Weight interpolation — no training.** θ(α) = (1 − α)·θ_B5 + α·θ_adapted, encoder weights only, for α ∈ {0.25, 0.5,
0.75}, on each of P1.2.1's five walk-end checkpoints. Interpolating fine-tuned and pretrained weights often beats both
endpoints (WiSE-FT, Wortsman et al. 2022). It also guards against forgetting, which suits the project's own story.

**B2. Schedule and averaging,** one seed each: a 10% linear warmup then cosine decay to zero; and an exponential moving
average of the weights (decay 0.999), read from the average.

**B3. Learning rate and fine-tuning protections,** one seed each: ⅓× and 3× the pinned 3e-5; and layer-wise lr decay
(0.75 per block) with no weight decay on norms, biases, position embeddings and the `cls` token. (Shared with P1.2.2's
arms.)

**Combination.** If two levers each help on the selection set, their combination is one more arm. B1 can stack on any
arm, post hoc.

**Choosing.** The best arm by the selection set's scene linear read (JE's primary rung) at the end of the drive, with α
chosen the same way when B1 stacks. Recorded in this doc before the confirmation.

**Confirmation.** The chosen recipe × 5 seeds, read once on val against P1.2.1's bars as written (and against the
strongest frozen floor, below).

### Pass bars (fixed before any of this substudy's data)

All on the primary rung (JE: linear), both tasks, on BDD val, with seed × image intervals. Adapted-vs-adapted contrasts
resample both arms' seeds (a two-sample bootstrap; engineering below).

**Attribution (A1).** For each control *c*, the contrast is the stream gain over that control's gain, D_c = Δ_BDD-IID −
Δ_c. Per control:

- **STREAM-SPECIFIC** — D_c > 0 with one-sided p ≤ 0.025 on either task (Holm over two at 0.05).
- **GENERIC** — on both tasks, D_c's upper 97.5% bound is below +0.01: that control gets essentially all of the gain.
- **MIXED** — anything else. The share D_c / Δ_BDD-IID is reported either way.

The attribution verdict is the **weaker of the two**: STREAM-SPECIFIC only if it holds against both COCO and Places;
GENERIC if either control is GENERIC; MIXED otherwise. If the two disagree, that is itself informative: COCO
STREAM-SPECIFIC with Places GENERIC says the value lies in *scene-level* images, not in driving as such.

**Against the strongest floor (A2, confirmation).** The floor is, per task, whichever frozen backbone reads highest on
the primary rung — chosen after reading, which only makes the test harder.

- **BEATS THE BEST FLOOR** — the confirmed recipe's Δ against it has one-sided p ≤ 0.025 on either task (Holm).
- Otherwise, **DOES NOT**; and if DINO is the floor that wins, A3 runs.

**The claim P1.2 can make** combines the two:

| Attribution | Against the best floor | Claim |
| -- | -- | -- |
| STREAM-SPECIFIC | beats it | **Strong** — continuous learning on the stream beats the best off-the-shelf backbone tried |
| STREAM-SPECIFIC | does not | **Qualified** — the stream adds value over its own init, but a better frozen start matches it (A3 then decides) |
| GENERIC or MIXED | beats it | **Qualified** — beats every frozen floor, but mostly through the objective, not the stream |
| GENERIC | does not | **Weak** |

**What a Weak claim means.** It is not by itself a No-Go: leg (a) passed as pre-registered. It is a finding the project
must face, and it is carried forward with an explicit scale caveat. One pass over ~56k frames at 224 px, against
ImageNet-scale pretraining, may simply be too little new data, or too small a domain shift, for stream-specific value to
show. The longer horizon (BDD's driving videos, in P1.3) and the larger shift (ZOD, Phase 4) are where that is tested.
It is raised with the user before P1.3 starts, beside the plan's own reframe (selection-for-efficiency).

### Engineering

Shared with P1.2.2: the lr schedule in the deploy harness; optimizer parameter groups (layer-wise decay, no-decay
group); selection-set reads in the reader (`pool.kind=selection`). New here:

- **Off-domain sources.** An `AttributeSource` for each control, one regime, no canary, holding 56k images as uint8 in a
    decode cache: COCO read straight from `train2017.zip` with the label filter above; Places365 read from its tar (only
    the selected members) with the category filter. Each applies its geometry rule above, and is unit-tested on a
    synthetic folder. The resumed well's split fingerprint will not match a different source, so a deliberate override
    (`resume_check_split=false`) for this one use.
- **Weight averaging.** An EMA shadow in the drive, saved in its checkpoints.
- **An interpolated encoder in the reader:** `kind: interp` with `a`, `b` and `alpha`.
- **A two-sample bootstrap** for adapted-vs-adapted contrasts (both arms' seeds resampled, images paired).
- **Floors:** `encoder=vit_b16_dino` (added; weights at `/home/mauricio/data/weights/`, mount that directory) and
    `encoder=vit_b16_pretrained` (mount `~/.cache/torch`).

### Cost

A1: 2 controls × 3 drives (~2.2 h one card), plus building their caches. A2: three frozen reads (~20 min). B1: 15
interpolated encoders through the selection-set and val reads (~1 h). B2–B3: ~5 one-seed drives (~2 h). Confirmation: 5
seeds + the val ladder (~2.5 h). A3 if triggered: heads + 5 seeds + ladder (~2.5 h). About 8–10 h of card time; run
beside P1.2.2, one card each, ~10 h wall for both.

## Interpretation

### Why the attribution holds, and where it is thin

**The concern was that any images would do.** SimSiam training teaches augmentation invariance, and MAE features are
weak under linear probing, so JE might have gained on any data. The controls rule that out for most of the gain. COCO —
the same number of natural images, the same horizon and the same geometry — moves the linear probe by +0.003 and +0.009,
against BDD's +0.028 and +0.039. So the objective alone is not what helps.

**Places365 is the harder control, and it takes a real share.** Outdoor scenes without roads still recover about half of
the traffic-light gain and 40% of the scene gain. That is the expected gradient: the closer the images are to driving
scenes, the more of the gain they carry. The upshot is that part of what JE learns on BDD is "outdoor scene structure",
which any scene-centric data teaches. The stream-specific remainder is significant on both tasks, but its scene interval
just touches zero.

**One caveat about which recipe was attributed.** The controls were matched to P1.2.1's recipe, the one whose gain was
in question. The confirmed recipe (with protections) was not re-run on the controls. Its larger gain is attributed to
the stream only by analogy.

### What widening bought

**The protections are the lever, and they act most where P1.2.1 was weakest.** The linear gain grows modestly (scene
+0.025 → +0.038). The larger change is breadth: kNN and few-shot *k* = 100 flip from flat-or-negative to positive on
scene, and the night kNN loss disappears. Keeping the early blocks close to B5 (layer-wise lr decay) and stopping weight
decay from shrinking norms and biases preserves the pretrained geometry, while the top blocks still adapt.

**Interpolation (WiSE-FT) helps the base recipe, but not the protected one.** On the selection set, mixing P1.2.1's
checkpoints back toward B5 lifts them (α = 0.25: 0.612 against 0.571 for α = 1.0). Stacked on the protections arm it
does nothing (α = 1.0 is best). Both are ways of staying near the pretrained weights, and doing it once is enough.

**Schedule and averaging did not help** on the selection set's linear read. A cosine decay and the EMA did lift kNN
there (0.55 and 0.51 against 0.45), echoing P1.2.1's mid-drive kNN peak, but they were not chosen on the primary rung.

### Against the strongest floors

**Adapted JE beats frozen DINO on scene, not on traffic light.** On the traffic-light task, both DINO and the supervised
ViT-B start ~2 points above B5, and adaptation's +0.031 over B5 leaves only +0.013 over DINO. The kNN rung is where the
floors are strongest (supervised ViT-B: scene 0.665, traffic light 0.637, against the confirmed recipe's 0.597 and
0.600). So for a deployment that reads embedding distances directly — as the project's selection filter does — a frozen
supervised or DINO backbone is still the better off-the-shelf start. Whether adapting *those* would beat them is the
question A3 would have asked; it did not trigger on the pre-registered rung.

### What it means for P1.2's claim

By the claim table, STREAM-SPECIFIC and BEATS THE BEST FLOOR is the **Strong** claim: continuous learning on the stream
beats the best off-the-shelf backbone tried. Its honest scope is narrower than the words:

- the linear probe, on the scene task (on traffic light it beats B5 but not DINO);
- a stream-specific margin that is thin against outdoor scenes (Places365);
- one pass over ~56k frames at 224 px.

## Insights

- **The stream's content matters, not just the objective.** Matched non-driving images recover a fifth or less of the
    gain (COCO); scene-centric ones recover about half (Places365).
- **Fine-tuning protections widen JE's gain and repair its geometry.** They turn kNN and few-shot gains positive and
    remove P1.2.1's night kNN loss. For P1.3 this matters twice: it is the better recipe, and it weakens the forgetting
    lead P1.2.1 found (that lead was measured under P1.2.1's recipe).
- **Frozen DINO and supervised ViT-B are stronger floors than B5,** most of all on kNN. The project's B5 is the
    init-matched floor, but a deployment choosing a frozen backbone would not choose MAE.
- **The selection set is noisy at one seed.** A repeat of P1.2.1's recipe read 0.018 below its first run there, so close
    arms are not separable. The big effects (decoder for MAE, protections for JE) survived the val confirmation.

## How to run

The launchers above, in that order (about 1 h for A2 and B1–B3 on one card; ~2 h for the confirmation and its read; ~1 h
per control's three drives). The off-domain sources need their archives the first time, to build the caches: `data=coco`
/ `data=places365` with `data.cache_dir=...`, mounting `/home/mauricio/data`. Each control drive is P1.2.1's IID command
with
`data=<control> stream.support_per_canary=10 stream.query_per_canary=10 stream.stratify_canary=false resume_check_split=false`.
Then, on CPU:

```bash
uv run python scripts/analyze_adaptation.py mode=p121 root=outputs/p124/confirm 'backbones=[je]' n_iid_seeds=0
uv run python scripts/analyze_adaptation.py mode=p124 root=outputs/p124 n_iid_seeds=3 \
  p124.confirm_ladder=outputs/p124/confirm/ladder/je \
  +p124.floors.dino=outputs/p124/floors/dino +p124.floors.sup=outputs/p124/floors/sup
```
