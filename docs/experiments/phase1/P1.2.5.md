# Does the gain survive a strong start, and when does it pay? DINO-init adaptation, the time-to-payoff curve and a wider task panel `[engineering + new runs]` (P1.2.5)

**What this substudy is.** The last P1.2 sub-study, following [P1.2.4](P1.2.4.md) (see the
[Phase 1 overview](index.md)). Until here, P1.2's evidence that adaptation beats the frozen floor rested on one start:
the MAE-pretrained ViT-B, continued with a joint-embedding objective ("MAE-init JE"). That start is weak under linear
probes, which flatters any adaptation. P1.2.5 asks the question the project actually has to answer: **in a correlated
driving stream, does adapting the best available start on the stream beat keeping that start frozen — and after how much
of the stream?**

**Verdict: Qualified, by the pre-registered table — adapting a strong start on the stream beats keeping it frozen. The
gain is firm on the two gate tasks, about 4 points of linear accuracy on scene, and narrow beyond them.** Three arms
ran, each against its own frozen start on BDD val; Part G then re-chose DINO-init SimSiam's learning rate on three seeds
and ran both joint-embedding starts without the fine-tuning protections.

- **DINO-init SimSiam passes its gate.** DINO's weights continued with SimSiam gain on scene and traffic light. Generic
    images do not reproduce the gain, so it comes from the domain's data. Read at Part G's choice (the protections at lr
    9e-5), the gain is +0.044 on scene, and the `cls` readout no longer reverses it. It is narrow: 0 of 4 panel tasks by
    the bootstrap, which decides; the seed-level test counts 3 of 4, so the two disagree on breadth. The panel could
    call a gain of about 2 points per task; three tasks gain 1.0–1.7, just below what it resolves. Part A's recipe (lr
    3e-5) gained half as much, on 1 of 4 panel tasks and none of 15 in the wider exploratory panel (Part F). The higher
    rate costs geometry: scene kNN ends 5.5 points below the frozen start.
- **The protections protect the geometry, not the gate tasks' linear gain** (Part G). Without them both joint-embedding
    starts still pass, with the same linear gain on the gate tasks. What the protections buy is kNN (+10.6 points on
    scene for DINO-init SimSiam), fine-tuning (+5.9 at 100 shots) and the large-vehicle task. Without them, DINO-init
    SimSiam's linear gain reaches three small-object tasks in Part F's exploratory panel, at a kNN loss on six.
- **MAE-init JE passes again, smaller than in P1.2.4.** P1.2.4's confirmed recipe, repeated under this substudy's
    conditions, keeps its PASS. Its linear gain shrinks from +0.038 to +0.024 on scene. Its kNN gain reaches P1.2.4's
    size mid-drive but is gone by the end. With the protections its attribution reads MIXED; without them, under the
    same conditions, STREAM-SPECIFIC, as in P1.2.4. So the recipe, not the conditions, moved the verdict, though task by
    task each recipe keeps a driving-specific remainder on one gate task (Part G). Its linear gain is the broadest of
    the arms: 2 of 4 panel tasks, and 5 of 15 in Part F, on objects smaller than one patch.
- **DINO's own objective is INCONCLUSIVE.** No linear gain, but gains on the kNN and fine-tune rungs. BDD's frames are
    so alike that DINO's centring flattens its own training target (Part E).
- **Within one pass, the linear gain carries across regimes and is not given back. Under SimSiam's objective the kNN
    rung gains early and erodes late** (Parts B and F), so the end-of-drive kNN read sees none of its gain. The
    fine-tune rungs, read at every checkpoint (Part G), follow kNN on MAE-init JE and the linear rung on DINO-init
    SimSiam.

| Arm | Scene Δ (linear) | Traffic light Δ | Gate | Panel (C) | Wider panel (F, exploratory) |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| DINO-init SimSiam, lr 9e-5 (Part G's choice) | +0.044 (+0.029, +0.059) | +0.023 (+0.004, +0.041) | PASS | 0 of 4 (seed-level: 3) | 1 of 15 |
| DINO-init SimSiam, lr 3e-5 (Part A's choice) | +0.023 (+0.007, +0.038) | +0.016 (−0.002, +0.035) | PASS | 1 of 4 | 0 of 15 |
| MAE-init JE | +0.024 (+0.009, +0.039) | +0.022 (+0.003, +0.040) | PASS | 2 of 4 | 5 of 15 |
| DINO-init DINO | +0.009 (−0.005, +0.023) | +0.008 (−0.009, +0.025) | INCONCLUSIVE | 0 of 4 | 1 of 15 |

By the pre-registered rule, DINO-init SimSiam is P1.3's JE vehicle, at Part G's recipe. P1.3 runs it beside MAE-init JE
and MAE as equal arms, over a longer horizon, and chooses only after ([Handover](#handover--what-p13-inherits)).

## Motivation

**The comparison that matters.** The project's setting is a temporally correlated stream; a deployment cannot opt out of
the correlation. So the main indicator is the correlated walk against the same start frozen, as in [P1.2.1](P1.2.1.md)'s
gate. The shuffled (IID) drive stays as an ablation.

**Why a strong start.** [P1.2.4](P1.2.4.md) found frozen DINO and a frozen supervised ViT-B ahead of the MAE start, most
of all on kNN (by 4–7 points), the read that matters for the selection filter. Adapted MAE-init JE beat frozen DINO on
scene only. A reviewer's first question is whether adaptation still pays when the start is already strong. A strong
start also brings the two floors P1.2 uses together ([P1.2.0](P1.2.0.md#how-a-gain-is-measured-against-which-floor)):
frozen DINO is both the init-matched floor and the best frozen backbone on the primary (linear) rung. So a DINO pass
answers both questions at once. DINO is a label-free joint-embedding method, the same family as SimSiam. Its known
failure mode, collapse, is what Phase 0's joint-embedding signals and collapse control were built for, so adapting it
stays inside the project's discipline.

**Why DINO's own objective, not only SimSiam.** P1.2's "JE" is a hybrid: MAE's weights under a SimSiam objective.
Continuing DINO with SimSiam would be another hybrid. Continuing DINO with DINO — the init's own objective — gives the
clean, MAE-free joint-embedding result. SimSiam on DINO weights runs beside it: it needs no new code, sits on the
signals P1.1 validated, and separates "a better start" from "a different objective".

**Why a curve, not an end point.** "Is adaptation better?" depends on how much of the stream the model has seen.
P1.2.1's walk overtook the frozen start after roughly 10–15k frames and then plateaued on scene, while traffic light
kept climbing. A deployment needs that curve. Whether *more* of the same data keeps paying (a second pass) is a horizon
question, and belongs to P1.3.

**Why more tasks.** Both of P1.2's tasks are narrow. The scene label has three classes and little headroom. The traffic
light is smaller than one 16-pixel patch at 224 px, so that task mostly reads intersection context. Larger objects and a
spatial (per-patch) read test what a driving deployment would use the features for.

**Why read by object and by regime (Part F).** The panel's "1 of 4" and "2 of 4" leave open whether the gain depends on
what is being detected. And a pooled curve can hide what happens at the stream's regime changes: gains while the stream
is in a regime, losses after it leaves.

**A loose end from P1.2.4.** Its attribution controls were matched to P1.2.1's recipe, not the confirmed protections
recipe whose gain it reports.

## Design

Common to every drive unless stated: BDD100K train, the three canary scenes, 224 px squash, batch 32, one pass, the
P1.2.1 reservations (`stream.well_fraction=0.05`, canary 100 + 100 per cell), heads settled on the frozen encoder first
(`warmup.freeze_encoder=true`), so step 0 *is* the frozen start. Every drive uses P1.2.4's protections recipe
(layer-wise lr decay 0.75, no weight decay on norms, biases, position embeddings and tokens). It is adopted, not
re-tested: it beat P1.2.1's recipe once, for MAE-init JE in P1.2.4. Part G's drives are the only ones here that run
without it. The full JE health vector is logged every 25 steps. Reads are on BDD val (the P1.2.1 pool and split), on the
readout ladder.

**The selection set, enlarged and labelled.** P1.2.4 found the 150 + 150-per-scene set too noisy to separate close arms
at one seed (a repeat of the same recipe read 0.018 apart). It grows to 450 + 450 per scene (2,700 frames) and is read
on every panel task, not the scene alone. Its cost is a shorter drive: 53,903 frames and 1,692 steps (P1.2.1: 55,614 and
1,748). The split changes, so the settled-head wells are rebuilt for MAE-init JE and DINO-init SimSiam; DINO-init DINO
needs none. Drives are compared within P1.2.5, not against P1.2.1's or P1.2.4's step counts.

**Labels and weights.** Two inputs were missing at the start, and each was checked before use.

- *Val's box geometry and drivable-area maps.* BDD's official server no longer resolves, so both come from public
    copies. Val's boxes come from the FiftyOne export our val images came from; its per-image box counts and attributes
    match our val labels on all 10,000 frames. `scripts/bdd_legacy_labels.py` converts it to `geometry/` under the BDD
    root. The drivable-area maps are BDD's official label-id PNGs from an unmodified redistribution. Its README gives
    the ids wrongly: the maps hold 0 = background, 1 = direct, 2 = alternative. Read that way, they agree with val's
    polygons on 99.2% of pixels. `data/fetch_bdd_geometry.sh` on the project share re-fetches both.
- *DINO's full training checkpoint* (student, teacher, heads; 830.7 MB, sha256 in `backbone/dino.yaml`). Its teacher
    equals the released backbone bit for bit. It holds **no centre and no training arguments**, so DINO-init DINO runs
    at the released run's published settings, and its centre is estimated before step 0 (Part A).

### Part A — The DINO-init gates (primary)

Two backbones from the same DINO ViT-B/16 start, each with its own gate, as P1.2.1 ran JE and MAE. The exit gate's
adaptation test, at a strong start, passes if either passes. Their shared frozen floor, **B5-DINO**, is the released
DINO teacher backbone (`encoder=vit_b16_dino`); each drive's step-0 encoder must be bit-identical to it.

- **DINO-init DINO** (the init-matched objective). The full checkpoint resumed intact, so no heads need settling. The
    student trains; the teacher is its moving average (momentum 0.996 → 1 over the drive) and is the encoder read, as in
    DINO's own evaluation. Multi-crop at the released run's settings: 2 global crops at 224 (scale 0.25–1), 10 local
    crops at 96 (scale 0.05–0.25); teacher temperature 0.07, student 0.1, centre momentum 0.9. The centre is set before
    step 0 to the teacher's mean logits on the well's frames, with no gradient, so step 0 is still the frozen floor.
- **DINO-init SimSiam.** DINO's backbone with SimSiam heads settled on the frozen encoder, then the SimSiam drive, as
    MAE-init JE.

**Which arm carries forward as P1.3's JE vehicle** (fixed before any data). The passing DINO arm with the larger gain
over B5-DINO on the primary rung. A tie goes to DINO-init DINO: DINO-init SimSiam is chosen only if the two-sample
contrast between the arms excludes zero in its favour on at least one gate task, and in DINO's favour on none. The
reason is the confound P1.2.5 exists to remove: SimSiam on DINO's weights switches the objective again. If neither arm
passes, the MAE-init hybrid stays the vehicle.

**The supervised ViT-B stays a frozen floor.** The ImageNet-supervised ViT-B/16 is read frozen beside B5-DINO, so the
strongest kNN floor stays in view. It is not adapted: it is not one of the project's self-supervised families and does
not feed P1.3.

**The recipe, chosen without reading val.** Per backbone, the protections recipe at three learning rates — 1e-5, 3e-5,
9e-5 — one seed each, on the walk. The arm with the best selection-set scene linear read at the end of the drive is
chosen before its confirmation:

| Backbone | 1e-5 | 3e-5 | 9e-5 | Frozen | Chosen |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| DINO-init DINO | 0.636 | 0.636 | **0.650** | 0.634 | 9e-5 |
| DINO-init SimSiam | 0.652 | **0.669** | 0.667 | 0.634 | 3e-5 |

DINO-init DINO's choice sits at the top of the grid; Part E's higher-lr drives show the objective collapsing by 1e-3.
DINO-init SimSiam's 3e-5 and 9e-5 read 0.002 apart on this one seed. Part G re-ran the grid at three seeds, and there
9e-5 leads by 0.022; the claim is read on it.

**The confirmation.** Per backbone, the chosen recipe on the walk × 5 fresh seeds, plus its IID control × 3 (secondary),
with the curve's checkpoints (Part B).

**Readouts.** Every encoder is read on the mean-pooled patch embedding, as in P1.2.1; the gates use it. DINO and the
supervised ViT-B were trained on their `cls` token, so every encoder is also read on `cls`, secondary. If `cls` reverses
a gate's direction, the claim says so.

**The few-shot learning rate, per floor.** [P1.2.0](P1.2.0.md#the-few-shot-learning-rate) fixed it once, on the MAE B5.
Here it is chosen per frozen start by P1.2.0's procedure (the grid {1e-5, 3e-5, 1e-4} at *k* = 100 on the train-side
validation reservation), before any adapted encoder is read: B5-DINO 1e-5 (0.680), the supervised ViT-B 1e-4 (0.666), B5
keeps 3e-5. The supervised ViT-B's best rate sits at the top of the grid, so its few-shot reads may be slightly
understated; it is a reference floor only.

**The resolution stays at 224 px.** It is the standard input for ViT-B/16 frozen-probe evaluation. The one peer-reviewed
study found that reads a frozen linear probe of self-supervised features on BDD, HPT (Reed et al., WACV 2022), runs its
linear analysis at 224 px. On ImageNet, DINOv2 gains only 0.2 points of linear probe from doubling the resolution.

### Part B — The time-to-payoff curve

Every confirmation drive (Part A's, and a re-run of P1.2.4's confirmed MAE-init recipe, whose drives saved only their
end) saves checkpoints at steps 100, 212, 423, 846 and 1,269, and at its end (1,692): about 3.2k, 6.8k, 13.5k, 27.1k,
40.6k and 53.9k frames. Each is read on val, frozen rungs (kNN, linear). In a single pass every frame is new, so this is
also the curve over unique frames. The checkpoints are kept, so P1.3 can resume from them.

*The caveat this cannot lift.* BDD val has the same distribution as BDD train. The curve measures adapting to a fixed
new domain, not to a stream that keeps shifting.

### Part C — The wider task panel

**Candidates.** `presence_cell:{bus,truck,pedestrian}` (presence, balanced within each scene × time-of-day cell, as the
traffic-light task); `large_cell:car,bus,truck:0.02` (a car, bus or truck box covering at least 2% of the frame, several
patches wide); `weather` (clear / rainy / snowy); and `drivable`, a per-patch probe on patch tokens (is each 16 × 16
patch mostly drivable?), the one spatial read.

**The screen** (P1.2.0's method, on the BDD *train* pool with frozen encoders only): a task is eligible if scene + time
of day predict it at most 0.60 (overlap) and its headroom (full-label fine-tune ceiling minus frozen linear) is at least
+0.05 on **frozen DINO**. Every eligible task joins the panel, up to four, in order of headroom, recorded before any
adapted encoder is read. On P1.2.0's train-side pool (14,000 frames):

| Task | Overlap | Frozen kNN | Frozen linear | Ceiling | Headroom | Panel |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| `weather` | 0.402 | 0.713 | 0.734 | 0.838 | +0.104 | ✓ |
| `large_cell:car,bus,truck:0.02` | 0.500 | 0.772 | 0.819 | 0.919 | +0.100 | ✓ |
| `presence_cell:bus` | 0.500 | 0.540 | 0.540 | 0.638 | +0.098 | ✓ |
| `presence_cell:pedestrian` | 0.500 | 0.632 | 0.607 | 0.698 | +0.092 | ✓ |
| `presence_cell:truck` | 0.500 | 0.542 | 0.541 | 0.619 | +0.078 | eligible, fifth |
| `drivable` (per patch) | 0.521 | 0.930 | 0.926 | 0.939 | +0.013 | below the headroom bar |
| *`scene` (reference)* | — | 0.630 | 0.618 | 0.713 | +0.094 | — |

The panel is weather, the large vehicle, bus and pedestrian. The drivable task fails the headroom bar: frozen DINO's
patch tokens already separate drivable patches nearly as well as a fine-tuned model. The panel therefore has no spatial
read.

### Part D — Attribution under the confirmed recipes

P1.2.4's two controls and a BDD IID drive, all with the protections recipe, from each arm's settled-head well: COCO at 3
seeds, Places365 and BDD IID at 5. The extra seeds are there because P1.2.4's Places365 margin cleared the bootstrap but
not a test over its three seeds alone. Same matching as P1.2.4, re-matched by step count to the shorter drive (1,692 ×
32 + 40 probes = 54,184 images, a seed-fixed subset of each cache). For MAE-init JE they answer P1.2.4's question for
the recipe it reports. For DINO-init, they run **only if DINO-init SimSiam passes its gate**: they test the
objective-switch confound, which DINO-init DINO does not have.

**A secondary read from the same drives.** P1.2.1's forgetting lead was the walk costing JE's kNN at night against the
IID control. P1.2.4's protections removed the loss against B5, but the walk-vs-IID contrast was never re-run with them.
Every arm here has both orders, so P1.2.1's secondary reads (walk vs IID, by time of day) are computed, descriptively.

### Part E — A collapse control for DINO's objective

Phase 0's discipline for any new JE method. Drives from the DINO checkpoint with one of DINO's two documented collapse
switches thrown: centring off (one dimension dominates) and sharpening off (a uniform output). The JE signals (projector
and backbone rank, spread) must fire on both, and the labelled canary must confirm the damage, before any DINO-objective
drive is read as healthy (bar E). The control ran in three steps: 300 steps at lr 3e-5, the full drive at the chosen lr
(9e-5), and a positive control at 3e-4 and 1e-3 (600 steps, each switch read against an unswitched twin).

**Two corroborating experiments**, designed after bar E was not met and before either was run:

1. *DINO-init DINO's val read*, as a corroborating result rather than a gated pass, with the same analysis as the other
    arms.
1. *Is it the data?* The released teacher on 1,024 frames each from BDD train, Imagenette (DINO's pretraining domain),
    COCO and Places365 (driving categories excluded, as in Part D). Per set: the teacher target's entropy with its own
    centre, with none and with BDD's centre (a uniform target is ln 65,536 ≈ 11.09 nats), the prototypes in use, and
    how alike the frames look to the teacher (mean pairwise `cls` cosine). *The prediction, fixed before the run:*
    BDD's own-centre entropy is the highest of the four and its frames the most alike; Imagenette's entropy is the
    lowest.

### Part F — Gain by object size and frequency, and by regime over the drive

Exploratory and descriptive: no pass bar, no Holm. Designed after the first results and fixed before any of its data was
read.

**F1 — A wider panel, by object size and frequency.** Presence tasks for BDD's box categories, split by the size of the
frame's largest box of the category (`size_cell:<category>:<min>:<max>`): smaller than one 16 × 16 patch at 224 px
(under 1/196 of the frame), one to four patches, and four or more (the large-vehicle task's 2%). A positive is a frame
whose largest box falls in the bin; a negative is a frame with no box of the category; frames whose largest box falls in
another bin are not used. Both are cell-balanced. Categories: traffic sign, pedestrian, truck, bus, bicycle, rider and
motorcycle, each in every bin and at any size. Car is left out: almost every frame holds one. A task is read if cell
balancing leaves at least 200 positive query frames. Frozen rungs (kNN, linear), on every drive's checkpoints and floor.
Frequency is a task's share of positive frames in BDD-train (the study's three scenes, the pool the drive samples).

**F2 — The curve by regime.** Every val frame carries its time of day and weather, so each checkpoint's Δ can be read
within a slice. The stream's timeline is known: daytime eras until step ~800 (clear 0–325, then partly cloudy, overcast,
and short rainy and snowy eras at 675–800), dawn/dusk to ~925, then night to the end, with short rainy and snowy eras at
1,575–1,675. The windows, each a change between two checkpoints of the same seeds:

- *Tracking:* the night slice from step 846 (before the stream reached night) to the end. Does the slice the stream is
    in gain?
- *Forgetting:* the daytime slice over the same steps. Does the slice the stream left lose?
- *Pooled:* the whole val set over the same steps — what the pooled curve shows there.
- *Weather:* each of the daytime rainy and snowy slices' change from step 423 to 846 (spanning their eras), minus
    daytime clear's change; and the night rainy and snowy slices' change from 1,269 to the end, minus night clear's.

A slice is read if each class keeps at least 20 frames in it. Seed × image bootstrap intervals, uncorrected: with 15
tasks per drive and rung, about 0.4 intervals per direction would exclude zero by chance alone.

**Five drives, compared only among themselves.** Part F also reads P1.2.1's two walks: MAE-init JE under P1.2.1's recipe
(no protections), and MAE with its own objective, which P1.3 carries. Their ladders read the same val query frames, and
their drives kept checkpoints at steps 100, 437, 874, 1,311 and the end. Their stream follows the same timeline about 25
steps later, so the windows move to steps 437, 874 and 1,311. Part F's numbers are compared only among these five
drives, not with P1.2.1's published numbers: F1 reads P1.2.1's drives on P1.2.5's mean-pooled readout (P1.2.1 read
`embed`), and F2 reads their gate tasks on P1.2.1's own ladder. Every Δ is against a floor read the same way, so each
drive's numbers are sound. Between a P1.2.1 drive and a P1.2.5 drive, though, the recipe and the conditions differ
together (wells, stream size, protections), so a difference between them is not a recipe effect. P1.2.4's confirmation
kept only its ends and is not included.

### Part G — The protections ablation, and every rung at every checkpoint

Designed after the first results and fixed before any of its data was read. It adds a factor that Parts A–F held fixed,
so it is reported as added after the fact.

**Why.** Every drive in Parts A–F uses the protections recipe, and its edge was measured once: on MAE-init JE, under
P1.2.4's conditions. Here both the start and the conditions changed. The protections are not mild. Layer-wise decay runs
the first block at about 3% of the heads' learning rate and the last at 75%, so a strong start's early blocks barely
move. That may be why DINO-init SimSiam's gain is narrow. Separately, MAE-init JE's attribution weakened to MIXED here,
with the recipe and the conditions changed together. And the curve showed the kNN rung moving through the drive, while
the fine-tune rungs were read only at the end.

**G1 — DINO-init SimSiam without the protections.**

- *The grid, on the selection set.* With the protections: Part A's rates, 1e-5, 3e-5 and 9e-5. Without them: 3e-6, 1e-5
    and 3e-5. The unprotected grid sits one step lower because without layer decay every block runs at the full rate.
    Each cell runs three seeds on the walk (0, 6 and 7; the protected cells' seed 0 is Part A's drive), and saves its
    end.
- *The choice.* The cell with the best selection-set scene linear read, averaged over its three seeds. This is Part A's
    criterion, read on three seeds instead of one. That cell is the recipe DINO-init SimSiam's gate, breadth and claim
    are read on. The choice is written down before any Part G drive is read on val.
- *The confirmation.* The best unprotected cell runs on the walk × seeds 1–5, with Part B's checkpoints. It is read on
    val whichever cell wins, because it is the ablation's other half. If the winning cell is not Part A's (protections
    at 3e-5), its confirmation is the one the claim is read on, and Part D's controls for DINO-init SimSiam re-run under
    it (COCO × 3, Places365 × 5, BDD IID × 5).

**G2 — MAE-init JE without the protections, under P1.2.5's conditions.** P1.2.1's recipe (lr 3e-5, no layer decay,
weight decay on every parameter) from P1.2.5's MAE-init well: the walk × seeds 1–5 with Part B's checkpoints, and Part
D's set (BDD IID × 5, COCO × 3, Places365 × 5). There is no grid: P1.2.4 already chose this rate for this recipe. With
P1.2.4's two rows and the protected row here, it completes a two-by-two of recipe × conditions, for the gain and for its
attribution. If attribution reads STREAM-SPECIFIC without the protections, the protections weakened it; if MIXED, the
conditions did. MAE-init JE is not the claim's backbone, so G2 changes no verdict.

**G3 — Every rung at every checkpoint.** Part G's drives are read at every checkpoint on every rung: kNN and linear (on
the mean-pooled readout and on `cls`), and the few-shot fine-tunes (*k* = 10 and 100) on the gate tasks. F1's size tasks
are read on the frozen rungs. The three confirmation drives of Parts A and E are re-read at every checkpoint with the
fine-tune rungs on the gate tasks. Their ends are read again too, as a check against the numbers above. Each start
fine-tunes at its floor's rate (DINO-init 1e-5, MAE-init 3e-5).

**Reads.** Each unprotected arm against its own floor, by bars A–D as written. Beside it, the recipe contrast at every
checkpoint: protected minus unprotected, two-sample, seed × image bootstrap. On the primary rung at the end it is tested
over the gate tasks with Holm; the other rungs, tasks and checkpoints are descriptive.

### Pass bars (fixed before any of this substudy's data)

Seed × image bootstrap intervals throughout, as P1.2.1; one-sided tests at family-wise 0.05 with Holm. The bootstrap
decides every verdict. Beside it, a seed-level test over the per-seed reads alone is reported (a one-sample *t*-test on
the per-seed Δ; one-sided Welch for adapted-vs-adapted). If the two disagree, the claim says so.

**A — the DINO-init gates.** Per backbone, P1.2.1's bars as written, with B5-DINO as the floor: primary rung linear on
the mean-pooled readout, gate tasks scene and cell-balanced traffic light.

- **PASS** — on at least one gate task, Δ (adapted − B5-DINO) has one-sided p ≤ 0.025 (Holm over two).
- **FAIL** — on both gate tasks, every rung's upper bound is below +0.02.
- **INCONCLUSIVE** — anything else.

**B — the curve (descriptive).** Time to payoff: the first checkpoint whose linear Δ has its lower bound above zero, per
task and arm.

**C — breadth (secondary).** On each panel task, Δ on the linear rung against the arm's own floor, Holm over the panel.
Reported as "gains on *k* of *n* tasks", with the kNN rung beside it. It qualifies the claim below.

**D — attribution.** P1.2.4's bars as written, per control; the claim is the weaker of the two.

**E — the collapse control.** It fires (both switches, signals and canary), or the DINO-objective arm is not read.

| DINO-init gate (either backbone) | Breadth (the passing backbone) | Claim |
| -- | -- | -- |
| PASS | gains on most panel tasks | **Strong** — adapting the best start tried on in-domain data beats keeping it frozen, broadly |

### Engineering

All in `tests/unit/test_p125.py` (28 tests); pre-commit clean.

- **A fine-tune fix, found by Part G's re-reads.** DINO's objective keeps its teacher, the encoder read, with gradients
    off, and the reader hands that module over as it is. The few-shot fine-tune trained a copy of it, so on DINO-init
    DINO's checkpoints it trained the head alone, while the frozen floor beside it was fully fine-tuned. Every other
    encoder arrives trainable, including the settled-head wells, which freeze their encoder only inside the warm-up.
    `fine_tune_predict` and `fine_tune_patch_predict` now set the copy trainable; a test hands over a frozen encoder and
    checks that the copy moves and the original does not. DINO-init DINO's fine-tune rungs were re-read and
    `adaptation_report.json` regenerated; no other number changed.

- **DINO's objective** (`cafl4ds/ssl/dino.py`, `backbone=dino`). The read encoder is the teacher; the student trains.
    The teacher follows the student after every optimizer step, its momentum ramped over the drive's own length. Local
    crops run at 96 px on interpolated position embeddings. The health monitor reads the JE vector, with the teacher
    head's 256-d bottleneck as the `_proj` surface. The collapse switches: `ssl.centering=false` drops the centre;
    `ssl.sharpening=false` puts the teacher at the student's temperature. Each crop draws its augmentation once per
    batch, as SimSiam does here, so the two DINO arms differ in objective only. The released run's gradient clip (0.3),
    last-layer freeze and weight-decay schedule are not used: every P1.2 arm runs the harness's clip (1.0) and the
    protections recipe. `DINO.prime` sets the centre before step 0, on a forked RNG, so neither the encoder read nor the
    drive's draws move. `max_steps` stops a drive early with the momentum ramp still spanning the full drive.

- **Readouts.** `readout=mean` reads every encoder on its patch-token mean; `cls_rungs=true` adds the frozen rungs on
    the `cls` token (`<task>|<rung>@cls`).

- **The selection set as an attributed pool** (`pool.kind=selection`), carrying every label the source does.

- **The new tasks** in `cafl4ds/adaptation.py`: `large_cell:<categories>:<min_area>` from box areas; `drivable`, a patch
    labelled 1 when more than half of it is drivable, probed on patch tokens and resampled by frame, not patch; and Part
    F's `size_cell:<category>:<min_area>:<max_area>`.

- **The geometry labels** (`data.boxes`, `data.drivable_dir`) join the decode cache's key only when set, so earlier
    caches keep their names.

- **The seed-level tests** (`adaptation.seed_ttest`, `seed_welch`), written on the incomplete beta function (scipy is
    not a dependency); they reproduce tabled Student-*t* critical values to 1e-4.

- **The analysis.** `scripts/analyze_adaptation.py mode=p125` reads `ladder/<arm>/` per arm and returns the gate, the
    `cls` reversal, breadth, the curve, walk vs IID, attribution, the carry-forward and the claim level. `mode=p125f`
    reads Part F: per drive, Δ by slice at every checkpoint, the windows (`adaptation.bootstrap_combination`, a signed
    sum of within-slice changes bootstrapped over shared seeds and images) and each task's train frequency. Its slices
    and windows are in `configs/analyze_adaptation.yaml`. `mode=p125g` reads Part G: each unprotected arm as `p125`
    reads its arms, the protected-minus-unprotected contrast at every checkpoint, and the curve on every rung read
    there.

- **The centring check** (`scripts/dino_centring_check.py`): Part E's experiment 2, forward passes only.

## Interpretation

**The carry-forward and the claim.** DINO-init SimSiam is the only passing DINO arm, so the pre-registered rule names it
P1.3's JE vehicle. No contrast between the DINO arms is needed. Part G's three-seed grid moved its recipe to the
protections at 9e-5, and the claim is read there: PASS, 0 of 4 panel tasks, so **Qualified**. The bootstrap and the
seed-level test disagree on breadth (0 against 3 of 4), and the bootstrap decides, as fixed in advance. The disagreement
is the claim's main caveat: three panel tasks gain 1.0–1.7 points with intervals touching zero.

The panel could have found a Strong result. It was fixed before any adapted encoder was read, each task has 9–10 points
of headroom, and MAE-init JE's gain registers on it (2 of 4). What it cannot do is resolve a broad gain much under 2
points per task, which is where the three tasks sit. So the reading is "firm on the gate tasks, at most small beyond
them", not "nothing beyond them". Telling those apart needs more evaluation images, not more drives (Handover). Part F
is exploratory and does not change the claim level. It does sharpen it at Part A's recipe: there the passing DINO arm's
gain is narrow on every panel tried, while MAE-init JE's widens to small objects.

**What it adds up to.** Adapting on the stream beats the frozen start on both SimSiam-objective arms, by 2–4 points of
linear accuracy on the gate tasks, and the gain is BDD-specific. It is narrow. It reaches the kNN rung only for the
first half of the pass, and at the higher rate not even then: the extra linear gain is paid for in kNN and in
fine-tuning. On scene, one pass at 9e-5 captures about half of the headroom between the frozen floor and the fine-tune
ceiling (~9 points); on the panel, little. And a 2–4-point gain is near or below what [P1.1.1](P1.1.1.md)'s canary can
resolve on a single drive (it calls a dent of 6–8 points and misses ~3). A loss of the gain would therefore be hard for
the live monitor to see.

**What is robust, and what is not.** Two MAE-init JE drives, under different recipes and conditions, pass and gain on
the same small-object tasks. That is the robust part. The size moves between +0.020 and +0.038 on scene. The kNN gain
replicates mid-drive, not at the end: under SimSiam's objective the kNN rung gains early and drifts back through the
drive's late phase, so an end-of-drive kNN number depends on how much drift has built up by then. MAE-init JE's
attribution verdict depends on the recipe: STREAM-SPECIFIC without the protections, under both P1.2.4's conditions and
these; MIXED with them. What holds across both recipes is narrower: against road-free scenes, a driving-specific
remainder on at least one gate task (on both in P1.2.4's conditions; on one or the other in these). DINO-init SimSiam's
gain is STREAM-SPECIFIC at both of its rates.

**What the protections are for.** They were adopted for a larger, broader linear gain ([P1.2.4](P1.2.4.md)). Read with
and without them on both starts, under one set of conditions, they leave the gate tasks' linear gain unchanged. What
they hold is mostly what the gate's linear probe does not read: the neighbourhoods kNN uses, how well the encoder still
fine-tunes, and the large-vehicle task. On the strong start they also seem to hold back small-object gains. That is the
stability side of P1.3's trade-off, already visible in one pass. It also explains Part G's choice: the selection rule
reads scene linear alone, so it is blind to what the protections protect, and it picked the highest protected rate,
which spends more of it than Part A's.

**DINO's own objective on BDD.** DINO's centring pushes the teacher's votes to spread evenly over 65,536 prototypes.
BDD's frames are too alike, so the centred target goes near-uniform: a weak signal pulling toward the uniform output. At
low lr the head absorbs it; at 1e-3 it reaches the backbone. It is the objective, not the features: frozen DINO is the
best frozen start on BDD. The literature predicts the mechanism. The closest study trains DINO on nuScenes and finds it
the weakest method under linear probes, naming centring's implicit equal-prototype prior as the cause, which clashes
with driving scenes [1]. Batch-statistic regularizers carry a hidden uniform-cluster prior that hurts on imbalanced data
[2]; the released DINO models already use only ~2% of their prototypes [3]; and narrow, scene-centric or correlated data
degrades DINO-style training [4–6]. Where collapse shows first — head or backbone — is not studied for resumed models;
the head absorbing it fits work showing information lost at the projector survives in the backbone [15, 16]. That part
is new rather than corroborated.

**Why tuning stops here.** Part F removes one reason to stop and supplies others. On the linear rung the pooled curve
hides no regime dynamics, so tuning on it would not optimize the wrong target. But the linear gain looks insensitive to
the recipe: two MAE-init JE drives with different recipes give the same breadth at similar sizes. The live effect is the
kNN erosion, which grows with continued training; a lower lr or stronger protections would trade it against the linear
gain, which is P1.3's stability–plasticity question at a horizon one pass cannot reach. DINO-init SimSiam's missing
breadth looks like a strong-start question — less headroom to gain — that tuning one pass would not settle. One lever
was the exception: the protections hold a strong start's early blocks nearly still, which could have narrowed the gain
by themselves. Part G tested them. On the panel the gain without them is no wider; on small objects it is, at a large
kNN cost, which leaves the trade to P1.3.

## Insights

- **Linear and kNN reads disagree, by objective and over time.** SimSiam's objective adds linearly readable information
    that holds, and a kNN gain that comes early and is gone by the end. DINO's objective builds kNN and fine-tuning and
    leaves the linear read flat. A health or forgetting reader that watches one rung misses half the picture, and the
    selection filter lives on the kNN side.
- **A recipe chosen on the linear rung spends the others.** The protections leave the gate tasks' linear gain unchanged
    and hold kNN and fine-tuning; a higher rate buys linear gain with both. A selection rule that reads one rung cannot
    see that trade, so P1.3 needs a rule that reads more than one.
- **One seed cannot choose a learning rate here.** Part A's one-seed grid put two rates 0.002 apart; three seeds put
    them 0.022 apart, in the other order.
- **The gain lives on small objects.** Where MAE-init JE gains, it gains on objects smaller than one patch, which a
    frozen probe reads poorly. Frequency in the stream does not predict it.
- **Within one pass the gain transfers forward and holds.** The night slice gains before the stream reaches night, and
    the daytime slice keeps its gain after the stream leaves. Forgetting, if it comes, needs a longer horizon.
- **A centring objective needs varied batches.** On a narrow, correlated domain DINO's centre removes most of what the
    teacher sees. That is a property of the objective family, worth recalling for any deployment on a single camera.
- **One val pool limits what can be resolved.** About 5k query frames leave the rare size bins and the weather windows
    underpowered, and the panel cannot call a gain under about 2 points, which is where the claim's breadth sits. A
    longer drive does not fix that; a larger held-out pool would.

## Handover — what P1.3 inherits

- **Three equal arms over a longer horizon** (the user's call). One pass is too short to pick or discard a vehicle. P1.3
    runs DINO-init SimSiam (the pre-registered rule's vehicle), MAE-init JE (the broadest linear gain) and MAE (the
    plasticity question) as peers, and chooses only after. DINO's own objective is not carried forward. What "longer"
    means — repeated passes, or more of BDD — is P1.3's design choice.
- **The recipes P1.3 starts from.** DINO-init SimSiam at Part G's choice, the protections at 9e-5: the pre-registered
    rule's recipe, with the largest linear gain and a clear kNN and fine-tune cost. MAE-init JE with the protections at
    3e-5, P1.2.4's recipe. Both are linear-rung choices (Insights); the protections stay, because without them every
    other rung loses more. Their checkpoints are kept, and Part A's 3e-5 drives sit beside them as the gentler setting.
- **Tuning moves to P1.3.** No further recipe tuning on one pass (Interpretation). Part G settled the one exception, the
    protections. P1.3 tunes at its own horizon, with a selection rule that judges quality on the linear and fine-tune
    rungs and holds kNN as a constraint, so a choice cannot spend the geometry unseen.
- **kNN is not a quality gate; it has two narrower jobs (recommended).** The geometry a backbone reorganizes can be
    benign: P1.1.1's compression undid itself at no canary cost, and P1.2.1's night reshaping kept the information. So a
    kNN drop alone does not say the backbone got worse. Here it was not benign. The protections held kNN and fine-tuning
    together, and the rate that cost kNN cost fine-tuning too. P1.1.1 judged "benign" with a linear canary, so this is
    the first read of fine-tuning beside the geometry. P1.3 should therefore read kNN for two jobs. First, as the
    selection filter's input: whether a kNN loss matters is tested by whether selection gets worse, for example by
    running the filter on adapted and on frozen embeddings and comparing what it picks. Second, as a forgetting warning,
    which counts when a fine-tune rung drops with it.
- **Read every rung at every checkpoint.** Parts A–F read the frozen rungs (kNN, linear) at the checkpoints and the
    fine-tune rungs at the end only; Part G reads them all at every checkpoint, and so does P1.3. A fine-tune read costs
    about two minutes per checkpoint, small beside a drive. kNN is the rung to watch: its erosion is the clearest
    forgetting signal P1.2 found, and the selection filter uses the same neighbourhoods. The fine-tune rungs add what
    kNN and linear miss: on MAE-init JE they rise and erode with kNN, and without the protections they lose from the
    first checkpoints.
- **Check that every fine-tuned encoder is trainable.** A method's read encoder can arrive with gradients off (DINO's
    teacher did); the fine-tune now forces it on, with a test (Engineering).
- **Breadth, with the power to call it.** P1.2's claim stays Qualified on breadth alone. The gain is firm on the gate
    tasks, and three panel tasks gain 1.0–1.7 points, below the roughly 2 points the panel resolves (Interpretation). So
    the breadth question is open, not answered no. P1.3 reads breadth with a held-out pool large enough to resolve about
    1 point per task (halving an interval takes about four times the images), with bus-sized tasks powered like the
    rest. It states that resolution, the panel and the test before reading. The bootstrap over seeds and images stays
    the deciding test; the seed-level test is reported beside it.
- **Part F's panel, fixed in advance.** The 15 read tasks plus scene and traffic light, with the regime windows written
    down before P1.3's data exists, so the small-object finding is tested rather than rediscovered. Dense checkpoints
    (every 100–200 steps) to resolve the erosion's shape, and a larger held-out pool for the rare bins.
- **Two health-signal reads designed here.** The transfer of P1.1.0's healthy bands to a DINO start, and a label-free
    reader for MAE-init JE losing its MAE-pretrained information ([P1.1.3](P1.1.3.md)'s per-block CKA drift). Every
    drive here logged the full health vector, so P1.3 can read them without new runs.
- **Checkpoints kept.** Every confirmation drive's checkpoints, for P1.3 to resume from.
