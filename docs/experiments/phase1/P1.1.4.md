# The reading foundation for P1.3's setup `[engineering + analysis + new runs]` (P1.1.4)

**What this substudy is.** The fifth P1.1 sub-study (see the [Phase 1 overview](index.md)), opened by the
[P1.1 audit](../audits/P1.1.md) (D1–D4, C1). P1.1.0–P1.1.3 earned the right to read degradation on one setup: a 119-step
drive over BDD *val*, warm wells, MAE's 64-d decoder, MAE-init only. P1.2 then moved P1.3 onto another. P1.3 reads
forgetting on the joint-embedding (JE) starts and plasticity on MAE, over passes of BDD *train* of ~1,700 steps or more,
with settled heads, MAE's large decoder, the fine-tuning protections and a DINO start. P1.1.4 rebuilds the reading tools
on that setup, so P1.3 inherits readers that were tested where it runs.

**What carries over, and what does not.** P1.1's *method* carries over unchanged: the matched IID control, the
single-drive rule with leave-one-out validation, damage of known size with a specificity control, and pass bars fixed
before the data. Its *numbers* do not: bands, thresholds, minimum detectable differences and the reader screen's results
were all measured on the old setup.

**Verdict:** *placeholder — written when the results are in.*

## Motivation

The audit found four gaps between P1.1's deliverable and what P1.3 needs.

- **The bands and thresholds were calibrated elsewhere (D1).** The healthy spread of every instrument depends on the
    start, the recipe and the horizon. None of these is P1.3's.
- **Nothing was calibrated on a DINO start (D2).** DINO-init SimSiam is one of P1.3's three arms, and its geometry and
    objective history differ from the MAE-started hybrid P1.1 read.
- **No one has said how small a past-regime loss the referee must resolve (D3).** P1.3's referee is P1.2's readout
    ladder, read per regime at every checkpoint. Forgetting shows first, and small, on a regime the stream has left. The
    canary resolves only coarse losses, on one task, and splits only by time of day.
- **The label-free forgetting reader serves the wrong backbone (D4).** P1.1 built CKA drift for MAE, and found no
    label-free reader for JE. P1.3 now reads forgetting on JE.

A fifth point comes from C1. Every P1.1 reading on MAE carried the 64-d decoder that [P1.2.2](P1.2.2.md) found was
damaging its last blocks. The MAE readers were never tested on the repaired recipe.

A sixth comes from D5. P1.2.5's own evidence may bring a fourth arm into P1.3, DINO-init DINO, whose objective drifts
toward collapse through a mode P1.1's collapse reader misses. P1.1 has no reader calibrated on it.

**Why it gates P1.3.** A degradation claim is only as good as the reader that makes it. If P1.3 read its arms with
uncalibrated bands, a healthy drive could look damaged and a damaged one healthy, and the run would have to be repeated.
So P1.3 reads degradation on an arm only once that arm passes P1.1.4's bars (below).

## Design

Four arms: the three P1.3 carries as equals, and a provisional fourth. P1.3 picks between its arms only after its longer
horizon, so P1.1.4 calibrates all of them.

| Arm | Start | Recipe | Existing healthy drives |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| DINO-init SimSiam | DINO ViT-B/16, SimSiam heads settled | protections, lr 9e-5 (P1.2.5 Part G) | walk × 5 (6 checkpoints each), IID × 5 (end only) |
| MAE-init JE | MAE ViT-B/16, SimSiam heads settled | protections, lr 3e-5 (P1.2.4) | walk × 5 (6 checkpoints each), IID × 5 (end only) |
| MAE | MAE ViT-B/16, 512-d × 8 decoder settled | protections, lr 1e-4 (P1.2.2) | walk × 5 on P1.2.1's split (end only); no IID |
| DINO-init DINO (provisional) | DINO's full checkpoint, centre primed before step 0 | protections, lr 9e-5 (P1.2.5 Part A) | walk × 5 (6 checkpoints each), IID × 3 (end only) |

**The provisional fourth arm.** P1.2.5 did not carry DINO-init DINO forward, because its gate reads the linear rung and
the arm gains nothing there. But P1.2.5's handover judges quality on the linear and fine-tune rungs, with kNN as a
constraint. By that rule the arm reads well: the largest fine-tune gain of any arm, and kNN gains where the SimSiam-
objective arms lose kNN. The P1.2 audit re-evaluates it. P1.1.4 calibrates it beside the three, so P1.3 is not held up
if it joins. Two things set it apart for reading:

- *The encoder read is the teacher,* a moving average of the student (momentum 0.996 → 1). Damage must therefore hit
    both, or the average would dilute it over hundreds of steps (Part 2).
- *Its objective drifts toward collapse as the rate rises* ([P1.2.5](P1.2.5.md)'s Part E: the backbone collapses by
    1e-3). With centring off, its projector collapses while backbone RankMe stays put. That is a mode P1.1.1's collapse
    reader, projector with backbone RankMe, calls benign (the audit's C4). On this arm a healthy band on the label-free
    panel is therefore also its collapse watch, and Part 2 reads its natural collapse.

These are the recipes P1.2.5 hands to P1.3. Both JE recipes were chosen on the linear rung, and P1.3 retunes at its own
horizon with a rule that also reads kNN and fine-tuning ([P1.2.5](P1.2.5.md)'s handover), as it does MAE in its second
MAE pass. So the bands certified here are for the handover recipes. What transfers to a retuned recipe is the method,
the reader screen's verdicts and the referee's power, which rest mostly on the pool and the damage, not on the rate. A
retuned recipe's bands are re-fitted by Part 1b's procedure on its own IID controls, which P1.3 runs anyway. Part A's
gentler DINO-init SimSiam drives (protections, lr 3e-5: walk × 5 with checkpoints, IID × 5) are read in Part 1a too, at
no cost, to show how far the bands move with the rate.

**What the matched null sees, and what it does not.** The bands are fitted on IID drives of the same recipe. So they
flag what the *correlated diet* does beyond the recipe, which is the exit gate's degradation question. They cannot see
what the recipe itself costs against the frozen start, on any diet. At 9e-5, DINO-init SimSiam's walks end 4.4 points
above the floor on scene linear, but 5.5 below it on scene kNN and 2.6 below it on the 100-shot scene fine-tune
(P1.2.5's Part G). The walk itself accounts for 3.8 of the kNN loss: that is the walk-vs-IID difference, which the bands
should see. The rest, about 1.7 points, is the recipe's, and the bands cannot see it. The fine-tune loss cannot yet be
split this way, because P1.2.5 read fine-tunes on the walks only. Part 1b's ladder reads on the IID drives supply the
missing half. The recipe's own cost is the stability side of P1.3's trade-off. P1.3 reads it on the ladder against the
floor, not with these bands.

**Two canary panels: information and geometry.** P1.1.1 read the canary's kNN and linear channels as one panel, and
called any fire degradation. [P1.2.5](P1.2.5.md)'s handover recommends that kNN is not a quality gate. A reorganized
geometry can be benign, as P1.1.1's projector compression was. So kNN is read for two narrower jobs: as the selection
filter's input, and as a forgetting warning that counts when a fine-tune rung drops with it. P1.1.4 therefore splits the
canary into two panels, each at α = 0.05 over the drive, and adjudicates a drive as follows (fixed before 1b is read):

- `quiet` — nothing fired;
- `movement` — only the label-free panel fired;
- `geometry` — the kNN canary panel fired and the linear one did not. The selection filter's input moved; whether that
    matters is P1.3's selection read;
- `degradation` — the linear canary panel fired; or the kNN panel fired and, at a checkpoint at or after the fire, a
    fine-tune rung on scene or traffic light fell below every healthy IID seed's read at that checkpoint (Part 1b's
    ladder reads give 10 such seeds).

The label is tagged transient or persisting as in P1.1.1. The split reads P1.1's own evidence the same way: surgical
damage costs the linear channel about twice the kNN channel, so `degradation` still catches it, while the collapse dial
hit kNN harder (the audit's E2), which is P1.4's collapse question, read on projector and backbone RankMe.

**Conditions.** P1.2.5's, throughout: BDD train, its enlarged selection set, one pass of 1,692 steps, the walk and its
IID control, the full health vector every 25 steps, checkpoints at steps 100, 212, 423, 846, 1,269 and the end. The MAE
arm has no drives on this split, so it gets its own settled-head well and its walks re-run. DINO-init DINO needs no
well: DINO's full checkpoint resumes intact, as in P1.2.5. Its projector surface is the DINO head's 256-d bottleneck, as
P1.2.5 logged it.

**What one pass cannot settle.** P1.3's horizon is longer than one pass. P1.1.4's bands cover one pass. Past it, P1.3
extends them with its own IID controls, by the same method. If P1.3's tuning changes an arm's recipe, the arm's bands
are re-checked the same way.

### 1. The healthy null and the bands, per arm

**1a — Preliminary, no runs.** On both JE arms and on DINO-init DINO, from P1.2.5's corpora. DINO-init DINO has only 3
IID seeds, so its null has 56 splits and its leave-one-out 3 folds; its 1a read is descriptive.

- *The null* ([P1.1.0](P1.1.0.md)'s method). Walk against IID, 5 seeds each, per instrument: the exact 252-split
    permutation test with the maximum over checkpoints taken inside each split. Which instruments does correlation alone
    move on P1.3's setup?
- *The rule* ([P1.1.1](P1.1.1.md)'s). Bands and panel thresholds fitted on the 5 IID drives, leave-one-out. Then each
    walk read against them. Which instruments fire on a healthy walk?
- *A DINO-against-MAE-init comparison* (D2's question, descriptive). How the two JE arms' band widths and walk-vs-IID
    gaps differ, instrument by instrument.

Five seeds underestimated a random-walk-like spread in P1.1.1, so 1a is read as a first look. It sets no threshold P1.3
uses.

**Settings, fixed before reading** (1a and 1b). P1.1.1's panels and settings, with the canary split as above: the
label-free panel (projector and backbone RankMe, projector uniformity, projector and backbone CKA drift, cosine drift;
on MAE the backbone ones and uniformity), the linear canary panel and the kNN canary panel (each pooled and per
time-of-day stratum), the canary smoothed over 5 readings, the label-free signals raw, the local-SD floor at 0.25, α =
0.05 per panel, and readings after a batch under 8 images skipped. One setting is rescaled. P1.2.5 logs every 25 steps,
69 readings per drive, where P1.1.1 read ~120. So the local-SD span covers the same share of the drive: ±8 readings,
where P1.1.1 used ±15.

**The in-drive canary against the referee.** Both read the same mean-pooled embedding, but on different frames: the
canary on the train-side canary reservation, with 900 support images, and the referee on val, with a fit pool of ~3,500.
1a compares them per seed at the walks' and IID drives' ends, on scene kNN and linear. The question is whether the
canary sees the walk-vs-IID losses the referee sees (DINO-init SimSiam's scene kNN, −3.8 on val).

**1b — Full.** Per arm, IID seeds 6–15 join seeds 1–5, all saving the checkpoints above. On DINO-init DINO, seeds 4–15
join seeds 1–3. Those three logged the health vector but saved only their end, so on this arm the ladder's and the
per-block readers' IID bands come from seeds 4–15. Every new drive is read on the ladder at every checkpoint, every rung
(kNN, linear, the 10- and 100-shot fine-tunes) on scene and traffic light. Those reads give the fine-tune rung's healthy
range for the `degradation` rule, and the referee's own walk-vs-IID null. The MAE arm first gets its settled-head well
on P1.2.5's split, then its walk × 5 (seeds 1–5) and IID × 5 (seeds 1–5). The rule is calibrated on IID seeds 1–10, as
P1.1.1 calibrated on 10, and checked on fresh seeds 11–15. The null is re-read as walk × 5 against IID × 15, an exact
permutation over all 15,504 splits.

**Canary power, corrected for B2.** P1.1.0's minimum detectable difference (MDD) is the power of a single-checkpoint
*t*-test, but the verdicts come from the permutation test's maximum over checkpoints, which needs a larger gap. Here the
power is read for the test actually run. Per arm, a drop of known size is injected into 5 of the 15 IID seeds and tested
against the other 10, over many such splits. Three gap shapes: sustained over the drive, over its last quarter, and at a
single reading. The MDD per shape is the drop caught 80% of the time, per time-of-day stratum and pooled. In 1a there is
no spare IID seed to inject into, so only P1.1.0's single-checkpoint figure is reported there.

**Pass bars, per arm and panel** (label-free, linear canary, kNN canary):

- Out-of-sample false alarms (leave-one-out over seeds 1–10, plus fresh seeds 11–15) at most 2 of 15. That is P1.1.1's
    result, written as a bar.
- The `degradation` label fires on at most 1 of the 15 out-of-sample IID drives.
- Reported beside it: the threshold; the walk's fires, instrument by instrument, as in P1.1.1; the Wilson interval on
    the false-alarm count; and the drive-level rate, either panel firing, since a drive is read on both (the audit's
    B3).

### 2. A degradation of known size on the DINO-start and JE arms, and their reader screen

**The damage.** P1.1.1's surgical branches, on both JE arms and on DINO-init DINO. On DINO-init DINO the student and the
teacher are damaged identically, and the centre is left as it stands. Each branch resumes a healthy walk from its
step-846 checkpoint, with seeds 1–3. Step 846 is the last checkpoint before the stream reaches night: the daytime
regimes are behind it and dawn/dusk is under way. The branch damages the encoder, then drives the rest of the walk (846
steps), saving checkpoints at 1,269 and the end. A resumed branch restarts the optimizer, so every dose is read against
a **dose-0 twin** resumed the same way.

- *The dose ladder.* Gaussian noise on every weight matrix of all 12 blocks, at four multiples of each tensor's own SD.
    The multiples are picked by a probe-only **dose pilot** (P1.1.1's [`dose_pilot.py`](../../../scripts/dose_pilot.py))
    on each arm's step-846 states, to span a canary dent of about 2 to 15 points. The ladder is recorded before any
    branch runs.
- *The specificity control.* Re-initializing the top 4 blocks: a large weight change. On MAE it cost the canary nothing
    in P1.1.1, but on JE it cost about 2 points. It counts as a specificity control on an arm only if the pilot puts its
    canary cost under 1 point. Otherwise it is read as one more dose.
- *The arbiter.* A damage dose counts as **called** on a seed when Part 1b's adjudication reads it as `degradation`.
    Beside that, the full ladder (kNN, linear and both fine-tunes, on scene and traffic light) on val at the post-damage
    state, step 1,269 and the end of every branch and twin.

**The readers screened** ([P1.1.2](P1.1.2.md)'s screen, on the same three arms):

- every label-free signal in the health vector, backbone and projector surfaces: RankMe, uniformity, alignment, CKA
    drift, cosine drift, feature variance, off-diagonal covariance;
- per block, from the checkpoints: plain and debiased CKA drift, and RankMe, on the canary's probe images, anchored at
    step 0 (the frozen start, which is the floor every P1.2 gain is measured against);
- [P1.1.3](P1.1.3.md)'s middle-block panel, blocks 4–11, as P1.1.3 fixed it. It was chosen on MAE-objective data, so on
    JE it is a test, not a refit.

Each reader is read as a single-drive panel against bands from IID seeds 1–10 at the same checkpoints (span 3, floor
0.25, α = 0.05, as P1.1.2's per-block panels).

**Pass bar, per reader and arm** (P1.1.2's bar, with P1.1.3's single-drive conditions):

- *Sensitive.* Fires on every seed of every called dose, at the first checkpoint after the damage.
- *Specific.* Quiet on the 3 dose-0 twins at their end. Quiet on at least 4 of 5 healthy walks, at every checkpoint.
    Leave-one-out false alarms on IID seeds 1–10 at most 2 of 10. Quiet on re-init 4, where it counts as a control.

**A natural JE degradation, descriptive.** Part G's walks without the protections lose kNN and fine-tune accuracy
against the protected walks from the first checkpoints, on both JE arms, with the same seeds and checkpoints. On
DINO-init SimSiam at 3e-5, scene kNN ends 10.6 points and the 100-shot fine-tune 5.9 points below the protected walk.
That is a kNN loss with a fine-tune loss beside it, the `degradation` pattern. Every reader that passes is read on them.
The catch is the confound P1.1.3 met with the lr-1e-4 walk: without layer decay the early blocks move more, so a reader
can separate the two recipes by reading the lever rather than the loss. So this read has no bar. Its question is whether
the reader's contrast follows the loss across checkpoints.

**A natural DINO collapse, descriptive.** P1.2.5's Part E ran DINO-init DINO's objective, unswitched, at 3e-4 and 1e-3
for 600 steps, with the health vector logged. At 1e-3 it collapses on its own: backbone RankMe falls from 276 to 7 and
the canary's kNN read loses 11.7 points. Every DINO-init DINO reader that passes is read on these two drives, against
the arm's bands, for when it first fires. One seed each, and the rate is again the lever, so this read has no bar
either.

### 3. The MAE readers on the repaired recipe

**The damage.** As Part 2, on the MAE arm: its own pilot, ladder, twin and seeds, from its re-run walks' step-846
checkpoints.

**The readers.** Those Part 2 screens, plus the MAE readers P1.1 left: CKA drift against the anchor (P1.1.2) and the
middle-block panel (P1.1.3). The anchor is now the settled-head, large-decoder start, so the audit's C1 point about a
mildly damaged anchor no longer applies.

**A natural MAE failure.** P1.2.1's recipe (the 64-d × 2 decoder, no protections, lr 1e-4) lost to its frozen start on
every rung within 100 steps. That is a label-confirmed natural MAE failure on BDD, the kind P1.1.2 and P1.1.3 said only
P1.3 would supply. It is re-run here on P1.2.5's split: its own settled-head well (64-d decoder), walk × seeds 1–3, the
same checkpoints. The ladder at its checkpoints confirms the loss on this split before any reader is read on it.

- *Pass bar.* The middle-block panel, as P1.1.3 fixed it, fires on at least 2 of 3 failing walks by step 423. It must
    also pass the Part 2 bar on the repaired arm.
- *The confound.* The failing and healthy walks differ in recipe, so drift may separate them by reading the decoder and
    the learning-rate groups. It is the same caveat as Part 2's natural read, and it is reported with the result. Unlike
    Part 2's, this one is a bar: P1.1.3 handed the middle-block panel to P1.3 for validation on a natural failure, and
    this is that validation, run before P1.3 rather than inside it.

### 4. The referee's power on past regimes

**The referee.** P1.2's readout ladder, on the val pool, sliced by regime as P1.2.5's Part F slices it (time of day ×
weather, a slice read if each class keeps at least 20 frames). Read at every checkpoint. Following P1.2.5's handover,
quality is judged on the linear and fine-tune rungs, and kNN is a constraint and a warning. So the power read covers the
10- and 100-shot fine-tunes as well as kNN and linear.

**The test P1.3 runs.** For a regime the stream has left at checkpoint *t*₀, the loss at a later checkpoint *t* is the
fall in its Δ (arm minus floor) from *t*₀ to *t*. P1.3 asks whether that loss is real at any later checkpoint, so the
test is a maximum over checkpoints, family-wise over the regimes read.

**The read.** From P1.2.5's per-image reads at every walk checkpoint, both JE arms and DINO-init DINO: the seed × image
bootstrap spread of each slice's change between checkpoints, and its correlation across checkpoints. From these, by
simulation:

- per slice and rung, the MDD of the max-over-checkpoints test at α = 0.05 (Holm over slices) and 80% power, at the
    current pool size;
- the pool size each slice needs to reach the target, scaling the image component by 1/√*n*. The seed component does not
    shrink with the pool. It sets a floor only more seeds can lower, so the seed count needed is reported too.

**The target, a proposal for the user to confirm.** Resolve a past-regime loss of **2 points** on the linear and
fine-tune rungs. On the gate tasks, P1.3's JE recipes gain 2–4.4 points of linear accuracy over one pass (P1.2.5): 2
points is about half the scene gain and the whole traffic-light gain. A referee that cannot see a loss the size of the
gain cannot protect it. The read also reports what 1 point would take.

**4b — Breadth, with the power to call it.** P1.2.5 left breadth open, not answered no: three panel tasks gain 1.0–1.7
points, under the ~2 points its val pool resolves. Its handover asks P1.3 for a held-out pool that resolves about 1
point per task, bus-sized tasks included. The same machinery sizes it: the end-of-drive Δ against the floor per panel
task, its seed and image noise from P1.2.5's reads, and the pool and seed count that resolve 1 point with Holm over the
panel. The likely answer is about 4× the images, which BDD val alone cannot supply for the three scenes. So 4b also
lists the sources that could: all of val's query frames at a smaller fit fraction, a train-side held-out reservation
taken before the stream (P1.2.0 used 14,000 such frames), or more seeds where the seed part dominates. Which source P1.3
uses is the user's decision.

**No pass bar.** Part 4 sizes P1.3's held-out pools. P1.3 pre-registers the target and the sizes.

### The gate P1.3 inherits

Per arm, DINO-init DINO included if it joins, P1.3 reads degradation only once:

1. Part 1b's three panels and the `degradation` label pass their false-alarm bars on that arm; and
1. Part 4 has sized the arm's per-regime and breadth pools, and P1.3 has pre-registered the target.

A label-free reader is not part of the gate. P1.3 reads degradation through labels, against matched controls, as P1.1
always intended. If no label-free reader passes on an arm, that arm's monitor is labels-only. That is a finding for the
Phase-3 closed loop, which needs a label-free channel, not a block on P1.3.

### Engineering

To be built and tested before any run:

- **Branches from P1.2.5's checkpoints.** P1.1.1's `resume` / `damage` path on a ViT-B with the protections' parameter
    groups and P1.2.5's split; a check that the branch's step-846 state reproduces the walk's own step-846 read at dose
    0\.
- **Damage on DINO's method.** `damage` and `dose_pilot.py` applied to the student and the teacher alike, the centre
    untouched, with a unit test that the two stay equal after damage and that dose 0 is a no-op. DINO-init DINO's dose
    pilot runs once this passes, so it is not in Stage 1.
- **The MAE wells on P1.2.5's split.** One with the 512-d × 8 decoder (the repaired arm), one with the 64-d × 2 decoder
    (the natural failure).
- **Block features from P1.2.5's checkpoints.** [`block_features.py`](../../../cafl4ds/block_features.py) on each arm's
    method, on the canary's probe images, anchored at step 0.
- **The power simulation** (Part 4), with unit tests on known cases.
- **Torch on 8 threads** for every CPU read: P1.1's SVD-based reads ran about 100× slower when they shared the cores
    with HPU jobs.

## Findings

*Preliminary: Parts 1a and 4, from existing drives only. Parts 1b, 2 and 3 await their runs. These first reads used
P1.1.1's single canary panel and the kNN and linear rungs only, on the two JE arms; the split panels, the fine-tune
rungs, DINO-init DINO and 4b are the handover's first steps.*

### 1a. The healthy null and bands on the JE arms, 5 seeds (preliminary)

|  | DINO-init SimSiam | MAE-init JE |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Threshold, label-free / canary | 5.77 / 7.24 | 6.83 / 6.98 |
| Leave-one-out false alarms, per panel | 1/5, 1/5 | 1/5, 1/5 |
| Drives with either panel firing (leave-one-out) | 2/5 | 2/5 |
| Healthy walk: what fires | projector RankMe and uniformity, 4/5 seeds | projector RankMe, uniformity and CKA drift, 5/5 |
| Healthy walk: canary panel | quiet on all 5 | quiet on all 5 |
| Walk vs IID, canary channels | no gap (every *p* ≥ 0.13) | night kNN ahead early, behind late |

- **The thresholds sit well above P1.1.1's** (3.7–4.6). That is the known cost of fitting a tail to 5 seeds, and it is
    why 1b calibrates on 10. No threshold here is for use.
- **The healthy walk looks as it did on P1.1's setup.** The projector compresses and the canary holds, on both arms. So
    P1.1.1's benign-specialization reading most likely carries over to P1.3's starts, pending 1b and Part 2's damage.
- **MAE-init JE's walk moves night kNN in two phases.** It runs ahead of IID by about 4 points at steps 200–500, while
    it is still streaming daytime. It then falls behind through the night legs and ends 3.9 points down (|*d*| 1.45).
    Only the early lead is significant on its own. The late deficit is P1.2.1's and P1.2.5's kNN erosion seen on the
    in-drive canary, a lead for 1b's 15 IID seeds to test. On DINO-init SimSiam no canary channel separates walk from
    IID.
- **D2: the DINO start's canary is noisier.** Its healthy canary bands are wider than MAE-init JE's (pooled kNN SD 0.020
    vs 0.014; daytime kNN 0.034 vs 0.020). At one checkpoint, P1.1.0's MDD is 3.8 points for pooled kNN and 6.8 for
    daytime kNN on DINO-init SimSiam, against 3.3 and 3.6 on MAE-init JE. The DINO arm's canary resolves less, so its
    forgetting reads lean more on the ladder.

### 4. The referee's power on past regimes (preliminary)

From P1.2.5's per-image reads at the walks' checkpoints: the loss the max-over-checkpoints test catches 80% of the time,
for a loss sustained after the stream leaves the slice. α = 0.05 is split over the slices read. The test runs over
P1.2.5's 2–3 later checkpoints; on a dense grid of 10 the bound in brackets applies.

| Arm | Task, rung | Daytime (1,458–2,545 frames) | Dawn/dusk | Weather regimes within daytime (134–1,067) |

| DINO-init SimSiam | scene kNN | 4.2 (6.0) | 4.3 | 6.6–9.0 |

|  | traffic light kNN | 2.4 (3.6) | 7.3 | 4.8–7.9 |

|  | traffic light kNN | 3.1 (4.1) | 4.6 | 4.4–9.2 |
|  | traffic light linear | 2.4 (3.1) | 4.9 | 4.4–9.2 |

Points of balanced accuracy.

- **On the daytime slice, the 2-point target is near.** Five of the eight cells reach it at 5 seeds with 1–2.3× the
    current pool, and at 10 seeds with 1–1.4×. The other three need more seeds, not frames. DINO-init SimSiam's scene
    kNN cannot reach it even at 10 seeds: its seeds diverge. Its scene linear and MAE-init JE's traffic-light kNN reach
    it only at 10 seeds (1.2× and 3.8× the pool).
- **Per weather regime, the target is out of reach.** These slices resolve 3.4–9.2 points. The seed and image parts of
    the noise are about equal (their ratio is 0.3–2.6), so both more frames and more seeds would be needed. At 5 seeds,
    35 of the 40 regime cells cannot reach 2 points with any pool up to 1,000×, and the other five need 5–40× the pool.
    At 10 seeds, 21 still cannot, and the rest need 3.7–64×. BDD's held-out frames per weather regime cannot grow that
    far. P1.3 can therefore pre-register forgetting reads per time of day, and pool the weather regimes or read them
    descriptively.
- **1 point is out of reach** everywhere but MAE-init JE's scene linear on the daytime slice (about 2× the pool).
- **The drives already show losses near the bound.** On the past daytime regimes, scene kNN falls by 5.2 and 5.3 points
    on DINO-init SimSiam (clear, partly cloudy) and by 3.0 and 4.0 on MAE-init JE, by the drive's end. Each is under its
    slice's MDD. This is P1.2.5's late kNN erosion, located on the regimes the stream left, and it is the size of loss
    P1.3 must resolve.
- **A lead on the canary: it may not see a kNN loss the referee sees.** On DINO-init SimSiam at 9e-5, P1.2.5 reads the
    walk costing scene kNN 3.8 points against IID on val, with an interval excluding zero. The in-drive canary's pooled
    kNN, the same embedding on the train-side canary frames, ends 0.2 points apart (*d* −0.09). The canary's
    single-checkpoint MDD there is 3.8 points, so this may be power alone. The per-seed comparison in 1a decides it.
