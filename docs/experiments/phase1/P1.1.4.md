# The reading foundation for P1.3's setup `[engineering + analysis + new runs]` (P1.1.4)

**What this substudy is.** The fifth P1.1 sub-study (see the [Phase 1 overview](index.md)), opened by the
[P1.1 audit](../audits/P1.1.md) (D1–D4, C1). P1.1.0–P1.1.3 earned the right to read degradation on one setup: a 119-step
drive over BDD *val*, warm wells, MAE's 64-d decoder, MAE-init only. P1.2 then moved P1.3 onto another. P1.3 reads
forgetting on the joint-embedding (JE) starts and plasticity on MAE, over passes of BDD *train* of ~1,700 steps or more,
with settled heads, MAE's large decoder, the fine-tuning protections and a DINO start. P1.1.4 rebuilds the reading tools
on that setup, so P1.3 inherits readers that were tested where it runs.

**What carries over, and what does not.** P1.1's *method* carries over unchanged: the matched IID control, the
single-drive rule with leave-one-out validation, damage of known size with a specificity control, and pass bars fixed
before the data. Its *numbers* do not: bands, thresholds, minimum detectable differences and the reader screen's results
were all measured on the old setup.

**Verdict: P1.3 can read degradation on all four arms, through labels. A label-free reader passes on MAE and on
DINO-init DINO, not on the JE arms.** Every arm's readers keep their false alarms within the bars on 15 healthy drives
they never saw, once the fine-tune check is calibrated like the other panels. The damage ladder lands at its designed
sizes on the referee, and the optimizer restart a branch carries is small there. None of the 14 health-vector signals
passes the reader screen on any arm. Per block, MAE's CKA drift and P1.1.3's middle-block panel pass on the repaired
recipe and fire on the old recipe's natural failure, with a recipe confound beside it. With no re-init control
surviving, that pass has no specificity anchor. DINO-init DINO's middle panel passes too, provisionally: on bands an
encoder going still keeps narrow, and under a twin rule settled after the results. On both JE arms the healthy walk
itself leaves the IID bands as far as a damaged branch does, so no label-free reader can pass there against them. The
referee resolves past-regime losses to the 2-point target on its best rung and slices, and breadth's 1 point needs a
train-side held-out pool.

## Motivation

The audit found four gaps between P1.1's deliverable and what P1.3 needs.

- **The bands and thresholds were calibrated elsewhere (D1).** The healthy spread of every instrument depends on the
    start, the recipe and the horizon. None of these is P1.3's.
- **Nothing was calibrated on a DINO start (D2).** DINO-init SimSiam is one of P1.3's three arms, and its geometry and
    objective history differ from the MAE-started hybrid P1.1 read.
- **No one has said how small a past-regime loss the referee must resolve (D3).** P1.3's referee is P1.2's readout
    ladder, read per regime at every checkpoint. Forgetting shows first, and small, on a regime the stream has left. The
    canary resolves only coarse losses, on one task, and splits only by time of day.
- **The label-free forgetting reader serves the wrong backbone (D4).** P1.1 built CKA drift for MAE, and found no
    label-free reader for JE. P1.3 now reads forgetting on JE.

A fifth point comes from C1. Every P1.1 reading on MAE carried the 64-d decoder that [P1.2.2](P1.2.2.md) found was
damaging its last blocks. The MAE readers were never tested on the repaired recipe.

A sixth comes from D5. P1.2.5's own evidence may bring a fourth arm into P1.3, DINO-init DINO, whose objective drifts
toward collapse through a mode P1.1's collapse reader misses. P1.1 has no reader calibrated on it.

**The canary and the referee.** Two label-side instruments recur through this doc, and they do different jobs. The
*canary* is the alarm inside a drive: a small labelled reservation read every 25 steps, cheap enough to run
continuously. The *referee* is the judge afterwards: P1.2's readout ladder on held-out val frames, read at the saved
checkpoints, too costly to run continuously but much sharper. A smoke detector and a building inspector is the analogy:
one is always on and coarse, the other comes by occasionally and decides.

|  | Canary | Referee |
| -- | -- | -- |
| What it is | P1.1's in-drive probe, logged by the harness | P1.2's readout ladder (P1.2.5's val query) |
| When it reads | every 25 steps, inside the drive (69 readings) | at the 6 saved checkpoints, after the drive |
| Frames | a train-side canary reservation: 900 support, 900 query | BDD val: a fit pool of ~3,500, the rest as query |
| Tasks | scene only | the gate tasks (scene, traffic light), and P1.2.5's breadth panel (weather, large vehicle, bus, pedestrian) |
| Rungs | kNN and linear | kNN, linear, and the 10- and 100-shot fine-tunes (fine-tunes on the gate tasks) |
| Slices | time of day | time of day × weather (per regime) |
| What it resolves | ~0.5–3 points held over a drive (the group test), 1–7 at a single reading (Findings, 1b) | ~2 points per task at the end; 1–2 on the 100-shot fine-tune of a large slice (Part 4) |
| Its job here | the `degradation` label (Design, the two canary panels); the arbiter that calls a damaged seed (Part 2) | the walks' and IID drives' true cost; each control's confirmation and the damage's size (Part 2); P1.3's forgetting read (Part 4) |

The two read the same mean-pooled embedding, so they should agree in kind. Where they do not, the referee wins, because
its frames are held out from the stream and its rungs read more than one task. Part 1a measures how far they disagree.

**Why it gates P1.3.** A degradation claim is only as good as the reader that makes it. If P1.3 read its arms with
uncalibrated bands, a healthy drive could look damaged and a damaged one healthy, and the run would have to be repeated.
So P1.3 reads degradation on an arm only once that arm passes P1.1.4's bars (below).

## Design

Four arms: the three P1.3 carries as equals, and DINO-init DINO, which joins them as a fourth explored arm. P1.3 picks
between its arms only after its longer horizon, so P1.1.4 calibrates all of them.

| Arm | Start | Recipe | Existing healthy drives |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| DINO-init SimSiam | DINO ViT-B/16, SimSiam heads settled | protections, lr 9e-5 (P1.2.5 Part G) | walk × 5 (6 checkpoints each), IID × 5 (end only) |
| MAE-init JE | MAE ViT-B/16, SimSiam heads settled | protections, lr 3e-5 (P1.2.4) | walk × 5 (6 checkpoints each), IID × 5 (end only) |
| MAE | MAE ViT-B/16, 512-d × 8 decoder settled | protections, lr 1e-4 (P1.2.2) | walk × 5 on P1.2.1's split (end only); no IID |
| DINO-init DINO | DINO's full checkpoint, centre primed before step 0 | protections, lr 9e-5 (P1.2.5 Part A) | walk × 5 (6 checkpoints each), IID × 3 (end only) |

**The fourth arm.** P1.2.5 did not carry DINO-init DINO forward, because its gate reads the linear rung and the arm
gains nothing there. But P1.2.5's handover judges quality on the linear and fine-tune rungs, with kNN as a constraint.
By that rule the arm reads well: the largest fine-tune gain of any arm, and kNN gains where the SimSiam- objective arms
lose kNN. P1.1.4 was designed to calibrate it beside the three while the P1.2 audit re-evaluated it; it now joins P1.3,
which explores it beside the others. Two things set it apart for reading:

- *The encoder read is the teacher,* a moving average of the student (momentum 0.996 → 1). Damage must therefore hit
    both, or the average would dilute it over hundreds of steps (Part 2).
- *Its objective drifts toward collapse as the rate rises* ([P1.2.5](P1.2.5.md)'s Part E: the backbone collapses by
    1e-3). With centring off, its projector collapses while backbone RankMe stays put. That is a mode P1.1.1's collapse
    reader, projector with backbone RankMe, calls benign (the audit's C4). On this arm a healthy band on the label-free
    panel is therefore also its collapse watch, and Part 2 reads its natural collapse.

These are the recipes P1.2.5 hands to P1.3. Both JE recipes were chosen on the linear rung, and P1.3 retunes at its own
horizon with a rule that also reads kNN and fine-tuning ([P1.2.5](P1.2.5.md)'s handover), as it does MAE in its second
MAE pass. So the bands certified here are for the handover recipes. What transfers to a retuned recipe is the method,
the reader screen's verdicts and the referee's power, which rest mostly on the pool and the damage, not on the rate. A
retuned recipe's bands are re-fitted by Part 1b's procedure on its own IID controls, which P1.3 runs anyway. Part A's
gentler DINO-init SimSiam drives (protections, lr 3e-5: walk × 5 with checkpoints, IID × 5) are read in Part 1a too, at
no cost, to show how far the bands move with the rate.

**What the matched null sees, and what it does not.** The bands are fitted on IID drives of the same recipe. So they
flag what the *correlated diet* does beyond the recipe, which is the exit gate's degradation question. They cannot see
what the recipe itself costs against the frozen start, on any diet. At 9e-5, DINO-init SimSiam's walks end 4.4 points
above the floor on scene linear, but 5.5 below it on scene kNN and 2.6 below it on the 100-shot scene fine-tune
(P1.2.5's Part G). The walk itself accounts for 3.8 of the kNN loss: that is the walk-vs-IID difference, which the bands
should see. The rest, about 1.7 points, is the recipe's, and the bands cannot see it. The fine-tune loss cannot yet be
split this way, because P1.2.5 read fine-tunes on the walks only. Part 1b's ladder reads on the IID drives supply the
missing half. The recipe's own cost is the stability side of P1.3's trade-off. P1.3 reads it on the ladder against the
floor, not with these bands.

**Two canary panels: information and geometry.** P1.1.1 read the canary's kNN and linear channels as one panel, and
called any fire degradation. [P1.2.5](P1.2.5.md)'s handover recommends that kNN is not a quality gate. A reorganized
geometry can be benign, as P1.1.1's projector compression was. So kNN is read for two narrower jobs: as the selection
filter's input, and as a forgetting warning that counts when a fine-tune rung drops with it. P1.1.4 therefore splits the
canary into two panels, each at α = 0.05 over the drive, and adjudicates a drive as follows (fixed before 1b is read):

- `quiet` — nothing fired;
- `movement` — only the label-free panel fired;
- `geometry` — the kNN canary panel fired and the linear one did not. The selection filter's input moved; whether that
    matters is P1.3's selection read;
- `degradation` — the linear canary panel fired; or the kNN panel fired and the fine-tune reads dropped with it, at a
    checkpoint at or after the fire. The fine-tune reads (10- and 100-shot, on scene and traffic light) are read as a
    fourth panel, by the same rule as the other three: bands fitted on the healthy IID seeds' ladder reads, and one
    Gumbel threshold at α = 0.05 over every read and checkpoint. Those seeds are the ones read at every checkpoint (6–15
    on the JE arms), which include the fresh check seeds 11–15. So on those five, the fine-tune panel is read in-sample.
    The rule fixed first was simpler: a fine-tune read below every healthy seed's at any one checkpoint. Part 1b's
    healthy drives showed it fires by chance on most of them, so it was replaced before any damaged drive was read
    (Findings, 1b).

The label is tagged transient or persisting as in P1.1.1. The split reads P1.1's own evidence the same way: surgical
damage costs the linear channel about twice the kNN channel, so `degradation` still catches it. Collapse hits kNN harder
at total collapse ([P1.2.5](P1.2.5.md)'s Part E; at P1.1.1's milder dial the two channels lose about equally), which is
P1.4's collapse question, read on projector and backbone RankMe.

**Terms.** A *drive* is one run of the deployment stream: one pass over the stream's training images, 1,692 steps. Every
drive is one of two kinds, set by the order the images arrive in. A **walk** sees them in regime order, the way a car
would: daytime clear, the other daytime weathers, dawn/dusk, then night. An **IID drive** sees the same images shuffled,
so every regime is mixed in from the start. That is the whole difference: same images, same steps, same batch sizes,
same recipe. A **branch** continues a walk from a saved checkpoint (Part 2), and its **dose-0 twin** is the branch with
no damage.

Why both kinds. An IID drive has no regime order, so nothing in it can be a regime-driven loss: any panel firing on one
is a false alarm by construction. That is why the bands are fitted on IID drives and the false-alarm bars count them. A
walk is what P1.3 monitors. Its fires are findings to explain, not false alarms.

**Conditions.** P1.2.5's, throughout: BDD train, its enlarged selection set, one pass of 1,692 steps, the walk and its
IID control, the full health vector every 25 steps, checkpoints at steps 100, 212, 423, 846, 1,269 and the end. The MAE
arm has no drives on this split, so it gets its own settled-head well and its walks re-run. DINO-init DINO needs no
well: DINO's full checkpoint resumes intact, as in P1.2.5. Its projector surface is the DINO head's 256-d bottleneck, as
P1.2.5 logged it.

**What one pass cannot settle.** P1.3's horizon is longer than one pass. P1.1.4's bands cover one pass. Past it, P1.3
extends them with its own IID controls, by the same method. If P1.3's tuning changes an arm's recipe, the arm's bands
are re-checked the same way.

Four parts, cheapest first. Parts 1a and 4 need no new runs.

### 1. The healthy null and the bands, per arm

**1a — Preliminary, no runs.** On both JE arms and on DINO-init DINO, from P1.2.5's corpora. DINO-init DINO has only 3
IID seeds. Its null has 56 splits, and no threshold can be fitted, because each leave-one-out fit needs 3 seeds. So its
1a read is descriptive: the null, the bands, and each walk's largest *z* per panel.

- *The null* ([P1.1.0](P1.1.0.md)'s method). Walk against IID, 5 seeds each, per instrument: the exact 252-split
    permutation test with the maximum over checkpoints taken inside each split. Which instruments does correlation alone
    move on P1.3's setup?
- *The rule* ([P1.1.1](P1.1.1.md)'s). Bands and panel thresholds fitted on the 5 IID drives, leave-one-out. Then each
    walk read against them. Which instruments fire on a healthy walk?
- *A DINO-against-MAE-init comparison* (D2's question, descriptive). How the two JE arms' band widths and walk-vs-IID
    gaps differ, instrument by instrument.

Five seeds underestimated a random-walk-like spread in P1.1.1, so 1a is read as a first look. It sets no threshold P1.3
uses.

**Settings, fixed before reading** (1a and 1b). P1.1.1's panels and settings, with the canary split as above: the
label-free panel (projector and backbone RankMe, projector uniformity, projector and backbone CKA drift, cosine drift;
on MAE the backbone ones and uniformity), the linear canary panel and the kNN canary panel (each pooled and per
time-of-day stratum), the canary smoothed over 5 readings, the label-free signals raw, the local-SD floor at 0.25, α =
0.05 per panel, and readings after a batch under 8 images skipped (on P1.2.5's 25-step corpora no reading follows such a
batch, so the skip never applies). One setting is rescaled. P1.2.5 logs every 25 steps, 69 readings per drive, where
P1.1.1 read ~120. So the local-SD span covers the same share of the drive: ±8 readings, where P1.1.1 used ±15.

**The in-drive canary against the referee.** Both read the same mean-pooled embedding, but on different frames: the
canary on the train-side canary reservation, with 900 support images, and the referee on val, with a fit pool of ~3,500.
1a compares them per seed at the walks' and IID drives' ends, on scene kNN and linear, pooled and per time of day. The
question is whether the canary sees the walk-vs-IID losses the referee sees (DINO-init SimSiam's scene kNN, −3.8 on
val). The canary is read as the rule reads it, the mean of its last 5 readings. The rule, fixed before reading: if the
canary's walk-vs-IID gap has a 95% interval (Welch, over seeds) that excludes the referee's gap, the canary misses the
referee's loss; if the interval covers both zero and the referee's gap, the miss may be power alone; if the referee's
own interval covers zero, there is nothing to miss. Beside it, the paired *gap of gaps*: per drive, canary minus
referee, walk against IID, by exact permutation. It asks directly whether the two disagree on the walk's cost.

**1b — Full.** Per arm, IID seeds 6–15 join seeds 1–5, all saving the checkpoints above. On DINO-init DINO, seeds 4–15
join seeds 1–3. Those three logged the health vector but saved only their end, so on this arm the ladder's and the
per-block readers' IID bands come from seeds 4–15. Every new drive is read on the ladder at every checkpoint, every rung
(kNN, linear, the 10- and 100-shot fine-tunes) on scene and traffic light. Those reads give the fine-tune rung's healthy
range for the `degradation` rule, and the referee's own walk-vs-IID null. The MAE arm first gets its settled-head well
on P1.2.5's split, then its walk × 5 (seeds 1–5) and IID × 5 (seeds 1–5). The rule is calibrated on IID seeds 1–10, as
P1.1.1 calibrated on 10, and checked on fresh seeds 11–15. The null is re-read as walk × 5 against IID × 15, an exact
permutation over all 15,504 splits.

**Canary power, corrected for B2.** How small a walk-vs-IID gap can the null's test see? P1.1.0 answered with a
single-checkpoint *t*-test's minimum detectable difference (MDD). But the verdicts come from the permutation test's
maximum over checkpoints, which needs a larger gap. So here the power is measured for the test actually run, by
injection:

1. Take an arm's 15 healthy IID drives, with their real canary trajectories (real seed-to-seed and reading-to-reading
    noise).
1. Pick 5 of them to play the walk. Subtract a known drop from their canary readings, leaving the other 10 untouched.
1. Run the exact walk-vs-IID test (the max over checkpoints, all 3,003 splits of 15 into 5 + 10) and record whether it
    calls the gap at α = 0.05.
1. Repeat over 200 random choices of the 5, and find by bisection the drop caught 80% of the time. That is the MDD.

Three drop shapes: sustained over the whole drive, over its last quarter only, and at a single (the last) reading. A
sustained drop is also started at step 100, because at the first readings the healthy seeds nearly coincide. Per canary
channel, pooled and per time of day. This is the power of the *group* comparison P1.3 runs against its matched controls
(5 walks against 10 IID drives). A *single* drive's call is a different test, and Part 2's damage measures that one. In
1a there is no spare IID seed to inject into, so only P1.1.0's single-checkpoint figure is reported there.

**Pass bars, per arm and panel** (label-free, linear canary, kNN canary):

- Out-of-sample false alarms (leave-one-out over seeds 1–10, plus fresh seeds 11–15) at most 2 of 15. That is P1.1.1's
    result, written as a bar.
- The `degradation` label fires on at most 1 of the 15 out-of-sample IID drives.
- Reported beside it: the threshold; the walk's fires, instrument by instrument, as in P1.1.1; the Wilson interval on
    the false-alarm count; and the drive-level rate, either panel firing, since a drive is read on both (the audit's
    B3).

### 2. A degradation of known size on the DINO-start and JE arms, and their reader screen

**The damage.** P1.1.1's surgical branches, on both JE arms and on DINO-init DINO. On DINO-init DINO the student and the
teacher are damaged identically, and the centre is left as it stands. Each branch resumes a healthy walk from its
step-846 checkpoint, with seeds 1–3. Step 846 is the last checkpoint before the stream reaches night: the daytime
regimes are behind it and dawn/dusk is under way. The branch damages the encoder, then drives the rest of the walk (846
steps), saving checkpoints at 1,269 and the end. A resumed branch restarts the optimizer, so every dose is read against
a **dose-0 twin** resumed the same way.

**Why artificial damage, and what it can show.** Gaussian noise on the weights models no failure a deployed encoder
meets. Natural forgetting is structured: it sits in particular blocks (P1.1.3 found MAE's in the middle ones) and on the
regimes the stream has left. Noise is used anyway, as P1.1.1 used it, because it is the only damage available at a
known, graded size, on every arm, on demand. That makes it a **screen**, not a certificate.

- *What it can show.* A reader that misses a 10–15 point information loss is useless, and the noise ladder finds that
    cheaply, with a dose-response curve rather than one case.

- *What it cannot.* A reader can pass by sensing that "the weights were scrambled" without sensing information loss.
    That is the trap P0.5.3 fell into. Two things guard against it: the specificity control (weights move a lot, the
    information stays), and the natural failures (Part G's unprotected JE walks here, the old MAE recipe in Part 3),
    which make the final call on a reader.

- *The dose ladder.* Gaussian noise on every weight matrix of all 12 blocks, at four multiples of each tensor's own SD.
    The multiples are picked by a probe-only **dose pilot** (P1.1.1's [`dose_pilot.py`](../../../scripts/dose_pilot.py))
    on each arm's step-846 states, seeds 1–3, to span a linear-canary dent of about 2 to 15 points on the seed mean. The
    ladder is recorded before any branch runs (Findings, the doses).

- *The specificity control.* Re-initializing the top blocks: a large weight change that may leave the information
    intact. A reader should stay quiet on it, so it tests whether a reader reacts to information loss or to benign
    weight movement. It counts as a control on an arm only if the information really is intact:

    - *In the pilot,* the linear canary's cost is under 1 point on the 3-seed mean, with no seed over 2. The larger
        re-init that passes is used, top 4 before top 2. Why these two numbers:
        - *1 point on the mean* is about the smallest loss the canary resolves at all: held over a whole drive from step
            100, the group test catches 0.6–1.2 linear points (Findings, 1b). A cost under it is a cost the canary could not
            have seen, so a reader that fires there is not reacting to anything the label-side instrument registers.
        - *2 points on any seed* is about the smallest loss the canary resolves at one reading (1.9–3.4 linear points). The
            mean alone can hide one damaged seed: MAE-init JE's re-init 4 averages 0.9, but one seed costs 3.6. Each seed's
            branch is read as its own drive, so each seed must hold the bound, at the resolution a single reading has.
        - *Why the linear canary:* it is the panel that reads information (Design, the two canary panels).
        - *Why not tighter:* below these bounds the pilot cannot tell a cost from its own noise, so a stricter rule would
            only reject controls at random. The confirmation below carries the rest.
    - *The kNN canary's cost does not disqualify it.* Following the split canary, a kNN loss with the information intact
        is `geometry`, not damage. A control that moves the geometry while keeping the information is the sharper test: a
        reader that fires there is a geometry reader, which may serve P1.3's selection read, but it fails as a degradation
        reader.
    - *In Stage 2,* the control status is confirmed on the referee. The pilot reads only the canary, at the moment of
        damage. The referee reads more tasks and rungs, and reads them again as the branch trains on. The rule: at the
        branch's start state, step 1,269 and the end, no linear or fine-tune read, on scene or traffic light, may fall
        more than one twin SD below the twin on the seed mean. The difference is seed-paired (branch minus twin), and the
        twin SD is the spread of the 3 twins' reads at that checkpoint.
        - *Why one SD, and not a significance test.* With 3 seeds a test would almost never reject, so a control could carry
            a 2-point loss and still pass. The two possible errors are not equally costly. Accepting a damaged control fails
            every reader that rightly fires on it, for specificity. Rejecting an intact one costs little: it is set aside
            (next point), not turned into a dose. So the bar sits at the read's own noise level, and errs toward rejecting.
        - *When the bound was set.* The design asked only for a read "against the twins' spread". The one-SD bound was
            chosen after MAE-init JE's referee reads had been seen, and before the other three arms' were. What carries the
            verdicts is a read at the branch's start state, before it trains, lower than its twin on all three seeds on
            every arm (Findings, Parts 2 and 3).
        - *kNN is exempt again,* as in the pilot: a kNN loss with the information intact is `geometry`.
    - *A re-init that passes the pilot but not the referee is read descriptively.* Its reads and its fires are reported,
        but it counts for no reader's bar, neither as a control nor as a dose. This is the user's call, and the reasoning
        runs as follows.
        - *Not a control.* The referee shows a loss, so a reader firing there may be right. Scoring it as a control would
            fail a reader for seeing a real loss.
        - *Not a dose either.* The losses that disqualify a control are small: 1–2.5 points, about one twin SD, on one or
            two reads at a time (Findings, Part 2). The smallest noise dose on the same arms costs 3–6 scene linear points
            on the referee. As a dose, any seed the label happened to call would demand an immediate fire on a loss the size
            of the referee's noise. The bar would then test the re-init's ambiguity, not the reader.
        - *What is lost.* The re-init was the one probe that moves the weights a lot while keeping the information: the
            sharpest guard against P0.5.3's trap. That guard now rests on the twins (a restart, no information lost), the
            healthy walks, and the natural failures. The noise doses carry sensitivity as before.
    - An arm where no re-init passes the pilot reads re-init 4 as one more dose (DINO-init DINO). There the canary saw the
        cost, so the label's call on it is a loss of the designed size.

- *The arbiter.* A damage dose counts as **called** on a seed when Part 1b's adjudication reads it as `degradation`.
    Beside that, the full ladder (kNN, linear and both fine-tunes, on scene and traffic light) on val at the branch's
    start state (after the damage), step 1,269 and the end of every branch and twin.

- *Read through the drive.* Every reader is read at every logged reading from the damage on, every 25 steps, not only at
    the end: the bands are restricted to the branch's stretch of the grid. Sensitivity asks for a fire at the first
    reading after the damage; specificity asks for quiet at every reading.

- *The restart, and how it is kept out of the verdicts.* A branch restarts the optimizer and the augmentation draws,
    because the walks' checkpoints hold the model only. So a branch is not the walk's exact continuation, even at dose
    0: MAE-init JE's first twin matches its walk at step 846, but by step 1,175 its CKA drift is 0.40 against the walk's
    0.35 (P1.1.2 saw drift fire on every restart). Every branch and its twin share the restart: same start state, same
    restart, same seed. Two reads follow from that.

    - *Paired reads cancel it.* The damage's size (each branch minus its twin, seed by seed) and the control's
        confirmation on the referee subtract the twin, and with it the restart. What is left is the damage.
    - *Single-drive reads do not.* A reader's verdict on a branch is read against the IID bands, as a deployed monitor
        would read it, and the IID drives never restarted. So a reader that reacts to restarts fires on every branch,
        damaged or not, and its fires on damaged branches would pass for sensitivity. The twin bar is what catches this: a
        reader that fires on the twins fails specificity, so its sensitivity counts for nothing. The twin bar is applied
        as a bar, not as a caveat. It is also fair to the monitor: a deployed drive can restart too (a resume from a
        model-only checkpoint, a federated round that resets the optimizer).
    - *Measured, not assumed.* Each reader's restart effect is reported directly: twin against its own walk, at the same
        seed and steps, from step 846 on. A reader that fails the twin bar can then be read on the paired branch-minus-
        twin contrast instead, as a candidate whose restart sensitivity P1.3 would have to remove. That is a finding, not
        a pass.

**The readers screened** ([P1.1.2](P1.1.2.md)'s screen, on the same three arms):

- every label-free signal in the health vector, backbone and projector surfaces: RankMe, uniformity, alignment, CKA
    drift, cosine drift, feature variance, off-diagonal covariance;
- per block, from the checkpoints: plain and debiased CKA drift, and RankMe, on the canary's probe images, anchored at
    step 0 (the frozen start, which is the floor every P1.2 gain is measured against);
- [P1.1.3](P1.1.3.md)'s middle-block panel, blocks 4–11, as P1.1.3 fixed it. It was chosen on MAE-objective data, so on
    JE it is a test, not a refit.

Each reader is read as a single-drive panel against bands from IID seeds 1–10 at the same checkpoints (floor 0.25, α =
0.05; span 3 for the per-block readers, as P1.1.2's per-block panels, and span 8 for the 25-step health vector, as Part
1).

**Pass bar, per reader and arm** (P1.1.2's bar, with P1.1.3's single-drive conditions):

- *Sensitive.* Fires on every seed of every called dose, at the first reading after the damage: step 850 for the health
    vector (its first logged reading), the start state at step 846 for the per-block readers. A fire by step 1,269 is
    reported beside it.
- *Specific.* Quiet on the 3 dose-0 twins at every reading after their first. Quiet on at least 4 of 5 healthy walks, at
    every checkpoint. Leave-one-out false alarms on 10 IID drives at most 2 of 10 (seeds 1–10 for the health vector; for
    the per-block readers, the IID drives with every checkpoint saved: seeds 6–15 on the JE arms, 4–13 on DINO-init
    DINO, 1–10 on MAE). Quiet on the re-init branch, where it counts as a control.
- *Why a twin's first reading is skipped.* At dose 0 it is the walk's own state: the step-846 checkpoint itself for the
    per-block readers, 4 steps past it for the health vector. A fire there is the walk's, which the walks bar already
    counts. The design said both "at their end" (here) and "at every reading" (Read through the drive). The read settled
    it as every reading after the first, the user's call: stricter than the end alone, and fair to the twins.
- *This rule was settled after the results, and it decides one verdict:* DINO-init DINO's middle-block panel. One of its
    twins (seed 3) fires at its first reading only, the walk's own step-846 checkpoint, where walk seed 3 fires too and
    is counted under the walks bar. The panel passes under "at their end" and under "after the first", and fails only
    under the strictest wording, "at every reading". No other verdict changes between the three.

**A natural JE degradation, descriptive.** Part G's walks without the protections lose kNN and fine-tune accuracy
against the protected walks from the first checkpoints, on both JE arms, with the same seeds and checkpoints. On
DINO-init SimSiam at 3e-5, scene kNN ends 10.6 points and the 100-shot fine-tune 5.9 points below the protected walk.
That is a kNN loss with a fine-tune loss beside it, the `degradation` pattern. Every reader that passes is read on them.
The catch is the confound P1.1.3 met with the lr-1e-4 walk: without layer decay the early blocks move more, so a reader
can separate the two recipes by reading the lever rather than the loss. So this read has no bar. Its question is whether
the reader's contrast follows the loss across checkpoints.

**A natural DINO collapse, descriptive.** P1.2.5's Part E ran DINO-init DINO's objective, unswitched, at 3e-4 and 1e-3
for 600 steps, with the health vector logged. At 1e-3 it collapses on its own: backbone RankMe falls from 276 to 7 and
the canary's kNN read loses 11.7 points. Every DINO-init DINO reader that passes is read on these two drives, against
the arm's bands, for when it first fires. One seed each, and the rate is again the lever, so this read has no bar
either.

### 4. The referee's power on past regimes

**The referee.** P1.2's readout ladder, on the val pool, sliced by regime as P1.2.5's Part F slices it (time of day ×
weather, a slice read if each class keeps at least 20 frames). Read at every checkpoint. Following P1.2.5's handover,
quality is judged on the linear and fine-tune rungs, and kNN is a constraint and a warning. So the power read covers the
10- and 100-shot fine-tunes as well as kNN and linear.

**The test P1.3 runs.** For a regime the stream has left at checkpoint *t*₀, the loss at a later checkpoint *t* is the
fall in its Δ (arm minus floor) from *t*₀ to *t*. P1.3 asks whether that loss is real at any later checkpoint, so the
test is a maximum over checkpoints, family-wise over the regimes read.

**The read.** From P1.2.5's per-image reads at every walk checkpoint, both JE arms and DINO-init DINO: the seed × image
bootstrap spread of each slice's change between checkpoints, and its correlation across checkpoints. From these, by
simulation:

- per slice and rung, the MDD of the max-over-checkpoints test at α = 0.05 (Holm over slices) and 80% power, at the
    current pool size;
- the pool size each slice needs to reach the target, scaling the image component by 1/√*n*. The seed component does not
    shrink with the pool. It sets a floor only more seeds can lower, so the seed count needed is reported too.

**The target, a proposal for the user to confirm.** Resolve a past-regime loss of **2 points** on the linear and
fine-tune rungs. On the gate tasks, P1.3's JE recipes gain 2–4.4 points of linear accuracy over one pass (P1.2.5): 2
points is about half the scene gain and the whole traffic-light gain. A referee that cannot see a loss the size of the
gain cannot protect it. The read also reports what 1 point would take.

**4b — Breadth, with the power to call it.** P1.2.5 left breadth open, not answered no: three panel tasks gain 1.0–1.7
points, under the ~2 points its val pool resolves. Its handover asks P1.3 for a held-out pool that resolves about 1
point per task, bus-sized tasks included. The same machinery sizes it: the end-of-drive Δ against the floor per panel
task, its seed and image noise from P1.2.5's reads, and the pool and seed count that resolve 1 point with Holm over the
panel. The likely answer is about 4× the images, which BDD val alone cannot supply for the three scenes. So 4b also
lists the sources that could: all of val's query frames at a smaller fit fraction, a train-side held-out reservation
taken before the stream (P1.2.0 used 14,000 such frames), or more seeds where the seed part dominates. Which source P1.3
uses is the user's decision.

**No pass bar.** Part 4 sizes P1.3's held-out pools. P1.3 pre-registers the target and the sizes.

### The gate P1.3 inherits

Per arm, DINO-init DINO included, P1.3 reads degradation only once:

1. Part 1b's three panels and the `degradation` label pass their false-alarm bars on that arm — on the recipe P1.3
    reads, so after P1.3's retuning, re-fitted by Part 1b's procedure on its own IID controls; and
1. Part 4 has sized the arm's per-regime and breadth pools, and P1.3 has pre-registered the target.

A label-free reader is not part of the gate. P1.3 reads degradation through labels, against matched controls, as P1.1
always intended. If no label-free reader passes on an arm, that arm's monitor is labels-only. That is a finding for the
Phase-3 closed loop, which needs a label-free channel, not a block on P1.3.

### Engineering

To be built and tested before any run:

- **Branches from P1.2.5's checkpoints.** P1.1.1's `resume` / `damage` path on a ViT-B with the protections' parameter
    groups and P1.2.5's split; a check that the branch's step-846 state reproduces the walk's own step-846 read at dose
    0\. *Checked* on seed 1 of all three existing arms, on CPU. At dose 0 the branch's whole method equals the saved
    step-846 file bit for bit. The ladder's loader builds the same read encoder, and its read of real val frames is
    identical. What this does not re-check is the resumed stream's batches. P1.1.1's unit test and the P1.1 audit (A1)
    cover those.
- **A branch continues the drive's schedules.** DINO's teacher momentum ramps from 0.996 to 1 over the drive. Its step
    counter is not in the checkpoint, so a branch resumed at step 846 used to restart the ramp from 0.996. It then
    reached only about 0.998 by the end, where the walk reaches 1. A dose and its twin would still pair, but the twin
    would not be the healthy walk's continuation, and Part 2 reads twins against the walk's bands. The harness now
    passes the resume step to `set_horizon`, with a unit test. A learning-rate schedule would restart the same way.
    P1.1.4's recipes use none.
- **Damage on DINO's method.** `damage_method` gives the student and the teacher the identical perturbation: the same
    re-drawn weights under re-init, and the same additive delta under noise, sized by the teacher's tensors. So two
    equal backbones stay equal, and the teacher's lag behind the student is kept. The centre and the heads are
    untouched. Methods without a student are damaged exactly as P1.1.1 damaged them. The harness, `dose_pilot.py` and
    the block-feature rebuild all use it. Unit tests cover dose 0 as a no-op on the whole method, equal staying equal,
    the kept lag, P1.1.1's draw without a twin, and a mismatched twin refused. *Checked* on a real step-846 DINO-init
    DINO state: a 0.3-SD noise dose changes teacher and student by 30% each, with their gap kept to 6e-8. DINO-init
    DINO's dose pilot can now run, so it can join the other pilots.
- **A branch saves its own start state.** With `resume.save_start`, the harness checkpoints the branch right after the
    damage, before its first step (`step00846.pt` in the branch's own dir), beside step 1,269 and the end. The ladder
    reads it like any checkpoint, so the post-damage read needs no rebuilding. An end-to-end test checks that the saved
    start is the parent state with only the damaged blocks changed.
- **Branches cannot resume the optimizer.** The walks' checkpoints hold the model only, so a branch restarts the
    optimizer and the augmentation draws (Design, Part 2: the restart). P1.1.4 handles it by pairing and by the twin
    bar. If P1.3 means to branch from its drives, its checkpoints should also hold the optimizer and RNG state, about
    twice the size on disk.
- **The MAE wells on P1.2.5's split.** One with the 512-d × 8 decoder (the repaired arm), one with the 64-d × 2 decoder
    (the natural failure).
- **Block features from P1.2.5's checkpoints.** [`block_features.py`](../../../cafl4ds/block_features.py) on each arm's
    method, on the canary's probe images, anchored at step 0.
- **DINO reads its encoder twice.** Found in Stage 2: DINO's `embedding_surfaces` runs the teacher once per surface, so
    the block hooks caught two passes (24 outputs for 12 blocks) and the extraction stopped. `_one_pass` now keeps the
    first pass, after checking that the repeats match (to 1e-5), and refuses a capture that is not whole passes. Unit
    tests cover a method read twice, partial and differing captures, and DINO's teacher read once. DINO-init DINO's
    features were re-extracted on card 0. No other arm was affected: their methods read the encoder once.
- **DINO-init DINO's health-vector drift has no common anchor.** The harness anchors CKA and cosine drift to a well, and
    DINO-init DINO has none, so each run anchors to its own first reading. A branch is a new run: its drift restarts at
    0 at step 846, where the walks' and IID drives' count from step 0. The four drift signals (CKA and cosine, backbone
    and projector) are therefore unreadable on this arm's branches and are left out of its screen. The per-block drift
    is unaffected: it is anchored at DINO's released checkpoint for every run. The four signals stay in the branches'
    split labels, where they change no call. *For P1.3:* a DINO-start drive should anchor its drift at the released
    checkpoint too.
- **The power simulation** (Part 4), with unit tests on known cases.
- **Torch on 8 threads** for every CPU read: P1.1's SVD-based reads ran about 100× slower when they shared the cores
    with HPU jobs.

    100 the old recipe sits 3.0 points below the floor on scene kNN, 2.3 on linear and 2.2 on the 100-shot fine-tune,
    where the repaired walks stay within about 1.5 points. The middle panel fires on all 3 failing walks at step 100
    (the bar: 2 of 3 by step 423), and so do both CKA panels. *The confound:* the panel reads *z* 42–44, the level of
    the 12–15 point doses, for a 2–3 point loss. The health vector fires at the step-0 reading, which follows a single
    update. So the panel reads the recipe change (the small decoder, no protections) at least as much as the loss. This
    validates the panel's direction on a natural failure, not its scale.
- **DINO-init DINO's collapse drives: every health-vector reader fires early.** At 3e-4 every reader fires by step 100;
    at 1e-3 by step 50, with backbone RankMe at *z* 228. These drives saved only their end, so the per-block panels read
    nothing earlier. Where Part 1a found the healthy walk's projector drifting toward the collapse mode, these drives
    show the health vector does see a collapse that happens.
- **The JE arms' unprotected walks: every panel fires, which says nothing.** They lose kNN and fine-tune accuracy
    against the protected walks, and every per-block panel fires on them by step 100. But the protected walks fire too,
    so a fire cannot separate the loss from the walk.

### 4. The referee's power on past regimes, every rung

The MDD of the max-over-checkpoints test, for a loss sustained after the stream leaves the slice, at 5 seeds and the
current pool, α split over the slices read. The seed part of the noise is a bootstrap over 5 seeds, scaled by √(5/4) so
its variance is unbiased ([P1.1 audit](../audits/P1.1.md), G4). Each cell is daytime / dawn-dusk (2,545 / 412 scene
frames, 1,458 / 242 traffic-light frames). Points of balanced accuracy.

| Arm | Task | kNN | Linear | 10-shot | 100-shot |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| DINO-init SimSiam | scene | 4.6 / 4.4 | 2.9 / 5.2 | 4.2 / 4.1 | 1.9 / 3.2 |
|  | traffic light | 2.5 / 7.6 | 2.5 / 5.3 | 4.2 / 3.2 | 1.5 / 3.1 |
| MAE-init JE | scene | 2.4 / 4.8 | 1.4 / 3.3 | 3.2 / 3.4 | 1.9 / 3.7 |
|  | traffic light | 3.3 / 4.9 | 2.5 / 5.2 | 2.1 / 2.3 | 1.2 / 2.6 |
| DINO-init DINO | scene | 0.9 / 1.5 | 1.3 / 1.0 | 0.6 / 0.8 | 0.4 / 0.3 |
|  | traffic light | 1.7 / 2.1 | 1.5 / 1.3 | 0.5 / 0.5 | 0.6 / 0.4 |
| MAE | scene | 1.2 / 3.0 | 2.0 / 3.7 | 1.8 / 4.0 | 1.0 / 3.3 |
|  | traffic light | 1.3 / 3.5 | 2.1 / 6.1 | 2.3 / 2.5 | 1.3 / 2.6 |

- **The 100-shot fine-tune is the best-resolved rung.** On the daytime slice it reaches the 2-point target on both
    SimSiam-objective arms and both tasks, at the current pool and 5 seeds (1.2–1.9 points). Per weather regime, at 10
    seeds, it reaches 2 points in 16 of 20 cells: 14 of them with 1–10× the pool, and two only at 33–51×, where the seed
    part alone nearly fills the target. kNN and linear reach it in 16 of 40.
- **The 10-shot fine-tune is the noisiest.** Its seed part dominates, up to 3.5× the image part, because each seed's
    few-shot draws differ. More frames barely help it; more seeds or more draws do. On DINO-init SimSiam's scene it
    cannot reach 2 points at 10 seeds with any pool.
- **The fine-tune rungs show past-regime losses too.** By the drive's end, DINO-init SimSiam's daytime scene 100-shot
    read sits 1.7 points below its value when the stream left daytime (its MDD is 1.9). MAE-init JE's 10-shot read sits
    2.7 points below (its MDD is 3.2). Both sit just under what the test resolves. Beside the kNN losses found earlier,
    this is the `degradation` pattern on a past regime.
- **DINO-init DINO's tight numbers come from its encoder going still, not from a sharper referee.** Its read encoder is
    the teacher, whose momentum ramps to 1 over the drive. Between step 1,269 and the end, only 1–1.5% of its val reads
    change, against 13–32% on the SimSiam-objective arms. A still encoder cannot forget, so this power read says nothing
    for P1.3. Over P1.3's longer horizon, the schedule decides whether this arm can forget at all. Ramped to 1 once over
    the whole horizon, its read encoder goes still for a growing stretch of the stream; ramped per pass, it moves in
    every pass.
- **MAE resolves the daytime slice to 1–2 points on every rung, and shows little past-regime loss.** Its 100-shot
    fine-tune reaches 1.0 (scene) and 1.3 (traffic light) at the current pool. Per weather regime at 10 seeds, the
    100-shot fine-tune reaches 2 points in all 10 cells with 1–7× the pool; kNN and linear in 13 of 20, one only at
    about 100×. On the time-of-day slices its largest loss since leaving the slice is 1.5 points (daytime scene 10-shot,
    under its MDD of 1.8). Part of its tightness is a quieter encoder: between step 1,269 and the end, 4–9% of its scene
    kNN and linear reads change, against 12–19% on the JE arms. Unlike DINO-init DINO's, it is not still, so the power
    read applies to P1.3's MAE arm.

### 4b. Breadth: what resolves 1 point

P1.2.5's Part C panel: the end-of-drive linear Δ against the floor, one-sided, at Holm's strictest step (α = 0.0125).
Per cell, the MDD at the current pool and 5 seeds, then the pool multiple that brings it to 1 point at 5 / 10 seeds. "—"
means no pool up to 1,024× reaches it, because the seed part alone is too large.

| Task (frames) | DINO-init SimSiam | MAE-init JE | DINO-init DINO | MAE |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| Weather (4,087) | 2.5; 16× / 9× | 3.1; — / 25× | 2.4; 6× / 6× | 2.6; 7× / 7× |
| Large vehicle (3,434) | 2.0; — / 6× | 2.0; 6× / 5× | 1.6; 3× / 3× | 2.0; 13× / 7× |
| Bus (1,314) | 4.7; — / — | 4.4; — / 38× | 4.6; 27× / 24× | 4.3; — / 166× |
| Pedestrian (2,996) | 2.6; 11× / 8× | 2.7; 32× / 10× | 2.6; 49× / 10× | 2.7; 9× / 8× |

- **The current pool resolves 1.6–3.1 points, and 4.3–4.7 on bus.** That matches P1.2.5's "about 2 points".
- **On the linear rung, images dominate the noise.** The image SD is 1.7–12× the seed SD on every task. So a larger pool
    is the lever, and more seeds help little.
- **1 point needs 3–25× the pool at 10 seeds on three tasks (up to 49× at 5 seeds), and 24× or more on bus.** That is
    more than the design's "about 4×". The design reckoned an interval's half-width, and this is the MDD at 80% power
    under Holm, about 1.6× larger. Read as a half-width instead, every multiple shrinks by about 2.5×.
- **BDD val alone gives at most 1.7×.** The query is 60% of val's frames at a fit fraction of 0.4, and the fit pool
    cannot vanish. A train-side held-out reservation, taken before the stream, is the only source that reaches 5–25×.
    Bus at 1 point is out of reach within BDD by any route.
- **MAE's panel sits where the JE arms' does.** Its walks end 1.5 points above the floor on weather and large vehicle,
    and within 0.5 on bus and pedestrian, on the linear rung. Its MDDs (2.0–2.7, 4.3 on bus) and pool multiples (7–8× at
    10 seeds, 166× on bus) are in the same range, so the train-side reservation sized here serves it too.

## Handover

Every run is done and read. What is left belongs to the [P1.1 audit](../audits/P1.1.md): its remaining remediations,
which keep the P1.1 study open.

**Where things are.** Branch `feat/P1.1.4`, from the main checkout. `outputs/p114` links to the project share. CPU reads
use `uv run --extra cpu`, torch capped at 8 threads beside HPU jobs. Every log in `outputs/p114/*.log` is free of
`Traceback` and `QUERY MISMATCH`. A re-run overwrote its log, so earlier failed attempts are not kept (`panel_mae`'s
first attempts, on a card that would not acquire before a reboot, are gone).

*Provenance.* No run manifest records its commit: the harness read it from the installed package's version, which the
container's image install does not carry, so every manifest says `git_sha: null`. The harness now reads the checkout's
HEAD ([P1.1 audit](../audits/P1.1.md), G11). Some run sets also started on code committed only afterwards, and the
launcher was committed to while it ran, so the code behind a run is the branch's history around its start time, not a
recorded commit. The reports were re-run on the final code and reproduce bit for bit.

- *Runs:* the MAE wells (`wells/p114/`); the Stage 1 drives (`drive/`), every one with its 6 checkpoints; the dose
    pilots (`pilot/`, `pilot_fine/`); the 72 branches (`branch/<arm>_<kind>`, seeds 1–3); the ladders (`ladder/`),
    including `mae_panel`, Part 4b's MAE read; and the block features (`features/<arm>`, plus `features/mae_fail`).
- *Ladder naming:* P1.1.4's ladders name encoders by **drive seed** (`iid_s6_t00100`), P1.2.5's by seed index
    (`walk_s0_*` = drive seed 1). `{dir, names: seed}` in the config maps the first to the second, in every mode.
- *The launcher:* **never edit `run_p114.sh` in place while a process runs it** (bash reads by offset): write a new file
    and `mv` it over. Dry-run it only against a scratch share, because its `ladder` step makes claim dirs.

**For P1.3** (beyond the gate and the decisions below):

- *Save checkpoints at a steady cadence on every drive.* The label-free readers that pass (MAE's CKA panels, DINO-init
    DINO's middle panel) read the per-block features of saved checkpoints, not the 25-step health vector.
- *Anchor a DINO start's drift at the released checkpoint* (Engineering: DINO-init DINO's drift had no common anchor).
- *Save the optimizer and RNG state* if P1.3 branches from its drives (Engineering: branches restart the optimizer).
- *Keep provenance whole.* Commit before launching, check the manifests record the commit, and have launchers append to
    their logs rather than overwrite them.
- *Re-check DINO-init DINO's middle-panel pass* on P1.3's horizon and momentum schedule. Its narrow bands come from an
    encoder going still (Findings, Parts 2 and 3).

**Decisions taken** (the user's calls; P1.3 pre-registers them):

1. *The past-regime target is 2 points* on the linear and fine-tune rungs (Part 4). P1.3 also reports what 1 point would
    take.
1. *Forgetting reads are pre-registered per time of day.* Per weather regime, P1.3 tests the 100-shot fine-tune at 10
    seeds in the cells where Part 4 shows 2 points reachable within the pool it can build. Every other weather-regime
    cell, and kNN and linear per regime, are read descriptively.
1. *Breadth's held-out pool is a train-side reservation taken before the stream* (Part 4b), sized from 4b's final read.
    "Resolve" means the MDD at 80% power under Holm, since that is the test P1.3 runs. Bus cannot reach 1 point inside
    BDD, so it is read at 2 points or descriptively.
1. *P1.3 retunes before its degradation reads.* So the gate's bands are the retuned recipes', fitted by Part 1b's
    procedure (10 calibration seeds, 5 fresh) on P1.3's own IID controls, and held to Part 1b's bars. P1.1.4 certifies
    the method, the reader screen and the referee's power on the handover recipes; Part 1a shows why the numbers must
    be re-fitted, since the rate moves the bands more than the start does.
1. *DINO-init DINO joins P1.3* as a fourth explored arm, beside the three. Its Stage 2 runs. Two design calls pass to
    P1.3 with it: its teacher-momentum schedule over the longer horizon (Part 4), and a collapse watch read against the
    walk's own healthy spread or on the backbone, not against the walk-vs-IID bands (Part 1a).
1. *The canary-against-referee check is decided by the paired gap of gaps,* by exact permutation over seeds. The Welch
    rule fixed before reading is kept in the report beside it; it misread 5 seeds with one outlier (Part 1a).
1. *The specificity control is judged on information, not geometry* (Design, Part 2): linear-canary cost under 1 point
    on the 3-seed mean, no seed over 2, the larger passing re-init; a kNN cost does not disqualify it; confirmed on the
    referee in Stage 2.
1. *The fine-tune corroboration is a calibrated panel* (Design, the two canary panels; Findings, 1b). The rule fixed
    first fired on most healthy drives. The consecutive-checkpoint variant is kept in the report for comparison.
1. *A control is confirmed on the referee at one twin SD* (Design, Part 2). No linear or fine-tune read may fall more
    than one twin SD below the twin. Erring toward rejection is cheap, because a rejected re-init is set aside.
1. *A re-init that fails the confirmation is read descriptively,* neither control nor dose (Design, Part 2). All three
    pilot-passing re-inits failed it, so no arm has a re-init specificity probe.
1. *The twin bar skips each twin's first reading,* which is the walk's own state (Design, Part 2's pass bar).
1. *A label-free degradation channel for the JE arms is a Phase-2 question.* No reader passes on either JE arm here,
    because the healthy walk itself leaves the IID bands. A different reference (a walk's own healthy spread, or its
    own path) is the likely fix, and it is better chosen once P1.3 has shown the longer-horizon dynamics. P1.3 reads
    degradation through labels on every arm, as its gate requires.

## How to run

On CPU, from the repo root, torch capped at 8 threads:

```bash
uv run python scripts/analyze_p114.py mode=bands out=outputs/p114/bands_1a.json
uv run python scripts/analyze_p114.py mode=bands arm_set=arms_1b out=outputs/p114/bands_1b.json  # ~1 h: the injection
uv run python scripts/analyze_p114.py mode=power out=outputs/p114/power_4.json
uv run python scripts/analyze_p114.py mode=breadth out=outputs/p114/breadth_4b.json
uv run python scripts/analyze_p114.py mode=branches arm_set=arms_1b out=outputs/p114/branches.json  # Parts 2 and 3
```

`only=[<arm>]` restricts `mode=branches` to the arms named.

Stage 1's runs, on both cards, from the main checkout: `run_p114.sh card0` and `run_p114.sh card1` side by side (about
13 hours each), then `run_p114.sh reads` (the ladder at every checkpoint, about 17 card-hours). Stage 2:
`run_p114.sh stage2 0` and `run_p114.sh stage2 1` side by side.
