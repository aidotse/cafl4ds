# CKA drift deep dive — information loss vs benign adaptation `[analysis + new runs]` (P1.1.3)

**What this substudy is.** The fourth P1.1 sub-study (see the [Phase 1 overview](index.md)). [P1.1.2](P1.1.2.md)
screened every label-free signal against MAE damage of known size. One survived: **CKA drift against the well**, the
existing `cka_drift`. It tracks the damage and stays quiet on a control that moves the weights at no cost to the canary.
Every Phase-0 MAE candidate failed again. P1.1.3 asks whether that survivor can become a usable MAE reader, and under
what assumptions, before P1.3 relies on it.

**Verdict: CKA drift's problem was where it was read, not how it was calibrated. Natural forgetting shows in the middle
blocks.** At the output, none of the three fixes to calibration or data selection passes its bar on BDD: reading only
the strata the walk has left behind, calibrating on healthy walks, or reading JE's natural lr-1e-4 loss. The
optimizer-restart jolt turns out to be brief on MAE (1–2 readings) and absent on JE. Re-running Phase 0's seed-stable
MAE forgetting with saved states, the forgetting arm drifts 1.4–1.8× more than its healthy twin in blocks 4–11, on 5 of
5 seeds — while the output Phase 0 read cannot tell the two apart. A follow-up reads those blocks as a single-drive
alarm. It passes the BDD damage screen with a wider margin than the output, and it catches 4 of 5 forgetting arms
without their twin, where the output catches none.

## Motivation

**What CKA drift reads.** It measures how far the representation's content has moved from the well. It cannot say
whether a representation is good. It can only say the representation has left a state we certified as good. That
narrowing assumption — a competent, certified anchor — is what lets it pass the damage screen.

**The catch is that two kinds of movement look alike to it.** Both damage and healthy adaptation move the model away
from the well. P1.1.2 found two symptoms of this.

- **It fires on benign movement.** On the healthy MAE walk it fires on 3 of 5 seeds. It also fires on every optimizer
    restart. Its margin over that movement is only about 2× at the doses the canary calls.
- **It was faint on natural forgetting.** Phase 0 read it on the same pretrained ViT-B MAE, on the old task's images,
    anchored at the end of phase A. At [P0.3.9](../phase0/P0.3.9.md)'s seed-stable 5/5 fire, forgetting cost the probe
    6.5–8.5 points, yet CKA drift moved only ~0.005–0.010. The forgetting arm drifted more than the healthy replay arm
    on 4 of 5 seeds, by at most 0.002. Surgical damage moves it far more per point of canary lost: noise σ0.5, a
    ~4-point dent, moves the BDD backbone read by ~0.04. Different data and probes, so this is rough — but it is about
    an order of magnitude.

**What this means for the idea of reading drift where forgetting lives.** The natural fix for the walk problem is to
read drift only on the regimes the walk has left behind: healthy adaptation should move the regime being streamed, and
information loss should show on the regimes left behind. Phase 0 already read drift that way — on the old task's images
only — and it was still faint. So the prior for this idea is unfavourable. It remains worth testing on BDD, because the
substrate and the kind of shift differ (a correlated walk of driving regimes, not a switch between datasets). But a
negative there would agree with Phase 0.

**Phase 0 is a prior here, not a verdict.** Its findings about the instrument carry over: same ViT-B, same estimator.
Its findings about whether a failure occurs, and how large, do not: they rest on Phase 0's diet and horizon, which is
what Phase 1 exists to replace. P0.3.9 also tested only half of what a reader needs. It probed *sensitivity* to natural
forgetting — of one kind: an abrupt dataset switch at a toy horizon, shallow and almost fully recoverable (savings ≈
1.0). It never probed *specificity* to healthy adaptation on a correlated stream; its healthy arm was replay training on
both tasks. P1.1.3 tests specificity on the BDD walk (items 1–2) and the mechanism on P0.3.9's failure (item 5).

The deeper question is item 5's. Is natural information loss simply invisible to the pooled output's CKA drift, or does
it show somewhere else — in some block, or under the debiased estimator? Phase 0 read the pooled output only, with the
plain estimator, on 600 images against 768 features, where the plain estimator is biased toward "no change".

**The central question.** Does CKA drift tell information loss from benign adaptation? Whether it sees damage is already
settled.

## Design

Five items, cheapest first. Items 1–4 need no new drives at first; item 5 re-runs a Phase-0 operating point. Each pass
bar is fixed here, before any reading.

| # | Item | Origin | Data | New runs |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| 1 | Past-regime CKA drift | the tangent | P1.1.2 features | none |
| 2 | Walk-aware null | option (a) | P1.1.0 walk corpus, P1.1.1 walk branches | 5 fresh MAE walks, if item 2's preliminary passes |
| 3 | The optimizer-restart jolt | P1.1.2 finding | P1.1.1 dose-0 twins | none |
| 4 | A natural degradation: JE's lr-1e-4 walk | P1.1.1 finding | P1.1.1 dial and branch corpora | none |
| 5 | Phase 0's natural MAE forgetting, re-read per block | Phase 0 | P0.3.9's operating point | 5 seeds × 2 arms, ~1.5 h on both cards (approved) |

**Read rules settled with the user before any data was read.**

- *"Immediately"* means the restart-free damage record each branch report carries: the damage applied to the step-65
    state, before any update. The branch corpus's first row already includes one update with a fresh optimizer, so
    reading it would test the restart, not the damage. The rest of each branch is reported as description.
- Item 1 reads the IID damage branches on the walk's past set at step 65 (daytime and dawn/dusk), so all 18 called seeds
    get the same read.
- Item 1's "better than pooled" compares both reads on the same night-block grid, each calibrated there on the IID
    drives. Better means fewer walk fires at no cost in sensitivity or specificity; a tie is settled by the margin
    (called-damage *z* over the worst healthy-walk *z*).
- Item 5's test statistic is the paired *t* per reader. Its maximum is taken over every tested lens and layer in one
    test. TwoNN is reported, not tested: it has no harmful direction, and a two-sided test over 5 seeds cannot reach
    1/32.
- Item 5 reproduces P0.3.9 if every seed's BWT, both arms, lands within ±0.005 of the promoted value, with the same gate
    verdict.

**Option (b) is a decision, not an item.** CKA drift already works as a *comparative* reader: one arm against a matched
control. That is how P1.3 reads its legs (b) and (c), so P1.3 can use it as it stands. P1.1.3 asks whether it can also
be a single-drive alarm, which only the deployed monitor (Phase 3) needs.

### 1. Past-regime CKA drift

- **Read.** Per time-of-day stratum (daytime, dawn/dusk, night), CKA drift against the well on that stratum's probe
    images only. A stratum is **past** at a checkpoint if the walk streamed it earlier and has moved on. It is
    **current** if the walk is streaming it, and **future** if not yet reached. The walk's schedule: daytime legs to
    step 55, dawn/dusk 56–63, night 65–118. So daytime is past from step 56, and both daytime and dawn/dusk are past
    from step 65 on.
- **Estimator.** Debiased linear CKA (`measurements.debiased_linear_cka`). Each stratum has 135 query + 135 support
    images, 270 rows against 768 features, where the plain estimator's upward bias is no longer negligible. Report the
    plain estimator beside it.
- **Arbiter.** The per-stratum canary (`linear_acc_<stratum>`, `knn_acc_<stratum>`), logged at every step and in each
    branch's pre/post record.
- **Is it label-free?** The strata are time-of-day metadata. A deployment knows that from capture timestamps, so no
    human labels enter.
- **Pass bar.**
    - Fires on the past strata, immediately after the damage, on every canary-called MAE seed (σ0.75, σ1.0, re-init 12;
        IID and walk; 18 seeds).
    - Quiet on re-init 4 (6/6).
    - Quiet on at least 4 of 5 healthy MAE walks, read against the IID null on the same stratum, at the night-block
        checkpoints (65, 108, 109, 110, 114, 118, final).
    - **Better than pooled CKA drift** on the same leg-checkpoint grid. Pooled fires on 3 of 5 walks on the dense grid;
        re-count it on the leg grid for a fair comparison.

### 2. Walk-aware null

- **Read.** Calibrate pooled `cka_drift` on healthy *walk* drives instead of IID ones. Use P1.1.1's reading rule
    ([`reading_protocol.py`](../../../cafl4ds/reading_protocol.py)) on the dense corpus. Then read the walk damage
    branches against those bands.
- **The narrowing assumption.** The monitor is calibrated on a few healthy drives of the kind of stream it will be
    deployed on. A deployment does not know its diet in advance, so this assumption must be stated wherever the result
    is used.
- **Preliminary** (no runs): calibrate on the 5 P1.1.0 MAE walk seeds, leave-one-out. Thin, but enough to see whether
    the margin survives the wider bands.
- **Pass bar.** Leave-one-out false alarms on walks ≤ 1/5. Canary-called walk damage fires on all 9 walk seeds (σ0.75,
    σ1.0, re-init 12), immediately. Re-init 4 is quiet on all 3 walk seeds.
- **If the preliminary passes,** run 5 fresh MAE walk seeds (seeds 5–9, ~2 card-hours) with `checkpoint_legs=true`, so
    they also feed item 1. Then calibrate on 10 walks and check out of sample.

### 3. The optimizer-restart jolt

- **Read.** Each dose-0 twin restarts AdamW at step 65 and nothing else. Compare it with the original P1.1.0 drive of
    the same seed and order, step by step, in units of the null spread. Measure how big the jolt is and how many steps
    it lasts.
- **No pass bar** — this is descriptive. It sizes a confound for P1.3's comparisons, and for any federated design that
    resets optimizer state each round (P1.6).

### 4. A natural degradation: JE's lr-1e-4 walk

- **Why.** It is the only natural, label-confirmed degradation on BDD: the lr-1e-4 walk loses 6–8 canary points, twice
    the IID control's loss ([P1.1.1](P1.1.1.md#a-higher-learning-rate-turns-the-walk-harmful)). It is JE, not MAE, but
    it is the cheapest test of "natural information loss vs CKA drift" on our substrate.
- **The confound.** JE's walk compresses benignly at lr 3e-5 too, and that compression raises drift. So the comparison
    is a difference of differences:
    - (walk − IID) at lr 1e-4: `dial/je_walk_alpha0.0_lr1e-4_s012` − `dial/je_iid_alpha0.0_lr1e-4_s012`;
    - (walk − IID) at lr 3e-5: `branch/je_walk_none` − `branch/je_iid_none`.
    - All four branch from step 65 with a restarted optimizer, so the restart cancels.
- **Read.** Pooled backbone `cka_drift` only. These runs saved no checkpoints, so a per-stratum read would need re-runs
    with `checkpoint_legs=true`.
- **Pass bar.** The lr-1e-4 gap exceeds the lr-3e-5 gap at the end on 3 of 3 seeds, by more than the twin-effect
    threshold.

### 5. Phase 0's natural MAE forgetting, re-read per block

- **Why.** It is the one seed-stable natural MAE forgetting event we have: [P0.3.9](../phase0/P0.3.9.md)'s operating
    point (deep decoder-first well, `epochs_b=40`). There the forgetting arm (PC) loses 6.5–8.5 points of task-A probe
    accuracy while the healthy replay arm holds (|BWT| ≤ 0.028), on 5 of 5 seeds. Its pooled, plain CKA drift on task-A
    images barely separated the two (above). Item 5 asks whether the information loss shows anywhere CKA-like lenses can
    see it.
- **Engineering.** [`scripts/positive_control_forgetting.py`](../../../scripts/positive_control_forgetting.py) saves no
    model states. Add an opt-in `save_states` that writes the full method (`warmup.save_well`) after phase A and after
    phase B, for both arms, plus the task-A support/query images (or confirm they rebuild bit-identically from the
    seed). Check that both arms share the post-A state, which is the drift anchor. Then reuse
    [`block_features.py`](../../../cafl4ds/block_features.py) and [`lenses.py`](../../../cafl4ds/lenses.py) on the
    task-A images.
- **Runs.** P0.3.9's `epochs_b=40` command (its "How to run", with `decoder_warmup_epochs=30 epochs_a=60 epochs_b=40`),
    seeds 0–4, with `save_states=true`. ~20–30 min per seed; ~4 states × 330 MB per seed. The run must reproduce the
    promoted BWT values in `artifacts/P0.3.9/d1redo_epochsb40/` before its states are read.
- **Read.** Per layer (12 blocks + backbone), on task-A support + query (1,200 rows): plain and debiased CKA drift,
    Procrustes, RankMe and TwoNN, from post-A to post-B, PC vs healthy, paired by seed.
- **Pass bar.** Some lens separates PC from healthy at some layer: an exact sign-flip test over the 5 paired seeds, with
    the maximum over layers taken inside each flip (so the layer search is paid for), at p ≤ 1/32 — that is, 5/5 seeds
    in the forgetting direction at the layer the test picks. Report the plain backbone read beside it, to reproduce
    Phase 0's faint result.
- **One hypothesis item 5 can help settle: little was durably lost.** The probe is refit on the frozen features, so some
    information really was lost from the representation. But the loss was small and quickly re-learned. CKA drift's
    faintness may then be partly *right*: the representation's content barely changed. If so, deeper, permanent
    forgetting — P1.3's target — may move the content more. Relate each seed's drift to its crater depth, and read
    whether the change concentrates in low-variance directions (Procrustes moves where CKA does not).
- **What each outcome means.** A pass would locate natural forgetting in the network and give P1.3 a sharper read. A
    fail would mean CKA-like lenses do not see *shallow, recoverable* forgetting. With the hypothesis above, that leaves
    open whether they see permanent forgetting — and, without it, that CKA drift's pass in P1.1.2 may be specific to
    damage that rewrites the representation's content: a gross-damage detector, not a forgetting reader. That is the
    P0.5.3 lesson in a milder form, and P1.3 must know which.

### 6. Follow-up: a middle-block single-drive alarm

Added after item 5, with the user's approval; no new runs. Item 5 found natural forgetting in blocks 4–11, read as a
comparison against a matched twin. This asks whether those blocks also work as a *single-drive* alarm — one trajectory
read against healthy bands, with no twin.

- **The panel.** Plain `cka_drift` at blocks 4–11, one threshold shared by the 8 layers. The blocks were chosen on
    P0.3.9's data, not BDD's, so the BDD read pays no selection cost. The comparator is the pooled backbone read alone,
    on the same grid.
- **On BDD.** Calibrated on the 5 IID drives at their 18 leg checkpoints (span 3, floor 0.25, α = 0.05), as P1.1.2's
    per-block panels were. Each state is read against those bands alone, never against its twin: the healthy walks at
    every checkpoint, each damage branch's post-damage state at step 65 and its final state, and the dose-0 twins' final
    states.
- **On P0.3.9.** The 5 healthy (replay) arms' post-B drift is the null, one reading per seed; each PC arm is read
    against it. Caveat: the healthy arm is replay training on both datasets, not a deployment null.
- **Pass bars.**
    - BDD: leave-one-out IID false alarms ≤ 1/5; quiet on ≥ 4/5 healthy walks; immediate fire on all 18 called seeds;
        quiet on re-init 4 (6/6); quiet on the 6 dose-0 final states.
    - P0.3.9: leave-one-out false alarms on the healthy arms ≤ 1/5; fires on ≥ 4/5 PC arms.
    - Report the pooled backbone panel beside each.

## Findings

### 1. Past-regime drift is no better than pooled drift

Each read is calibrated on the 5 IID drives at the 7 night-block checkpoints, where both earlier strata are past. Damage
is read immediately (the rebuilt post-damage state against step 65); the healthy walks at the same 7 checkpoints. Margin
is the called damage's *z* over the worst healthy walk's *z* (median / minimum).

| Read (threshold) | Fires on called (18) | Quiet on re-init 4 (max *z*) | Quiet walks (worst *z*) | Margin |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| Past strata, debiased (5.30) | 18/18 | 6/6 (4.90) | 4/5 (6.24) | 2.18 / 1.81 |
| Past strata, plain (5.24) | 18/18 | 6/6 (4.91) | 4/5 (6.27) | 2.16 / 1.80 |
| Current stratum (night), debiased (5.65) | 18/18 | 6/6 (3.38) | 4/5 (7.79) | 1.61 / 1.04 |
| **Pooled**, plain (5.93) | 18/18 | 6/6 (3.36) | **5/5** (4.62) | **2.39** / 1.61 |

- **The past-strata read clears three of its four bars,** but not the fourth. On the same grid the pooled read is just
    as sensitive, quieter on the walks, and has the larger median margin. So the bar "better than pooled" fails.
- **Its one walk fire is a false alarm.** Walk seed 1 fires on the past strata (*z* 6.24). Over the night block its
    daytime and dawn/dusk canary sit at −1.3 and +2.5 points against the IID mean: nothing was lost there.
- **It also sits closer to the lever.** Re-init 4 reaches 0.92 of the past-strata threshold, against 0.57 of pooled's.
- **Pooled's 5/5 is partly a grid effect.** On the dense grid pooled fires on 3 of 5 walks, at steps 52–53, 56–57, 63,
    68 and 117. None falls on a night-block leg checkpoint, and the per-stratum read can only be made at saved
    checkpoints. So the comparison is fair, but both reads would fire more on a dense grid.
- **Debiasing changes nothing at 270 rows.** Plain and debiased per-stratum drift differ by a median of 0.00001. The
    larger gaps (up to 0.09) sit only on heavily damaged states, where drift is 0.35–0.64 and both reads fire anyway.

**A read that does not heal.** At the end of the drive, the past-strata read still fires on all 18 called seeds. Pooled
drift fires on only 3 and the night stratum on 5: they partly recover, while the canary dent persists (P1.1.2). This
holds on IID branches too, so it is about the daytime and dawn/dusk strata, not about being past. The catch is
specificity: the same end read fires on 3 of 6 re-init-4 seeds. Why within-stratum structure stays damaged while the
pooled read recovers is not resolved here.

### 2. A walk-aware null widens the bands more than it helps (preliminary)

Pooled `cka_drift` calibrated on the 5 healthy P1.1.0 walks (P1.1.1's span and floor). Each walk damage branch is read
on its restart-free damage record, against bands refit without its parent seed.

| Bar | Result |
| -- | -- |
| Leave-one-out false alarms on walks ≤ 1/5 | ✅ 0/5 |
| Called walk damage fires on all 9 seeds, immediately | ❌ **4/9** |
| Re-init 4 quiet on all 3 walk seeds | ✅ 3/3 |

- **Why it fails.** The walk null is wide where the branches start. Its seed spread is 1.3–1.75× the IID null's through
    the daytime and dawn/dusk legs (1.6× just before the branch point), and 1.0–1.4× in the night block. The
    leave-one-out thresholds, fit on 4 walks, are 4.2, 5.5 and 6.4, against 3.83 for the 10-seed IID null. Read against
    them, the called damage scores 3.9–6.6, against 6.2–10.2 against the IID null. The weakest called seed reaches 0.61
    of its threshold.
- **More walks would not rescue it.** Ten walks would tighten the threshold, perhaps toward the IID panel's ~3.8. But
    the wider spread is a property of the walk itself, not of the seed count, so the weakest called seeds (3.9–4.7)
    would likely still sit near it. So the preliminary does not pass. On that basis the 5 fresh walks were dropped, by
    decision with the user.
- **The rest of each branch, described.** Over the whole branch, read against the same out-of-seed walk bands, all 9
    called seeds fire within 10 steps (6 at step 65), re-init 4 stays quiet on 3 of 3, and so do the 3 dose-0 twins. The
    walk bands are wide enough to absorb the optimizer-restart jolt. This is not the bar — the trajectory carries the
    restart — but it shows a walk-aware null trades the jolt for a slower damage call.

### 3. The optimizer-restart jolt is large, brief, and MAE-only

Each dose-0 twin minus the original drive of the same seed and order, per step, in units of P1.1.1's 10-seed IID spread
(panel threshold: MAE 3.83, JE 3.34).

| Backbone | Peak (step 65) | Consecutive readings above the threshold | Consecutive readings above 2 SD | Settled \|gap\| (median) |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| MAE, IID twins | 4.3–7.5 | 1 | 1–5 | 0.8–1.4 |
| MAE, walk twins | 6.4–7.0 | 1–2 | 4 | 0.8–1.5 |
| JE, both orders | none (\|gap\| < 1.2 over steps 65–72) | 0 | 0 | 0.3–1.3 |

- **On MAE it is a 1–2-reading transient.** The gap to the original drive exceeds the threshold at step 65 on every
    twin, and at step 66 on one. After that, twin and original differ by a median of about one null SD — the floor of
    two runs of the same seed with different random draws. That floor is not small. On the walk, the gap later crosses
    the threshold in both directions (seed 2 at steps 68 and 91; seed 1 below it at steps 110–117), because the walk's
    drift wanders more than IID's. So two healthy walk runs of one seed can disagree by a full threshold. The restart
    costs the canary nothing (mean gap within ±1.4 points).
- **JE shows no jolt — and the learning rate is not why.** The natural suspect was the step size: AdamW's first step
    after a reset moves every weight by about one learning rate, and MAE trains at 1e-4 against JE's 3e-5. The JE dial
    runs give a free check, since they also restart AdamW at step 65, at lr 1e-4. Their first step multiplies JE's drift
    by 0.7–2.1× (gap to the original drive ≤ 1.1 SD over steps 65–72). MAE's first step multiplies it by 8–32×.
- **The jolt is large because MAE barely moves.** A healthy MAE drive sits ~0.003 from the well, against JE's
    0.004–0.07. So one full-size step is a big change *relative to* MAE's healthy drift, and the log-scale read
    registers it as such. The upshot: the jolt is a property of the backbone's healthy drift, not of the learning rate
    alone. This check is confounded with the lr change itself, so it does not settle how the jolt scales with lr on MAE.
    A clean test would be an MAE twin at lr 3e-5 (~1 card-hour). It is not run: it matters only if P1.3 restarts arms at
    different learning rates.
- **Why this matters.** A restart is a known event: a deployment that resets optimizer state (a federated round, for
    instance) knows when it did. Masking the 2 readings after it would remove the jolt. A comparison between resumed
    arms (P1.3's legs) cancels it outright.
- **The comparison is not quite "restart only".** A resumed twin sees the same batches, but not the same random masks
    and augmentations. That is why the settled gap is about one SD, not zero.

### 4. JE's lr-1e-4 walk does not move drift beyond the learning rate's own effect

(walk − IID) at lr 1e-4 minus (walk − IID) at lr 3e-5, on the protocol's log scale, in units of the JE IID null's spread
at the drive's end (step 117). All four runs branch from the same step-65 states with a fresh optimizer, and differ only
in the learning rate.

| Seed | Gap of gaps, end (peak during drive) | Fires (> 3.34) | Canary's own gap of gaps (points) |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| 0 | −0.50 (0.60) | no | −1.2 |
| 1 | −1.63 (0.06) | no | **−7.9** |
| 2 | −0.89 (1.41) | no | −0.7 |

- **The bar fails: 0 of 3.** The higher learning rate raises drift on both orders (end drift: walk 0.12–0.16 against
    0.06–0.12; IID 0.02–0.05 against 0.006–0.03). In proportion it raises IID's more. So the walk's *extra* drift does
    not grow, and on the log scale it shrinks.
- **The arbiter is weaker than the design assumed.** P1.1.1's seed-mean result holds: against the lr-3e-5 branches, the
    lr-1e-4 walk loses 6.4 points and IID 3.0. But per seed, the walk-specific loss is clear only on seed 1. On the
    other two, lr 1e-4 costs IID nearly as much as the walk.
- **Where the canary does see it, drift does not.** Seed 1 carries a 7.9-point walk-specific loss, and its drift gap of
    gaps is the most negative of the three. So on our one natural, label-confirmed BDD degradation, CKA drift does not
    separate information loss from the learning rate's benign movement. The caveats: it is JE, read at the backbone, on
    3 seeds.

### 5. Natural forgetting shows in the middle blocks — not at the output Phase 0 read

**The re-run reproduces P0.3.9 exactly.** On all 5 seeds, both arms' BWT equals the promoted value to the last digit,
with the same gate verdicts (PC −0.065 to −0.085, healthy −0.012 to +0.028). Both arms' post-A states are bit-identical,
so each seed's two arms share one drift anchor. Read on the CPU, Phase 0's pooled plain drift on the task-A query
matches the recorded value to ±0.00002.

**The pass bar is met.** One exact sign-flip test, maximum paired *t* over 4 lenses × 13 layers: *p* = 1/32, the
smallest 5 seeds allow, at `procrustes_drift@block08` (*t* 35). Each lens alone also reaches 1/32. Per layer, the change
from post-A to post-B (mean over seeds; "5/5" means PC above healthy on every seed):

| Layer | CKA drift, PC vs healthy | Seeds | Procrustes, PC vs healthy | RankMe change, PC vs healthy |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| blocks 1–3 | 0.007–0.010 vs 0.009–0.012 | 1–3/5 | 0.019–0.033 vs 0.021–0.030 | −8.7…+1.5 vs +5.1…+8.2 |
| blocks 4–11 | **0.013–0.043 vs 0.008–0.029** | **5/5** | 0.037–0.109 vs 0.030–0.094 | −13.3…−4.8 vs +10.0…+30.4 |
| block 12 | 0.050 vs 0.047 | 3/5 | 0.116 vs 0.111 | −4.9 vs +37.2 |
| backbone (Phase 0's read) | 0.0071 vs 0.0068 | 4/5 | 0.018 vs 0.017 | −9.3 vs +16.1 |

- **The forgetting arm drifts 1.4–1.8× more in blocks 4–11 (mean over seeds), on every seed.** Per seed the ratio runs
    from 1.2× to 2.8×, and the smallest PC-minus-healthy gap is 0.002 (block 4); from block 7 up it is at least 0.006.
    This is natural information loss, made visible to CKA drift.
- **Phase 0's faint result is about where it read, not how.** At the final normed output, PC and healthy are
    indistinguishable (0.0071 vs 0.0068), as Phase 0 found. Block 12 does not separate either: both arms move it the
    most (~0.05), so its shared movement swamps the difference. The final norm then compresses that movement ~7×.
- **The healthy arm took twice the steps.** Its phase B replays A alongside B, so it takes 44 batches per epoch against
    PC's 22. Yet it moves the middle blocks less on task-A images. So PC's extra drift is not "more training".
- **The debiased estimator changes nothing here.** It agrees with the plain one to within 0.0005 at every layer (1,200
    rows). The bias Phase 0's 600-row read might have carried was not what hid the signal.
- **The change is not confined to low-variance directions.** In blocks 4–11 the PC/healthy ratio is larger for CKA
    (1.4–1.8×) than for Procrustes (1.15–1.3×). CKA weights directions by variance, so the change sits in the
    high-variance ones — as for surgical damage (P1.1.2).
- **RankMe also separates, but read it with care.** PC's rank on task-A images falls in blocks 2–12 and at the output.
    Healthy's rises, presumably from its more diverse two-dataset diet. P1.1.2 found RankMe insensitive to surgical
    damage, so this is an observation about this event, not a reader.
- **TwoNN, reported only.** It separates 5/5 at blocks 8–9 (PC's dimension holds, healthy's falls) and nowhere else in a
    consistent direction.

**The "little was durably lost" hypothesis stays open.** Per-seed drift does not relate to crater depth in any
consistent way: Spearman ρ against PC BWT ranges from −0.97 to +0.87 across the separating layers. With 5 seeds and BWT
spanning only −0.065 to −0.085, a dose–response read has no range to work with. So item 5 shows that shallow,
recoverable forgetting is visible. It does not say whether deeper forgetting would move the middle blocks more.

### 6. Follow-up: the middle blocks also work without a twin

The pass bars were fixed before reading ([design](#6-follow-up-a-middle-block-single-drive-alarm)). The panel is plain

**On BDD, both panels pass every bar; the middle blocks hold a wider margin and do not heal.** Each state is read alone

- **Its walk fire is early and brief.** Walk seed 4 crosses at step 31 (blocks 8–10) and at steps 64–65 (block 6), *z*

## Answer to the central question

**Read at the output, CKA drift cannot tell information loss from benign adaptation as a single-drive alarm.** Each of
the three BDD attempts falls short. Reading only the strata left behind is no better than the pooled read (item 1). A
walk-aware null widens the bands until real damage reads under them (item 2). And JE's natural, lr-driven loss moves
drift no more than the learning rate's benign movement does (item 4). Only the optimizer-restart confound turns out
manageable: on MAE it lasts 1–2 readings and is known in advance (item 3).

**But the output is the wrong place to look for natural forgetting.** Item 5 locates it in the middle blocks (4–11). The
top block and the final norm swamp it at the output, which is why Phase 0 saw a faint signal. Per-block CKA drift
separates a natural forgetting arm from its healthy twin on 5 of 5 seeds. Read without the twin, it still catches 4 of 5
(follow-up). And the same blocks are quiet enough on BDD's healthy walks to pass the damage screen as a single-drive
panel (4 of 5 walks quiet), with a wider margin than the output.

**The insight, in one line: the fixes to *calibration* failed; the fix to *where* succeeded.** Items 1, 2 and 4 each
tried to make the output read smarter — better rows, a better null, a natural target — and each fell short. Item 5 and
its follow-up changed the layer, and the signal appeared. The other items still matter. They close off three plausible
routes, so P1.3 does not spend effort there. They turn the restart from an unexplained confound into a known, maskable
artefact. And they show that JE's lr-1e-4 "natural degradation" rests on one seed, which P1.4 inherits.

**What CKA drift rests on, wherever it is used.**

- *A certified anchor.* It reads change from a state known to be good, never quality itself.
- *A comparison first.* Item 5's strongest pass is arm-vs-twin, the form P1.3 reads its legs in. The single-drive
    middle-block panel passes its screens, but has not yet met a natural failure on BDD.
- *Reading the right layers.* For natural forgetting, the middle blocks. The pooled output, which the monitor logs
    today, sees surgical damage but not this.
- *Masking known restarts.* On MAE, the 2 readings after an optimizer reset.

## What this substudy does not do

- **It does not test sensitivity on BDD.** This can't happen in P1.1.3, because no natural MAE failure exists on BDD
    yet. Producing one is P1.3's research question, so P1.1.3 shouldn't absorb it. Phase 0's negative is a prior from
    shallow, recoverable forgetting after a dataset switch, not a verdict. Item 5 tests the mechanism on that failure;
    certification on the deployment substrate stays with P1.3.
- It does not revisit the other readers. P1.1.2 closed them.
- It does not touch JE collapse; that is P1.4's.

## Handover to P1.3, P1.4 and P1.6

- **P1.3 — read CKA drift per block, in the middle blocks, and validate it there.** Item 5 makes this the main
    hand-over. On P0.3.9's natural forgetting the signal lives in blocks 4–11. The pooled output the monitor logs today
    cannot see it. So P1.3's runs need per-block features: saved leg checkpoints (`checkpoint_legs=true`, then
    `scripts/extract_block_features.py`) or an inline per-block lens in the monitor. The engineering P1.1.2 dropped
    returns here. Read it comparatively (arm vs matched control, as item 5 did), with the debiased estimator beside the
    plain one. **It must still be validated on P1.3's first natural MAE degradation on BDD.** Item 5's forgetting was
    shallow, recoverable and on a dataset switch, so it shows the lens *can* see natural loss, not that it will see
    BDD's.
- **P1.3 — the single-drive alarm.** Not the past-strata read or a walk-aware null on the pooled output: both failed
    here. The candidate is the middle-block panel (blocks 4–11), calibrated on IID drives. It passes the BDD damage
    screen with a wider margin than the output and, unlike it, does not heal by the drive's end. It catches 4 of 5
    natural forgetting arms on P0.3.9 without their twin. Its sensitivity to a natural loss on BDD is the untested part.
- **P1.3 / P1.6 — the optimizer restart.** On MAE, mask the 2 readings after any optimizer reset (a federated round, a
    resumed branch). Comparisons between arms resumed the same way cancel it. One open point, deliberately skipped: how
    the jolt scales with the learning rate on MAE. If P1.3 ever restarts arms at *different* learning rates, the
    cancellation no longer holds; run the clean test first (an MAE dose-0 twin at lr 3e-5 against the lr-1e-4 one, ~1
    card-hour).
- **P1.4 — JE's lr-1e-4 walk.** Its walk-specific canary loss is clear on 1 of 3 seeds only; the seed mean hides that.
    Pooled backbone drift does not track it. Any JE reader P1.4 tests on this event should be read per seed.
