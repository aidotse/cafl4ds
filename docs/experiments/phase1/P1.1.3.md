# CKA drift deep dive — information loss vs benign adaptation `[analysis + new runs]` (P1.1.3)

**What this substudy is.** The fourth P1.1 sub-study (see the [Phase 1 overview](index.md)). [P1.1.2](P1.1.2.md)
screened every label-free signal against MAE damage of known size. One survived: **CKA drift against the well**, the
existing `cka_drift`. It tracks the damage and stays quiet on a control that moves the weights at no cost to the canary.
Every Phase-0 MAE candidate failed again. P1.1.3 asks whether that survivor can become a usable MAE reader, and under
what assumptions, before P1.3 relies on it.

## Motivation

**What CKA drift reads.** It measures how far the representation's content has moved from the well. It cannot say
whether a representation is good. It can only say the representation has left a state we certified as good. That
narrowing assumption — a competent, certified anchor — is what lets it pass the damage screen.

**The catch is that two kinds of movement look alike to it.** Both damage and healthy adaptation move the model away
from the well. P1.1.2 found two symptoms of this.

- **It fires on benign movement.** On the healthy MAE walk it fires on 3 of 5 seeds. It also fires on every optimizer
    restart. Its margin over that movement is only about 2× at the doses the canary calls.
- **It was faint on natural forgetting.** Phase 0 read it on the same pretrained ViT-B MAE, on the old task's images,
    anchored at the end of phase A. At [P0.3.9](../phase0/P0.3.9.md)'s seed-stable 5/5 fire, forgetting cost the probe
    6.5–8.5 points, yet CKA drift moved only ~0.005–0.010. The forgetting arm drifted more than the healthy replay arm
    on 4 of 5 seeds, by at most 0.002. Surgical damage moves it far more per point of canary lost: noise σ0.5, a
    ~4-point dent, moves the BDD backbone read by ~0.04. Different data and probes, so this is rough — but it is about
    an order of magnitude.

**What this means for the idea of reading drift where forgetting lives.** The natural fix for the walk problem is to
read drift only on the regimes the walk has left behind: healthy adaptation should move the regime being streamed, and
information loss should show on the regimes left behind. Phase 0 already read drift that way — on the old task's images
only — and it was still faint. So the prior for this idea is unfavourable. It remains worth testing on BDD, because the
substrate and the kind of shift differ (a correlated walk of driving regimes, not a switch between datasets). But a
negative there would agree with Phase 0.

**Phase 0 is a prior here, not a verdict.** Its findings about the instrument carry over: same ViT-B, same estimator.
Its findings about whether a failure occurs, and how large, do not: they rest on Phase 0's diet and horizon, which is
what Phase 1 exists to replace. P0.3.9 also tested only half of what a reader needs. It probed *sensitivity* to natural
forgetting — of one kind: an abrupt dataset switch at a toy horizon, shallow and almost fully recoverable (savings ≈
1.0). It never probed *specificity* to healthy adaptation on a correlated stream; its healthy arm was replay training on
both tasks. P1.1.3 tests specificity on the BDD walk (items 1–2) and the mechanism on P0.3.9's failure (item 5).

The deeper question is item 5's. Is natural information loss simply invisible to the pooled output's CKA drift, or does
it show somewhere else — in some block, or under the debiased estimator? Phase 0 read the pooled output only, with the
plain estimator, on 600 images against 768 features, where the plain estimator is biased toward "no change".

**The central question.** Does CKA drift tell information loss from benign adaptation? Whether it sees damage is already
settled.

## Design

Five items, cheapest first. Items 1–4 need no new drives at first; item 5 re-runs a Phase-0 operating point. Each pass
bar is fixed here, before any reading.

| # | Item | Origin | Data | New runs |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| 1 | Past-regime CKA drift | the tangent | P1.1.2 features | none |
| 2 | Walk-aware null | option (a) | P1.1.0 walk corpus, P1.1.1 walk branches | 5 fresh MAE walks, if item 2's preliminary passes |
| 3 | The optimizer-restart jolt | P1.1.2 finding | P1.1.1 dose-0 twins | none |
| 4 | A natural degradation: JE's lr-1e-4 walk | P1.1.1 finding | P1.1.1 dial and branch corpora | none |
| 5 | Phase 0's natural MAE forgetting, re-read per block | Phase 0 | P0.3.9's operating point | 5 seeds × 2 arms, ~1.5 h on both cards (approved) |

**Option (b) is a decision, not an item.** CKA drift already works as a *comparative* reader: one arm against a matched
control. That is how P1.3 reads its legs (b) and (c), so P1.3 can use it as it stands. P1.1.3 asks whether it can also
be a single-drive alarm, which only the deployed monitor (Phase 3) needs.

### 1. Past-regime CKA drift

- **Read.** Per time-of-day stratum (daytime, dawn/dusk, night), CKA drift against the well on that stratum's probe
    images only. A stratum is **past** at a checkpoint if the walk streamed it earlier and has moved on. It is
    **current** if the walk is streaming it, and **future** if not yet reached. The walk's schedule: daytime legs to
    step 55, dawn/dusk 56–63, night 65–118. So daytime is past from step 56, and both daytime and dawn/dusk are past
    from step 65 on.
- **Estimator.** Debiased linear CKA (`measurements.debiased_linear_cka`). Each stratum has 135 query + 135 support
    images, 270 rows against 768 features, where the plain estimator's upward bias is no longer negligible. Report the
    plain estimator beside it.
- **Arbiter.** The per-stratum canary (`linear_acc_<stratum>`, `knn_acc_<stratum>`), logged at every step and in each
    branch's pre/post record.
- **Is it label-free?** The strata are time-of-day metadata. A deployment knows that from capture timestamps, so no
    human labels enter.
- **Pass bar.**
    - Fires on the past strata, immediately after the damage, on every canary-called MAE seed (σ0.75, σ1.0, re-init 12;
        IID and walk; 18 seeds).
    - Quiet on re-init 4 (6/6).
    - Quiet on at least 4 of 5 healthy MAE walks, read against the IID null on the same stratum, at the night-block
        checkpoints (65, 108, 109, 110, 114, 118, final).
    - **Better than pooled CKA drift** on the same leg-checkpoint grid. Pooled fires on 3 of 5 walks on the dense grid;
        re-count it on the leg grid for a fair comparison.

### 2. Walk-aware null

- **Read.** Calibrate pooled `cka_drift` on healthy *walk* drives instead of IID ones. Use P1.1.1's reading rule
    ([`reading_protocol.py`](../../../cafl4ds/reading_protocol.py)) on the dense corpus. Then read the walk damage
    branches against those bands.
- **The narrowing assumption.** The monitor is calibrated on a few healthy drives of the kind of stream it will be
    deployed on. A deployment does not know its diet in advance, so this assumption must be stated wherever the result
    is used.
- **Preliminary** (no runs): calibrate on the 5 P1.1.0 MAE walk seeds, leave-one-out. Thin, but enough to see whether
    the margin survives the wider bands.
- **Pass bar.** Leave-one-out false alarms on walks ≤ 1/5. Canary-called walk damage fires on all 9 walk seeds (σ0.75,
    σ1.0, re-init 12), immediately. Re-init 4 is quiet on all 3 walk seeds.
- **If the preliminary passes,** run 5 fresh MAE walk seeds (seeds 5–9, ~2 card-hours) with `checkpoint_legs=true`, so
    they also feed item 1. Then calibrate on 10 walks and check out of sample.

### 3. The optimizer-restart jolt

- **Read.** Each dose-0 twin restarts AdamW at step 65 and nothing else. Compare it with the original P1.1.0 drive of
    the same seed and order, step by step, in units of the null spread. Measure how big the jolt is and how many steps
    it lasts.
- **No pass bar** — this is descriptive. It sizes a confound for P1.3's comparisons, and for any federated design that
    resets optimizer state each round (P1.6).

### 4. A natural degradation: JE's lr-1e-4 walk

- **Why.** It is the only natural, label-confirmed degradation on BDD: the lr-1e-4 walk loses 6–8 canary points, twice
    the IID control's loss ([P1.1.1](P1.1.1.md#a-higher-learning-rate-turns-the-walk-harmful)). It is JE, not MAE, but
    it is the cheapest test of "natural information loss vs CKA drift" on our substrate.
- **The confound.** JE's walk compresses benignly at lr 3e-5 too, and that compression raises drift. So the comparison
    is a difference of differences:
    - (walk − IID) at lr 1e-4: `dial/je_walk_alpha0.0_lr1e-4_s012` − `dial/je_iid_alpha0.0_lr1e-4_s012`;
    - (walk − IID) at lr 3e-5: `branch/je_walk_none` − `branch/je_iid_none`.
    - All four branch from step 65 with a restarted optimizer, so the restart cancels.
- **Read.** Pooled backbone `cka_drift` only. These runs saved no checkpoints, so a per-stratum read would need re-runs
    with `checkpoint_legs=true`.
- **Pass bar.** The lr-1e-4 gap exceeds the lr-3e-5 gap at the end on 3 of 3 seeds, by more than the twin-effect
    threshold.

### 5. Phase 0's natural MAE forgetting, re-read per block

- **Why.** It is the one seed-stable natural MAE forgetting event we have: [P0.3.9](../phase0/P0.3.9.md)'s operating
    point (deep decoder-first well, `epochs_b=40`). There the forgetting arm (PC) loses 6.5–8.5 points of task-A probe
    accuracy while the healthy replay arm holds (|BWT| ≤ 0.028), on 5 of 5 seeds. Its pooled, plain CKA drift on task-A
    images barely separated the two (above). Item 5 asks whether the information loss shows anywhere CKA-like lenses can
    see it.
- **Engineering.** [`scripts/positive_control_forgetting.py`](../../../scripts/positive_control_forgetting.py) saves no
    model states. Add an opt-in `save_states` that writes the full method (`warmup.save_well`) after phase A and after
    phase B, for both arms, plus the task-A support/query images (or confirm they rebuild bit-identically from the
    seed). Check that both arms share the post-A state, which is the drift anchor. Then reuse
    [`block_features.py`](../../../cafl4ds/block_features.py) and [`lenses.py`](../../../cafl4ds/lenses.py) on the
    task-A images.
- **Runs.** P0.3.9's `epochs_b=40` command (its "How to run", with `decoder_warmup_epochs=30 epochs_a=60 epochs_b=40`),
    seeds 0–4, with `save_states=true`. ~20–30 min per seed; ~4 states × 330 MB per seed. The run must reproduce the
    promoted BWT values in `artifacts/P0.3.9/d1redo_epochsb40/` before its states are read.
- **Read.** Per layer (12 blocks + backbone), on task-A support + query (1,200 rows): plain and debiased CKA drift,
    Procrustes, RankMe and TwoNN, from post-A to post-B, PC vs healthy, paired by seed.
- **Pass bar.** Some lens separates PC from healthy at some layer: an exact sign-flip test over the 5 paired seeds, with
    the maximum over layers taken inside each flip (so the layer search is paid for), at p ≤ 1/32 — that is, 5/5 seeds
    in the forgetting direction at the layer the test picks. Report the plain backbone read beside it, to reproduce
    Phase 0's faint result.
- **One hypothesis item 5 can help settle: little was durably lost.** The probe is refit on the frozen features, so some
    information really was lost from the representation. But the loss was small and quickly re-learned. CKA drift's
    faintness may then be partly *right*: the representation's content barely changed. If so, deeper, permanent
    forgetting — P1.3's target — may move the content more. Relate each seed's drift to its crater depth, and read
    whether the change concentrates in low-variance directions (Procrustes moves where CKA does not).
- **What each outcome means.** A pass would locate natural forgetting in the network and give P1.3 a sharper read. A
    fail would mean CKA-like lenses do not see *shallow, recoverable* forgetting. With the hypothesis above, that leaves
    open whether they see permanent forgetting — and, without it, that CKA drift's pass in P1.1.2 may be specific to
    damage that rewrites the representation's content: a gross-damage detector, not a forgetting reader. That is the
    P0.5.3 lesson in a milder form, and P1.3 must know which.

## What this substudy does not do

- **It does not test sensitivity on BDD.** This can't happen in P1.1.3, because no natural MAE failure exists on BDD
    yet. Producing one is P1.3's research question, so P1.1.3 shouldn't absorb it. Phase 0's negative is a prior from
    shallow, recoverable forgetting after a dataset switch, not a verdict. Item 5 tests the mechanism on that failure;
    certification on the deployment substrate stays with P1.3.
- It does not revisit the other readers. P1.1.2 closed them.
- It does not touch JE collapse; that is P1.4's.
