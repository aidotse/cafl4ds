# F2 — Non-IID label skew on a warm-started MAE

The first FL sub-study to ask a *scientific* question rather than validate the harness (see the
[federated track overview](index.md)). [F1](F1.md) established that FedAvg reproduces the centralized reference under an
IID partition; F2 introduces label-skewed heterogeneity (`partition.scheme=dirichlet`) and asks how much it moves global
representation health, with no selection filter in play.

The motivating asymmetry: the classic non-IID penalty in *supervised* federated learning is largely a classifier-head
effect — each client's head is pulled toward its own label distribution, and averaging heads that disagree about the
classes produces a bad global head. MAE has no labels and no head of that kind. A `Dirichlet(α)` partition over class
ids does not change *what* any client optimizes; it changes only the image statistics each client sees while optimizing
it. So the expected damage mechanism is representation divergence plus ordinary client drift, and the open question is
whether that is large enough to read at all.

## Why MAE only, and why warm-started

**MAE only.** SimSiam does not survive this configuration. At the scaled ViT-Tiny used here (see below) an IID 40-epoch
centralized run point-collapses — loss pinned near −0.9 (the trivial optimum is −1), RankMe 1.11–1.29 against
[P0.2.1](../phase0/P0.2.1.md)'s ~1.9 BatchNorm floor and ~5.8 healthy reference, and `knn_acc` *below* frozen random
init. Two deviations from P0.2.1's certified healthy recipe are implicated: no warmup+cosine LR (`run_loop.py` never
wires a scheduler) and a ~15× larger encoder, against P0.2.1's explicit finding that the fix is a fair training regime
and **not** a bigger model. Establishing a healthy SimSiam at this scale is its own task; F2 does not wait on it.

**Warm-started.** The federated harness is strictly single-pass — clients advance a persistent stream and drop out when
exhausted ([`orchestrator.py`](../../../cafl4ds/federated/orchestrator.py)), with no `epochs` knob, because single-pass
*is* the project's premise. At this data scale that means ~78 batches per client, against the 12,520 steps that build a
competent encoder. A from-scratch federated run therefore sits on the floor, and **a floor cannot degrade**: a
centralized single-pass from-scratch control reaches `linear_acc` 0.1162 against its own random-init bar of 0.0984, with
`knn_acc` *falling* 0.0558 → 0.0474. So the `I` factor carries the competence: pre-train once offline over an IID
multi-epoch stream, then every federated arm adapts that encoder single-pass. This also matches the deployment story — a
model trained centrally, shipped to edge devices, adapting on transient local streams.

## Setup

**Data (`D`).** CIFAR-100 train split at 32px, all 50,000 images (`max_per_class=null`). The global monitor's pool is
carved out *before* partitioning — 101 images/class, 10,100 total — so no client can train on what the aggregated model
is scored on, leaving 39,900 for clients. 4 clients; per-client reservations stay at 0, since a client's local eval sets
are never read in a federated run.

**Encoder.** [`TinyViTEncoder`](../../../cafl4ds/models/vit.py) scaled to standard ViT-Tiny geometry — `patch_size=4`,
`embed_dim=192`, `depth=12`, `num_heads=3`, `mlp_ratio=4`: 64 patches + CLS, ~5.4M parameters. Patch 4 rather than the
config group's default 8 is load-bearing for MAE: at 32px, patch 8 gives only 16 patches, so masking 75% leaves 4
visible tokens and the reconstruction task degenerates.

**Method (`C`).** MAE with `decoder_dim=192`, `decoder_depth=4`, `decoder_heads=3`, `decoder_mlp_ratio=4` at the default
`mask_ratio=0.75` and `norm_pix_loss=true`. The repo default 64-dim/2-block decoder is too weak to drive an encoder of
this width.

**Warm start (`I`).** `scripts/pretrain.py` over the IID stream, batch 128, AdamW at `lr=5e-4`, probe reservations
40/50/10 per class. Two bases were produced: **40 epochs** (12,520 steps) and **5 epochs**, to test whether a null
result is genuine or merely headroom-limited.

**Federation.** FedAvg. `steps_per_round` ∈ {1, 78} brackets the drift axis rather than guessing a middle value: at 1
the server averages after every batch, which with an IID partition is arithmetically close to centralized SGD at batch
512 and so acts as the null; at 78 each client trains its entire shard alone before a single average, the maximum-drift
extreme. Because the stream is consumed once, rounds are not free — they are shard size ÷ `steps_per_round`, so the two
settings give 78 rounds and 1 round respectively (84 and 2 under Dirichlet, whose largest shard is bigger).

## How sharp is α=0.1, measured

Worth pinning down, since label skew over *many* classes averages out more than the α value suggests. Measured directly
on [`dirichlet_partition`](../../../cafl4ds/federated/partition.py) with 100 classes and 4 clients (post-holdout, 399
images/class):

| α | classes absent per client | classes with \<10 images | shard sizes |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| 0.5 | 0–7 | 16–22 | 8.5k–11.0k |
| **0.1** | **2–42** | **52–56** | **9.2k–10.7k** |
| 0.01 | 44–78 | 64–78 | 7.0k–13.2k |

At α=0.1 a client is typically missing ~40 of the 100 classes outright, holds fewer than 10 images for another ~54, and
dominates (>50% of) about 25 — genuinely the "highly non-IID" setting of the FL literature, not a weak knob. Totals stay
balanced, so clients exhaust at similar rounds. **α=0.01 is not a clean sharpening**: its shard totals range 7.0k–13.2k,
so clients drop out at very different rounds and participation asymmetry rides along with the skew.

## The head warm-up confound, and its fix

The first round of F2 runs was confounded, and the diagnosis is the reusable part.

`save_encoder_checkpoint` stored the backbone alone, so every warm-started run rebuilt the MAE decoder randomly. The
first steps then backpropagated through an untrained decoder and *degraded* the restored encoder before recovering — the
same warm-up confound [audit P0.3](../audits/P0.3.md) caught, reappearing in the federated setting. On the 5-epoch base,
IID, 1 step/round:

| round | `loss` | `rankme` | `linear_acc` |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| 0 (warm start) | 1.1948 | 27.6 | 0.1974 |
| 1 | **1.3538** | 21.9 | 0.1866 |
| 5 | 0.8514 | 21.1 | **0.1636** |
| 22 | 0.7061 | **18.7** | 0.1820 |
| 77 | 0.6095 | 28.4 | 0.1902 |

Round 1's loss *rises* above the warm start, which identifies the cause: a random decoder's gradients are actively
harmful. `linear_acc` falls 17% relative and RankMe by a third before both return to roughly their starting values —
`linear_acc` in fact finishing slightly *below* where it began. The transient consumed roughly the first 50 of 78
rounds, so the dominant signal in every arm was decoder warm-up rather than the partition. That is why all four arms of
that round looked identical: not robustness, but a larger confound sitting on top.

The fix was to checkpoint the objective's heads alongside the encoder — `save_method_checkpoint` /
`load_method_checkpoint`, with `save_heads=true` the `pretrain.yaml` default and encoder-only files still accepted (the
load path reads the shape off the `encoder.`-prefixed keys and logs which it took). Verified continuous:

| warm start | step-0 `loss` | probe trajectory |
| -- | -- | -- |
| encoder only | 1.1948 | 0.1974 → 0.1636 → 0.1902 (crater and recovery) |
| encoder + heads | **0.4932** | 0.187–0.200 throughout (no crater) |

Both warm-start strengths were then re-run with head-restoring checkpoints, and those are the numbers the findings below
rest on. The pre-fix arms are kept in a separate table at the end, marked superseded.

## Findings

### The design is underpowered: the federated phase re-trains on data the warm start already saw

This is the load-bearing finding, and it bounds everything below.

`pretrain.py` runs an `EraStream` over the **full** CIFAR-100 train split, reserving 100 images/class for its own probe
sets and training on the other 400/class. `run_federated.py` then calls `holdout_split(per_class=101)` on that same full
split and partitions the remainder to clients. Both seed a fresh `torch.Generator` with `seed` and draw one
`randperm(500)` per class in sorted class order, so **the two permutation sequences are identical**. Checked directly:

|  | overlap |
| -- | -- |
| federated global hold-out ∩ pretrain's *eval* set | 100 / 101 per class |
| federated client pool ∩ pretrain's *training* set | **399 / 399 per class** |

The good half of that coincidence: the global probe is scored almost entirely on images the encoder never trained on, so
the dependent variable is clean. The damaging half: **every image a client trains on was already seen 5 (or 40) times
during pretraining.** The federated phase is a sixth pass over old data, not exposure to anything new.

That is exactly why training barely moves the probe. The 40-epoch centralized curve runs from ~0.19 at epoch 5 to 0.2400
at epoch 40, so an epoch near that point is worth on the order of 0.006 `linear_acc`. The federated phase has a budget
of one epoch, and run 0 delivers +0.0058 (0.1924 → 0.1982) — on prediction, not anomalous.

**The consequence for the non-IID question: if the whole federated phase is worth ~0.006, skew cannot cost more than
~0.006.** The measured skew effect of 0.001 is ~17% of a budget that is itself the size of the noise floor. So the
result below is not "non-IID is harmless"; it is "this design cannot resolve the question". Fixing it means giving
clients data the warm start never saw — see [Open follow-ups](#open-follow-ups).

### Neither label skew nor averaging frequency is measurable — the apparent effects do not replicate

Both warm-start strengths, post-fix, all four arms each. Warm-start values are each run 0's step-0 reading.

|  | partition | steps/round | rounds | `linear_acc` | `knn_acc` | `rankme` |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| **5-epoch base** | *(warm start)* | — | — | *0.1924* | *0.1122* | *27.6* |
|  | iid | 1 | 78 | 0.1982 | 0.1130 | 29.9 |
|  | iid | 78 | 1 | 0.1934 | 0.1164 | 30.6 |
|  | dirichlet 0.1 | 1 | 84 | 0.1972 | 0.1126 | 30.5 |
|  | dirichlet 0.1 | 78 | 2 | 0.1922 | 0.1148 | 31.4 |
| **40-epoch base** | *(warm start)* | — | — | *0.2438* | *0.1560* | *51.1* |
|  | iid | 1 | 78 | 0.2394 | 0.1598 | 55.5 |
|  | iid | 78 | 1 | 0.2416 | 0.1572 | 55.1 |
|  | dirichlet 0.1 | 1 | 84 | 0.2432 | 0.1576 | 54.3 |
|  | dirichlet 0.1 | 78 | 2 | 0.2424 | 0.1614 | 55.6 |

**The skew effect flips sign between bases.** On the 5-epoch base IID leads Dirichlet by 0.0010 (1 step/round) and
0.0012 (78). On the 40-epoch base Dirichlet leads IID by 0.0038 and 0.0008. Same configuration, opposite direction.

**The averaging-frequency effect flips too, and is inconsistent even within one base.** At 5 epochs, 1 step/round beat
78 by ~0.005 in both partitions. At 40 epochs, 78 beats 1 by 0.0022 under IID while 1 beats 78 by 0.0008 under
Dirichlet.

So the "consistent sign across the bracket" the 5-epoch base appeared to show was noise. Neither knob produces a
reproducible direction, and the whole 40-epoch spread — 0.2394 to 0.2432 — is 0.0038 wide against a within-run
trajectory range of 0.010 (run 0 spans 0.2350–0.2452). **No claim about non-IID survives this data.**

Two honest near-misses worth recording, both of which fail on inspection:

- *All four 40-epoch arms sit below their warm start* on `linear_acc` (−0.0006 to −0.0044), which would suggest a
    converged model can only be perturbed by a further pass. But `knn_acc` moves the *other* way in all four (+0.0012 to
    +0.0054), so the two probes disagree and neither direction is real.
- *The 5-epoch base gains and the 40-epoch base loses* on `linear_acc` (+0.0058 vs −0.0044). This sign flip is genuinely
    predicted by the budget argument above — an undertrained model benefits from another epoch of its own data, a
    converged one cannot — but at this effect size it is equally consistent with noise.

### The one reproducible movement is RankMe, and it does not reach the probe

`rankme` rises in **all eight** arms, and by much more than the probe noise: +2.3 to +3.8 on the 5-epoch base (27.6 →
29.9–31.4) and +3.2 to +4.5 on the 40-epoch base (51.1 → 54.3–55.6). Direction and rough magnitude are independent of
partition and of averaging frequency.

So the single federated pass does measurably change the representation's geometry — the effective rank expands — while
leaving downstream probe accuracy where it found it. Consistent with the centralized 40-epoch run, whose `rankme` was
still climbing (51.7) at a point where `linear_acc` had nearly flattened. Useful as a sanity signal that the federated
phase is doing *something*; not useful as a health discriminator here, since it fails to separate any arm from any
other.

### The pre-fix 40-epoch arms (confounded, superseded)

| run | partition | steps/round | `linear_acc` | `knn_acc` | `rankme` |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| 0 | iid | 1 | 0.2314 | 0.1504 | 51.1 |
| 1 | iid | 78 | 0.2306 | 0.1498 | 50.3 |
| 2 | dirichlet 0.1 | 1 | 0.2358 | 0.1522 | 53.1 |
| 3 | dirichlet 0.1 | 78 | 0.2342 | 0.1432 | 51.0 |

Run with the encoder-only warm start, so dominated by the decoder transient described above; superseded by the 40-epoch
rows in the main table. Recorded only to show that the confound *also* produced a null — by flattening everything rather
than by there being nothing to find.

### Reconstruction loss is anti-correlated with skew, so it is not a health readout here

The Dirichlet arms reach *lower* MAE loss than the IID arms (0.551, 0.579 vs 0.603, 0.706 pre-fix; 0.451 vs 0.498
post-fix at 1 step/round). Less diverse shards make the local reconstruction task easier, so the loss improves for a
reason unrelated to representation quality — the same trap [P0.3.8](../phase0/P0.3.8.md) documents for the recon gap
false-firing on distribution shifts. Read `linear_acc` / `knn_acc` / `rankme` in federated runs and treat `loss` as
diagnostic only.

A second reason to discount federated loss: at `steps_per_round=78` each client warms its own decoder independently and
four separately-trained decoders then get averaged, which is destructive in a way that averaging four nearly-identical
ones is not. The probe is unaffected because it reads the encoder.

### A residual optimizer transient

Post-fix, loss still spikes at step 1 (0.4932 → 0.9692) and settles by step 5. This is not the decoder: it is fresh
AdamW state, whose first update is badly scaled before the moment estimates warm up. Optimizer state is not
checkpointed. It no longer touches the probe, so it is noted rather than fixed.

## Centralized reference numbers

For reading the federated arms against, from the same encoder/method/data configuration:

| run | `linear_acc` | `knn_acc` | `rankme` |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| frozen random init | 0.0835 | 0.0570 | 2.86 |
| single pass, from scratch (313 steps) | 0.1162 | 0.0474 | 6.98 |
| 5-epoch warm start | ~0.19 | ~0.11 | ~27.5 |
| 40-epoch warm start | 0.2326 | 0.1524 | 43.1 |
| 40 epochs, end of run | 0.2400 | 0.1606 | 51.7 |
| 40-epoch base + centralized single pass | 0.2362 | 0.1560 | 58.1 |

The 40-epoch run was still improving monotonically at its final step, so 0.2400 is horizon-limited, not a ceiling. The
single-pass from-scratch row is the evidence for warm-starting: +0.018 `linear_acc` over random init, with `knn_acc`
going backwards.

## How to run

```bash
# Get CIFAR-100 (CIFAR100Source never downloads):
uv run python -c "from torchvision.datasets import CIFAR100; CIFAR100(root='data/cifar100', train=True, download=True)"

# Shared encoder/method overrides, used by every command below:
ENC="encoder.patch_size=4 encoder.embed_dim=192 encoder.depth=12 encoder.num_heads=3 encoder.mlp_ratio=4"
MAE="ssl=mae ssl.decoder_dim=192 ssl.decoder_depth=4 ssl.decoder_heads=3 ssl.decoder_mlp_ratio=4"
DATA="data=cifar100 data_root=data/cifar100 img_size=32 max_per_class=null max_train_per_class=null"

# Warm starts (save_heads=true by default — do NOT use an encoder-only checkpoint here):
for E in 5 40; do
  uv run python scripts/pretrain.py $MAE $ENC $DATA \
      stream.support_per_class=40 stream.query_per_class=50 stream.era_eval_per_class=10 \
      batch_size=128 optim.lr=5e-4 epochs=$E \
      pretrain_dir=checkpoints/mae_${E}ep_cifar100 device=cuda
done

# Centralized controls — from-scratch single pass, then warm-started single pass:
uv run python scripts/run_loop.py $MAE $ENC $DATA stream=iid init=from_scratch \
    stream.support_per_class=40 stream.query_per_class=50 stream.era_eval_per_class=10 \
    batch_size=128 optim.lr=5e-4 eval_every=25 \
    eval.compare_b5=true eval.probe=linear device=cuda
uv run python scripts/run_loop.py $MAE $ENC $DATA stream=iid \
    init=pretrained pretrain_dir=checkpoints/mae_40ep_cifar100 \
    stream.support_per_class=40 stream.query_per_class=50 stream.era_eval_per_class=10 \
    batch_size=128 optim.lr=5e-4 eval_every=25 \
    eval.compare_b5=true eval.probe=linear device=cuda

# The four F2 arms (swap pretrain_dir for the 40-epoch base):
uv run python scripts/run_federated.py -m \
    partition.scheme=iid,dirichlet partition.alpha=0.1 steps_per_round=1,78 \
    $MAE $ENC $DATA stream=iid num_clients=4 strategy=fedavg \
    init=pretrained pretrain_dir=checkpoints/mae_5ep_cifar100 \
    support_per_class=40 query_per_class=50 era_eval_per_class=10 \
    batch_size=128 optim.lr=5e-4 device=cuda
```

`stream=iid` is load-bearing: `federated.yaml` defaults to `class_blocked`, which would add temporal correlation on top
of the partition skew and make any result unattributable. Note also the cost asymmetry — the orchestrator probes the
global model every round with no cadence knob, so `steps_per_round=1` spends ~26 minutes doing 78 probes for 78 batches
of training per client, while `steps_per_round=78` finishes in ~35 seconds.

Each run writes `global_run_log.jsonl` (plus one per client) to its Hydra run dir; read back with
`cafl4ds.run_log.read_run` / `tabulate`.

## Open follow-ups

- **Disjoint pretrain / federated data — the blocking fix.** While clients re-train on the warm start's own training
    images, the federated phase has a one-epoch budget worth ~0.006 `linear_acc` and skew has almost nothing to damage.
    Two ways out. (i) *Disjoint slices of CIFAR-100*: pretrain on half of each class, federate on the other half.
    `max_per_class` takes the **first** N per class ([`_subsample_per_class`](../../../cafl4ds/data/sources.py) is
    order-preserving), so this needs a complementary `skip_per_class` knob — a small source-level addition. (ii)
    *Cross-corpus, zero code change*: pretrain on STL-10 at 32px and federate on CIFAR-100, reusing the cross-dataset
    pattern [P0.3.6](../phase0/P0.3.6.md) already established. (ii) is available today but conflates distribution shift
    with the federated question, so (i) is the cleaner instrument and (ii) the faster sanity check.
- **Seed replication — after the above, not before.** Replicating an underpowered design only measures its noise floor
    more precisely. Once the federated phase does real work, only `steps_per_round=78` is worth replicating: it is the
    maximum-drift condition and runs in ~35s, where the 1-step arms take ~26 minutes and are near-centralized by
    construction. `seed=0,1,2,3,4` × two partitions is ~10 runs. Note the two warm-start strengths already act as an
    informal replication, and they disagree on the sign of both effects.
- **Feature skew as the sharper axis.** Label skew reaches a label-free objective only through the feature distribution
    it induces, which over 100 classes is mild. Assigning different clients different pixel transforms via
    [`_apply_pixel_transform`](../../../cafl4ds/data/sources.py) (`grayscale`, `phase_scramble` — built for
    [P0.3.8](../phase0/P0.3.8.md)) would give genuinely different input distributions, which is what SSL responds to.
- **Strategies, once there is damage to repair.** FedProx and the adaptive server optimizers exist to buy low drift
    without frequent communication. Sweeping them before FedAvg demonstrably suffers gives nothing to compare against —
    and note that at 4 clients on this shard size, `steps_per_round=5` yields only ~16 rounds, too few for a server-side
    optimizer to differentiate. Use `steps_per_round=1` (78 rounds) for those comparisons.
- **Cross-client divergence as the direct readout — now built, not yet run.** The global-model probe is the right
    dependent variable but can hide the mechanism: two clients drifting hard in opposite directions can still average to
    a mediocre-but-not-bad global model, which is exactly the ambiguity F2 could not resolve.
    [`federated/divergence.py`](../../../cafl4ds/federated/divergence.py) now measures each participant's representation
    against the broadcast anchor, and every client pair against each other, on the shared global probe set — logged into
    the same per-round health series as `client_anchor_cka_{mean,max}` / `cross_client_cka_{mean,max}`
    (`track_divergence`, on by default). Re-running the four arms with this on is the cheapest way to tell "no
    heterogeneity effect" from "the readout cannot see one".
