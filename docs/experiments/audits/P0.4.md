# Audit — P0.4.x (instability / divergence-mode instrument calibration)

Peer-review-style audit of the instability failure-mode sub-studies (P0.4.0 LR-driven divergence → P0.4.1 data
surprise/shock), conducted 2026-08-12 against the substudy docs, their `latest-results` distillates, and the promoted
artifacts under [`../phase0/artifacts/`](../phase0/artifacts/) (`P0.4.0/{dose_seed0,dose_seed1,batch16_seed0}`,
`P0.4.1/{gate_seed0,gate_seed1,dataset_scrambled,scale_dose}`). Numbers were spot-checked against the JSON gates and
reproduce (the two-seed divergence dose-response, the batch-16 ablation, the two-seed surprise gate, the phase-scramble
non-fire, and the amplitude ladder were all re-read from the artifacts); this is a review of *conclusions*, not
arithmetic.

Each point carries a **→ Remediation** line tagging how it should be addressed: `[wording]` (doc edit, no runs),
`[analysis]` (post-hoc on existing data, no new runs), `[new runs]` (re-run an existing config, mostly seconds on CPU),
`[decision]` (needs an architectural call), or `[defer → Phase-1]`.

## Verdict

P0.4 is the cleanest of Phase 0's three modes, and honestly so — it is the one mode where the *mechanism* genuinely
reproduces at toy scale (an optimization runaway, not a representation phenomenon), so the calibration is not fighting
its vehicle the way P0.3 was. The two-sided gate passes on both poles, the leading-indicator payoff is real, the
cross-mode specificity check (grad norm stays bounded under collapse) is a genuine discriminator, and the two "vehicle"
findings — the loss must be *unbounded* (MAE MSE, P0.4.0) and, within MAE, *raw* (not `norm_pix`, P0.4.1) — are a
non-obvious, well-motivated pair. The distillates are already candid that the lead time is "most likely not
generalizable."

But the ✅ over-reads toward **operational readiness** in one load-bearing way the docs do *not* flag, and several
individual claims are stronger than the two-seed, single-operating-point data behind them. The single dominating gap:
**everything of value in this calibration is contingent on a training configuration the production system probably will
not use** — SGD + raw MSE. Under standard MAE pretraining (AdamW + `norm_pix`), the docs' own claims say the leading
indicator disappears and surprise detection disappears; what survives is a coincident NaN alarm that needs no
calibration (C1). That is the P0.4 analogue of the collapse audit's backbone mismatch (P0.2 C3) and the forgetting
audit's reader-availability gap (P0.3 C3).

Grouped below as over-claims (A), contradictions/tensions (B), structural gaps (C), and conclusions the data supports
but the docs did not draw (D).

## Strengths (so the criticism is calibrated)

- **The two vehicle findings are the substudy's real contribution** and are correctly framed as "the vehicle changes
    what the instrument can see," not as nuisance. The nesting (unbounded loss → then raw-vs-normalized *within* MAE) is
    a clean, generalizable lesson.
- **Cross-mode specificity is a true discriminator, not a smoke test.** Showing the collapse PC rides its loss to the −1
    floor while its grad norm stays bounded (peak ~2.3) establishes the instrument fires on *divergence*, not "something
    bad in general" — the one thing that separates a useful instability detector from a generic alarm.
- **The distributional-OOD refutation (P0.4.1) is the headline and it is honest.** Reframing "surprise detector" down to
    "content-shock (error-magnitude) detector — *not* a distribution-shift detector" on the strength of the
    phase-scramble non-fire is exactly the kind of scope-narrowing most work omits.
- **The lead-time honesty is good.** Both the P0.4.0 Insights and the distillate state the lead is regime-specific and
    inversely coupled to severity (longest at threshold, → 0 as instability worsens). This is a rare admission that the
    "payoff" is conditional.
- **The two-sided gate is genuinely two-sided here** (unlike the single-pass collapse corner, P0.2 B3): the healthy arm
    is a certified genuinely-learning MAE (final ≤ 0.9× initial, gated), not an under-developed relative reference.

## A. Over-claims / conclusions stronger than the evidence

**A1 — The load-bearing "AdamW damps the runaway to a no-lead knife-edge" claim has no preserved artifact.** This is the
*key finding* of P0.4.0 (it is what promotes the vehicle from "MAE" to "MAE + SGD"), and the doc states specific numbers
for it — "at `lr ∈ [1, 10]` the grad norm blows to `10³–10⁶` but the normalized step absorbs it … at `lr = 100` it goes
to NaN in a single step." Yet every promoted artifact is SGD; there is no AdamW run in the tree. A conference reviewer
would flag the paper's headline mechanistic claim as resting on an experiment that is described but not shown.
`optim=adamw` is a wired, one-flag swap (`configs/optim/adamw.yaml` exists) — this is a seconds-long confirmatory run,
not a research task.

→ **Remediation:** `[new runs]` — **highest-value, lowest-cost.** Run the P0.4.0 dose-response with `optim=adamw` and
promote it as a small `P0.4.0/adamw_seed0` artifact (or a slim `{lr, peak_grad_norm, nonfinite_step, lead}` table);
confirm the no-lead knife-edge. Until it exists, mark the AdamW numbers as "exploratory, not preserved" in the doc.

**A2 — The same gap applies to P0.4.1's `norm_pix` claim.** "A saturating noise shock under `norm_pix` gives only a
~1.5× grad blip" is the load-bearing justification for turning `norm_pix_loss` off — the second half of the nested
vehicle finding — and it too has no artifact. `ssl.norm_pix_loss=true` is a one-flag toggle over the existing gate
config.

→ **Remediation:** `[new runs]` (seconds) + `[wording]`. Re-run the `burst_scale=4` gate with `norm_pix_loss=true` and
record the ~1.5× blip as a promoted line (a natural companion to A1); or, if left unrun, label it "exploratory."

**A3 — The headline grad-norm contrast (`1.2×10⁶×`) is a truncation artifact and is not portable; only the binary
separation is.** The "smallest diverging peak" that anchors the contrast is whichever arm NaNs *earliest* (fewest finite
steps recorded before the blow-up), so it is not even monotone in LR: seed 0's min-diverging arm is `lr=10` (peak
`5.2×10⁵`, above `lr=3`'s `6×10⁸`), seed 1's is `lr=30` (`1.3×10⁵`), batch-16's is `lr=3` (`1.5×10⁴`). The reported
contrast consequently swings **`3.4×10⁴×` → `3.3×10⁵×` → `1.2×10⁶×`** across three runs of the *same* phenomenon — a
~35× spread. The verdict (every diverging arm clears the healthy peak by ≥ `min_separation`=100×; threshold `lr=3`; lead
5\) is rock-solid; the specific `10⁶` number is not. This is the instability twin of the collapse audit's "magnitudes are
thread/device-dependent, cite the verdict" (P0.2 A4).

→ **Remediation:** `[wording]`. Lead the distillate and `index.md` with the *verdict* (≥100× separation, threshold
`lr=3`, 5-step lead, both seeds) and demote the `1.2×10⁶×` figure to an illustrative, non-portable example. No new runs.

**A4 — "Lead time shrinks with severity" rests on n = 2 direct measurements.** The claim is stated crisply in both
distillates, but the only *measured* lead times on the severity axis are the two runaway rungs of `scale_dose`
(`scale 8 → 14`, `scale 16 → 5`); the transient rungs have no NaN, hence no lead, and P0.4.0's lead is a *single* point
(the `lr=3` operating point, `lead=5`, identical across all three seeds/batches). The higher-LR arms (`lr=10, 30`) never
had their spike-step logged, so P0.4.0 supports the coupling only through the *time-to-NaN* proxy (nonfinite step
`8 → 5 → 4`), not through measured lead. So the direct evidence for the inverse lead↔severity law is two data points on
one seed. The theoretical argument (geometric growth, per-step multiplier `|1 − LR·λ|`) is sound and makes it
*plausible*, but "shrinks with severity" is presented as an established empirical trend on n = 2.

→ **Remediation:** `[wording]` + optional `[analysis]`/`[new runs]`. Soften to "consistent with the operating-envelope
prediction (two runaway rungs: 14 → 5 steps)"; if a firmer trend is wanted, log the spike-step at `lr=10, 30` in P0.4.0
(post-hoc if the per-step traces survive, else a seconds-long re-run) to add points to the same curve.

## B. Contradictions / internal tensions

**B1 — "Same word, two denominators": `change_ratio` (within-arm) vs the headline fire (cross-arm).** The design section
defines the gate as "the shock's burst peak grad norm ≥ `change_ratio` × the control's same-step peak" — a *cross-arm*
ratio. But the artifact's per-arm `change_ratio` field is a *within-arm* ratio (shock burst peak / that arm's own
pre-burst base peak): at the operating point it reads **4.6×**, while the headline "78×" is the *cross-arm* fire
(`fire_ratio` = shock burst peak / control burst peak). Both are legitimate, but they are different quantities sharing
one name, and the doc's Reads bullet uses `change_ratio` for the cross-arm gate while the JSON uses it for the
within-arm number. The user flagged this directly and it is a real source of confusion.

→ **Remediation:** `[wording]`. Name them distinctly in the doc — e.g. `fire_ratio` (cross-arm, the gate) vs
`within_arm_change_ratio` (the change-point sanity check) — and state which one the "78×" headline is (cross-arm).

**B2 — The "forcing lever is the data, not the learning rate" intro sits awkwardly with the `lr_sweep` negative
control.** P0.4.1's framing is that LR is held fixed at `lr_op` and data is the lever; the `lr_sweep` then re-runs the
shock at `lr = 3, 10` to show LR-divergence is shock-independent. This is a legitimate negative control, but the doc
introduces `lr_sweep` in the Reads/Findings without reconciling it against the "LR is constant" premise, which reads as
a contradiction (the user flagged it). It is not one — `lr_op` is the operating point; `lr_sweep` is a deliberate
off-operating-point control — but the text does not say so.

→ **Remediation:** `[wording]`. One clause: "LR is fixed at `lr_op` for the surprise gate; the `lr_sweep` is a separate
*negative control* that deliberately raises LR to confirm warm-phase LR-divergence is shock-independent."

**B3 — Measurement-window ambiguity: the fire is read *during* the burst, recovery *after* it — the doc does not say so
plainly.** The user asked at what point the reading is taken. From the artifacts it is unambiguous — `fire_ratio` /
`surprise_ratio` are computed over the burst window (steps 300–308), `recover_ratio` / `tail_min_grad_norm` over the
tail (post-308) — but the prose blends "spikes then recovers" without stating that the *fire* verdict is a burst-window
measurement and the *transient-vs-runaway* verdict is a tail measurement.

→ **Remediation:** `[wording]`. State the two windows explicitly (fire = burst window; recovery = tail), and that
Finding #1's fire is a `burst_kind=noise` (saturating) shock while the refutation is `burst_kind=dataset` (the other
user question).

## C. Structural gaps to close before Phase 1

**C1 — [Highest] The whole valuable calibration is contingent on a non-default training config (SGD + raw MSE); the docs
never test whether it survives the production one (AdamW + `norm_pix`).** Standard MAE/ViT pretraining uses **AdamW**
and **`norm_pix_loss=true`**. By the substudies' *own* claims, under that config: (i) the grad-norm **lead time
vanishes** (AdamW damps the runaway to a one-step knife-edge, A1), and (ii) **data-surprise becomes invisible**
(`norm_pix` caps the per-patch loss ~1, A2). What is left is grad norm as a *coincident* NaN detector — which is trivial
and needs no calibration. So the two things that make P0.4 worth a substudy — the **leading indicator** and the
**change-point / surprise** read — are precisely the two the production config is claimed to erase. The mode ✅ is earned
for "SGD + raw-MSE MAE"; it is silent on whether *any* of the early-warning value ports to the optimizer/loss the
Phase-1 loop actually runs. This is the exact shape of P0.2 C3 (collapse suite is joint-embedding/terminal-BN-specific)
and P0.3 C3 (readers presuppose retained task-A data): a calibration valid *given* a vehicle the target system may not
adopt.

→ **Remediation:** `[decision]` + `[wording]`, partially discharged by A1/A2. Record in `project-plan`/`index.md` which
optimizer and MAE loss variant the Phase-1 streaming loop uses. If AdamW/`norm_pix` (the standard), state plainly that
grad norm ports only as a *coincident* NaN detector and the leading-indicator + surprise reads do **not** carry — and
that an early-warning instability signal under adaptive optimizers is a Phase-1 open problem (no instrument here
provides it). This should be flagged as the mode's *defining* readiness caveat, not folded silently into the ✅.

**C2 — The severity/lead-time and batch-robustness reads are single-seed; only the two-sided gate is replicated.** The
core gate is 2 seeds (good). But the batch-size ablation (`batch16_seed0`), the amplitude dose-response (`scale_dose`,
seed 0), and the cross-mode specificity read (off the collapse harness, unpreserved) are each **n = 1**. The batch claim
("not a batch-size artifact") rests on a single alternative batch size (128 → 16) at one seed; the severity ladder and
its lead-time trend (A4) are one seed. Cheaper to shore up here than anywhere else in Phase 0 (seconds/run), so the
single-seed status is a choice worth revisiting rather than an inherent limit.

→ **Remediation:** `[new runs]` (low priority, very cheap) + `[wording]`. Either add a seed to the `scale_dose` and
`batch16` reads, or explicitly label them "single-seed, illustrative" (the gate itself is 2-seed). Promote the
cross-mode specificity grad-norm trace as a tiny artifact rather than "reads off the collapse harness."

**C3 — Specificity is tested against collapse only, not against forgetting or a healthy shift.** Cross-mode specificity
established grad norm does not fire on *collapse*. It is untested against the *forgetting* PC (P0.3) and — more relevant
— against a benign in-regime change (the P0.4.1 distributional-OOD non-fire partly covers this: grad norm correctly
ignores a structure-destroying but in-range shift, a genuine benign-shift null). Forgetting is a slow representation
phenomenon and almost certainly will not spike the grad norm, but "specific to divergence" is asserted on one of the two
sibling modes.

→ **Remediation:** `[analysis]` or `[defer → Phase-1]`. Note that specificity is established vs collapse + vs in-range
distribution shift (P0.4.1), and that vs forgetting it is expected-but-unchecked; a one-line read off any P0.3 MAE run's
grad-norm trace would close it cheaply.

## D. Conclusions the data supports but the docs missed

**D1 — The healthy operating LR sits close to the divergence threshold — a narrow safe band worth stating.** The healthy
reference is `lr=1` and the threshold is `lr=3`: a **3× margin**. Moreover `lr=1` is *not* the best-learning arm —
`lr=0.1` descends further (seed 0: final 0.66× init vs `lr=1`'s 0.74×), and `lr=1` clears the "learns" bar (≤ 0.9×) only
modestly. So without warmup/clip the SGD-MAE has a thin corridor between "learns best" and "diverges" (~sub-decade in
LR). This is a positive framing of *why* the leading indicator matters (small overshoots are easy) and a concrete
toy-scale operating-envelope datum.

→ **Remediation:** `[wording]`. State the ~3× healthy→threshold margin and that the chosen healthy LR is a
deliberately-near-threshold reference (warmup/clip off), not the best learner. No new runs.

**D2 — The phase-scramble non-fire is a stronger result than "distribution shift doesn't fire": the loss *drops*.** On
the phase-scrambled burst the shock's burst-mean loss (0.030) is *below* the base (0.058) — `surprise_ratio` 0.54×, i.e.
the "harder" (structure-destroyed) input is *easier* to reconstruct because smooth texture is more inpaintable than
natural images. That is a sharper statement than "OOD is invisible": MAE reconstruction difficulty is *anti-correlated*
with the intuitive notion of novelty here. It is the single most convincing evidence that the grad norm reads
inpaintability, not novelty, and deserves to be the lead example of the refutation rather than a parenthetical.

→ **Remediation:** `[wording]`. Promote the "structure destroyed, yet loss drops" phase-scramble case to the primary
refutation illustration; it is the one preserved distributional artifact and the most counterintuitive.

**D3 — Only the phase-scramble refutation rung is a promoted artifact; color/grayscale/in-range-noise are cited but
unpreserved.** The refutation lists four distributional-OOD cases; the tree preserves one (`dataset_scrambled`). The
other three numbers (color CIFAR 1.18/1.97, grayscale 1.04/2.27, in-range noise 0.80/1.51) are re-runnable via the
`data_b` / `burst_scale` knobs but are not backed by promoted JSON.

→ **Remediation:** `[wording]` (note they are re-runnable-not-promoted) or `[new runs]`-lite (a slim combined
`refutation_ladder.json` if the four-way table is meant to be load-bearing). Phase-scramble alone already carries the
qualitative claim, so this is low priority.

Bottom line: the instability ✅ is earned for the narrow, real claim that **the grad-norm gate fires two-sided on
divergence, leads the loss NaN, and is specific to divergence — under an unbounded loss (raw-MSE MAE) and an undamped
optimizer (SGD).** As a *Phase-1-ready* instability monitor it is thinner than the ✅ implies: its two valuable reads
(leading indicator, surprise change-point) are, by the substudies' own mechanism, contingent on SGD + raw MSE and are
claimed to vanish under the standard AdamW + `norm_pix` config the production loop likely runs (C1) — and the two
findings that establish that contingency are themselves the mode's only *unpreserved* claims (A1/A2). Everything the
gate verifies at toy scale it verifies well; the open question is whether *anything but the coincident NaN alarm*
survives the transfer to the real training config.
