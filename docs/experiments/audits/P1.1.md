# Audit — P1.1.x learning to read degradation (2026-11-11)

Peer-review audit of the four P1.1 sub-studies: **P1.1.0** (the healthy-movement null and canary power), **P1.1.1** (the
single-drive reading rule), **P1.1.2** (the label-free lens screen) and **P1.1.3** (the CKA-drift deep dive). It reads
them against their detail docs ([`phase1/P1.1.0.md`](../phase1/P1.1.0.md), [`.1`](../phase1/P1.1.1.md),
[`.2`](../phase1/P1.1.2.md), [`.3`](../phase1/P1.1.3.md)), their `latest-results` distillates, the study summary
([`P1.1.md`](../latest-results/P1.1.md)), the [phase index](../phase1/index.md), and the promoted artifacts under
[`../phase1/artifacts/`](../phase1/artifacts/).

The code was reviewed as it ran (commit `818dcd4`, the tip of `feat/P1.1`). The shared modules were also read as they
stand at P1.2's close (`87643ae`), to check that P1.1's documented commands still compute what they did. Later studies
enter only as forward pointers, where a later result qualifies a P1.1 claim: P1.2.2's decoder (C1), the kNN erosion over
a full pass (P1.2.1, P1.2.5; C3), P1.2.5's collapse control (C4), and P1.2.5's handover on how recipes are judged (C2,
C3).

## Verdict

**P1.1 earns its ✅ on its central claims, and the code behind them is sound.** No result-changing bug was found. The
headline numbers recompute exactly from the raw corpora: the permutation p-values and MDDs, the reading rule's four
panel thresholds and fresh-seed maxima, the damage rebuilds, and P1.1.3's sign-flip test (*t* 35.14, *p* = 1/32). Every
analysis re-runs from the raw outputs to the promoted report, bit for bit or to float32 noise. The reading rule holds
about its 5% level in a larger simulation than the unit tests run.

The weaknesses sit in the claims, not the code, and fall into three groups.

- **One claim is in-sample.** P1.1.3's "the middle blocks catch 4 of 5 forgetting arms without their twin" is scored on
    the same data the blocks were chosen on. Read on all 12 blocks, it is 3 of 5 (B1).
- **Several numbers carry a wider scope than their test supports.** The canary's MDD bounds sustained gaps only (B2).
    "≲10% false alarms" is a point estimate with a wide interval (B3). The fine-tune ceiling is read at its best epoch
    on the set it scores (A3).
- **Later evidence has moved under three conclusions, and P1.1's docs mostly do not say so.** Every MAE reading carried
    the undersized decoder P1.2.2 later found, including the well CKA drift is anchored to (C1). "Benign" JE compression
    rests partly on a canary P1.1.1 itself shows is weak on collapse (C3). And the backbone-RankMe discriminator now has
    two knobs it calls benign while the projector collapses (C4). Only P1.1.0 points forward to the later evidence (F1).

The largest open item is structural. P1.1's job was to earn the right to read P1.3's runs, and P1.3's setup has since
moved: three arms and possibly a fourth (D5), forgetting read on the two JE starts (one from DINO), new recipes that
P1.3 will retune, and a longer horizon. So every number P1.1 hands to P1.3 was measured on a setup P1.3 does not run
(D1, D2). Two pieces were never built. No plan states the power the referee needs on past regimes, where forgetting
shows first (D3). And P1.1's label-free forgetting reader serves MAE, while P1.3 reads forgetting on JE, where P1.1
found none (D4). This is foundation work, so it stays with P1.1 rather than passing to the study that depends on it. It
opens a fifth sub-study, **P1.1.4**, which rebuilds the reading tools on P1.3's setup. P1.3 reads degradation only once
its arms' readers pass P1.1's bars.

## Strengths

- **The numbers reproduce.** Two independent reviews recomputed the load-bearing statistics from the raw `health.csv`
    and the promoted lens values, and every one matched the reports and the docs.
- **The matched control is exact.** Rebuilt on the real BDD labels, the IID control delivers the walk's frames at the
    walk's batch sizes and step count, at every overlap level and seed. The probes and selection set never touch the
    well or the drive.
- **The statistics were designed, not improvised.** An exact 252-split permutation test with the maximum taken inside
    each split. A reading rule whose leave-one-out refits bands *and* threshold. A sign-flip test that pays for its
    52-reader search inside the test.
- **Pass bars were fixed before the data.** P1.1.3 wrote each item's bar down first and reports its fails as plainly as
    its passes. Items 1, 2 and 4 closing off three routes is real value for P1.3.
- **The specificity control is a genuine contribution.** One arm with a large weight change and no canary cost sorted
    eleven candidate readers. It is the surgical analogue of P0.5.3's dose-down, and it should carry into every future
    reader screen.
- **The docs are candid about limits.** Short horizon, one well per familiarity level, surgical damage that screens but
    cannot certify, and a forgetting event that was shallow and recoverable.

## A. Code validity

The code audit ran first, in six parts: the data and stream layer, the instruments, the statistics, branching and
warm-up, the P1.1.2 / P1.1.3 analysis scripts, and a full re-run of every analysis from the raw outputs.

**Assessment of affected claims.** No claim rests on a bug. One code-level choice changes a published number mildly
(A3). The rest are latent defects that P1.1's settings do not trigger (A2), a noise floor worth stating (A4), and two
analyses that need hand-patched paths to re-run (A5). Nothing needs to be re-run before the rest of the audit proceeds.

- **A1 — No result-changing bug; the load-bearing numbers recompute from raw data.**

    - **Issue.** Checked and found correct:
        - *The stream:* IID exactness, RNG isolation of the shuffle, probe and selection-set disjointness, the drive
            bit-identical across overlap levels, the resumed tail, the recovery pool.
        - *The instruments:* debiased CKA against Kornblith's reference (to 1e-8 at P1.1's three shapes), plain CKA,
            Procrustes, TwoNN against `skdim`, RankMe, the canary's fit/score split and balance, drift anchoring.
        - *Block indexing:* 1-based, so "blocks 4–11" and `block08` mean what the docs say; `block12` is the last block
            before the final norm, and `backbone` is the monitor's surface.
        - *The statistics:* the permutation test, the MDD formula, the reading rule's bands, Gumbel threshold and
            leave-one-out.
        - *Wells and branches:* a well saves and reloads the whole method; damage touches only the stated weights; dose 0 is
            a no-op; the global RNG is untouched; no state leaks across a seed ensemble.
        - All P1.1 unit tests pass on the as-run tree. The shared modules at P1.2's close leave P1.1's computations
            unchanged at its settings.
        - *The reproduction:* all six analysis scripts were re-run on the as-run code from the raw outputs. Four reports are
            bit-identical to the promoted ones (both null reports, the reading report, the forgetting-lens report). The
            other two differ only in float32 noise: `analyze_lenses` in the encoder-wide weight change (~1e-8 relative),
            which shifts MAE's ρ against weight change by ≤ 0.013; `analyze_cka_drift` in the recomputed per-stratum drift
            (≤ 2e-4 relative), and bit-identical given the promoted stratum cache. No verdict, count or doc-quoted number
            changes.
    - **Remediation.** None for correctness.
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

- **A2 — Latent defects that P1.1's settings do not trigger.**

    - **Issue.**
        - `twonn_id` claims to drop duplicate rows but does not: `torch.cdist`'s matrix-multiply path does not return exact
            zeros. P1.1's probes are distinct images, so no number moves.
        - At a skipped checkpoint, the smoothed canary still yields a *z* from the previous 4 readings. The "persisting" tag
            is read at one such checkpoint (step 118). Calibration and test treat it alike, so the threshold absorbs it.
        - The grid checks compare only the first seed's step list; `_union` would silently overwrite a duplicated seed. The
            actual corpora share one grid and disjoint seeds.
        - `max_train_per_regime` combined with `well_overlap` swaps in frames the drive never streams; `drop_last=True`
            miscounts the walk's delivered set. P1.1 ran with neither.
        - Cosmetic: plain CKA runs in float32 (error below the rule's log floor); one docstring says debiased CKA can exceed
            1 (it cannot); `cka_reads._run`'s docstring does not match what it counts.
    - **Remediation.** `[engineering]` Fix each with a unit test that would have caught it, before P1.3 reuses the
        modules.
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

- **A3 — The fine-tune ceiling is read at its best epoch, on the set it scores.**

    - **Issue.** `supervised_ceiling.py` reports `best_drive_bacc`, the maximum over epochs of balanced accuracy on the
        drive pool, which is the scoring set. The ViT-B rows read 0.738 (MAE init) and 0.746 (ImageNet init) at their best
        epochs, against 0.683 and 0.691 at the final epoch. The tiny-ViT supervised rows have the same issue. So the
        "~0.74 practical ceiling", and the wells' "0.11–0.30 below" it, overstate the headroom.
    - **Remediation.** `[analysis]` Report the epoch chosen on the selection set (already logged in the trace), or the
        final epoch; restate the ceiling as ~0.69–0.74. `[wording]` in P1.1.0. ViT-B still clearly beats the tiny ViT.
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

- **A4 — HPU runs are not reproducible step for step, which sets a noise floor on per-seed twin reads.**

    - **Issue.** Two identical-config seed-0 runs (`dial/je_iid_alpha0.0_lr1e-4` and its `_s012` twin) match exactly to
        about step 101, then diverge by up to 1.7 kNN points and 2.0 RankMe by step 118 (4.4 points at α = 1). The RNG
        pairing between a dose and its twin is exact; floating-point noise on the HPU is not. Whether `torch.manual_seed`
        seeds the HPU's mask RNG is also unconfirmed. If it does not, a seed's masks depend on its place in the ensemble,
        though a dose and its twin still pair.
    - **Remediation.** `[wording]` State the floor where per-seed twin differences carry a claim: P1.1.1's late-branch
        dents, and P1.1.3 item 4's per-seed excess (−2.0, −7.7, +0.1 points). `[engineering]` Confirm HPU seeding with a
        two-seed order swap.
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

- **A5 — Two analyses do not re-run from the documented commands.**

    - **Issue.**
        - `analyze_lenses` rebuilds checkpoint paths by stripping the manifests' `/workspace/` prefix and resolving against
            the working directory. From a tree without `outputs/` and `wells/` it fails on the first checkpoint.
        - The P0.3.9 re-runs' `.hydra/config.yaml` hard-code container mounts (`/mnt/cifar100`, `/mnt/stl10`,
            `/mnt/imagenette`) that do not exist on the host, with no CLI override; `read_forgetting_lenses` needs
            hand-edited copies.
        - Thread-count-dependent float32 sums make two outputs non-bit-reproducible without the promoted caches (A1).
        - Caches land beside their inputs (`lens_values.json` in the features dir on the share), and `analyze_lenses`'s
            defaults (12 workers × 2 threads) oversubscribe a shared node when the cache is missing.
    - **Remediation.** `[engineering]` Resolve checkpoint and data roots from config, not from the manifest or the
        recorded mounts; accumulate the weight change in float64; send caches to the run dir; default to a modest worker
        count. `[wording]` Each "How to run" names the roots it needs.
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

## B. Over-claims

- **B1 — The middle-block single-drive read on P0.3.9 is in-sample.**

    - **Issue.** Blocks 4–11 were chosen from item 5's paired read, on the same 5 forgetting and 5 healthy arms that the
        follow-up then scores. The PC arms' max *z* (3.79, 5.18, 3.49, 3.00, 4.32) do not depend on the panel; only the
        threshold does. On the chosen panel it is 4 of 5. On all 12 blocks, all 12 plus the backbone, or blocks 1–11, it
        is 3 of 5, which misses the pre-registered ≥ 4/5 bar. The doc's "the BDD read pays no selection cost" is true of
        BDD and silent on P0.3.9. The paired sign-flip test is unaffected: it pays for its search.
    - **Remediation.** `[wording]` Label the P0.3.9 single-drive read in-sample, and report the unselected panel beside
        it, in the detail doc, the distillate, the P1.1 summary and the index row. `[analysis]` Optional: a
        leave-one-pair-out block choice, which is out-of-sample at 5 pairs.
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

- **B2 — The canary's MDD bounds sustained gaps, not the test that was run.**

    - **Issue.** The MDD is the power of a single-checkpoint, unadjusted two-sample *t*-test. The canary verdicts come
        from the max-over-checkpoints permutation test, which needs |*d*| ≈ 3.3, not ≈ 2. Injected into the real IID
        seeds, a drop held over the whole drive is caught at 1× MDD. A drop over the last 10 checkpoints needs about 2×
        MDD for 80% power, and a final-checkpoint drop is rarely caught even at 2×. So "no loss larger than ~2–4.5 points"
        holds for a sustained gap only. Two smaller slips: the doc calls the contrast "paired" (it is unpaired), and it
        judges the walk's within-arm change (0.629 → 0.618) against the between-arm MDD.
    - **Remediation.** `[analysis]` Report the max-test's power at a few gap shapes (sustained, late, transient).
        `[wording]` Scope the claim to sustained gaps in P1.1.0, its distillate and the summary.
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

- **B3 — "≲10% false alarms per panel" is a point estimate with a wide interval.**

    - **Issue.** It rests on 1–2 alarms in 15 out-of-sample trials. The 95% upper bounds are 32% (1/15) and 40% (2/15). A
        drive is read on two panels, so its alarm rate is higher than either panel's. And the fresh seeds 10–14 finished
        (09:10) before the final report (11:37) that replaced the simulated threshold. The doc scores both methods on "the
        same check", so the seeds were likely out-of-sample for calibration but not for the choice of method. The
        simulation supports the rule's level (about 4% mean), so the rule is sound; the empirical claim is loose.
    - **Remediation.** `[wording]` Quote the interval and the per-drive (two-panel) rate beside "≲10%", and note the
        method-choice point. P1.3's recalibration (D1) should pre-register its fresh seeds.
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

- **B4 — "Spearman over levels, all p ≥ 0.23" does not reproduce.**

    - **Issue.** No committed code computes it. Recomputed from the promoted shallow report, the smallest p is 0.017 (JE
        `linear_acc_daytime`) and several sit at 0.083, out of about 50 tests. That is still consistent with no
        familiarity trend, so the conclusion stands.
    - **Remediation.** `[analysis]` + `[engineering]` Commit the computation, and quote its minimum with the multiplicity.
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

- **B5 — The warm-up dip is attributed to continued SSL; most of it was fresh heads and an undersized decoder.**

    - **Issue.** P1.1.0's Insights say a pretrained backbone "first loses competence under continued SSL and then levels
        off". The MAE checkpoint is encoder-only, so the decoder and JE's heads start random at the well. P1.2.0 then
        removed JE's dip and half of MAE's by settling the heads first, and P1.2.2 traced the rest to the 64-d × 2
        decoder. The doc carries this only as a parenthetical in the setup. Three inline review comments in P1.1.0 raised
        the questions, and are carried here and removed from the doc: does the shallow null's "healthy movement" come from
        a decoder learned from scratch; is the Insight's dip the random-decoder phase; and why read loss and grad norm
        from max |*d*|. The answers: at *drive* start nothing is fresh (the well saves and reloads the whole method,
        verified key by key); at *well* start the heads are. The shallow null's movement is the from-scratch tiny ViT
        still settling as a whole, not fresh heads. One residue: the MAE well stopped on competence while its loss was
        still falling (0.70 → 0.64), so its decoder may still co-adapt in the drive. And "read loss and grad norm from max
        |*d*|" is because the last checkpoint follows a 3-image batch; both are unchanged with the short-batch checkpoints
        removed.
    - **Remediation.** `[wording]` Rewrite the Insight to name the fresh heads and the decoder, add the drive-start answer
        and the decoder residue, say beside the shallow null that its movement was the tiny ViT settling, not fresh heads,
        and add the one-clause reason for max |*d*|.
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

- **B6 — The final-norm "~7× compression" is stated as mechanism.**

    - **Issue.** P1.1.3 says the top block's movement swamps the difference and "the final norm then compresses that
        movement ~7×". `block12` is the pre-norm residual stream, where ViT-B carries a few very large outlier dimensions,
        and plain CKA weights by variance. Part of the "compression" may be the norm removing those dimensions. The
        empirical finding (the output cannot separate the arms) stands; the mechanism is untested.
    - **Remediation.** `[wording]` State it as an observation, or `[analysis]` check it by reading `block12` with the
        outlier dimensions removed.
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

- **B7 — Doc accuracy: legs, step 64, overlap, counts and read sets.**

    - **Issue.**
        - Under P1.1's reservation `dawn/dusk·foggy` keeps one training frame. It streams as a 1-frame batch at step 64 (73
            in the shallow sweep), which the loop skips. So the walk delivers 18 legs and trains on 17, and the last short
            batch is 1 frame, not "2–3". P1.1.3's schedule should read dawn/dusk 56–64. `step00064.pt` and `step00065.pt`
            hold the same weights.
        - The skipped batch drops a different frame in each order: walk and IID train on sets differing by 1 of 3,522
            frames.
        - At overlap 1 the well is 99.9% drive frames, not 100% (the run reports record the realized value).
        - P1.1.2's `daytime_end` (`step00055`) precedes the last 3-frame daytime leg.
        - P1.1.2 says CKA drift fired on "three" canary-quiet arms, all σ0.5 IID. The backbone read fires on 4; the fourth
            is σ0.3 IID seed 1 (*z* 5.47, a 3-point dent). "Three" is true of the per-block panel.
        - The monitor's `cka_drift` reads the 405 query images; the lens `cka_drift@backbone` reads 810 support + query. The
            docs do not say which they quote.
        - The leg checkpoints are said to sit "on the Gaudi node's local disk" (P1.1.0, P1.1.1). `outputs/p11*` now link to
            the project share. The promoted configs' `state_dict_path` needs an override to re-run.
    - **Remediation.** `[wording]` Correct each in the detail docs; none moves a verdict.
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

## C. Internal tensions

- **C1 — P1.1's MAE carried the small decoder throughout, and only P1.1.0 says so.**

    - **Issue.** P1.2.2 found that the 64-d × 2 decoder pushes ViT-B MAE's last blocks toward pixel detail. It then listed
        the P1.1 readings that rest on that decoder, none re-run with the repaired one:

        - P1.1.0's "MAE resists, again";
        - the canary's measured power on MAE (P1.1.1);
        - the MAE screens of the label-free readers (P1.1.2);
        - P1.1.3's middle-block alarm, whose blocks 4–11 overlap the damaged blocks 9–12.

        P1.1.0 carries the caveat. P1.1.1–P1.1.3 and the P1.1 summary do not. It also undercuts the anchor argument. P1.1.2
        justifies CKA drift by "the well is certified competent, so far from the well means far from good". But in the
        warm scout, MAE's linear read fell from 0.63 at the frozen start to 0.55 settled, so the MAE anchor is a mildly
        damaged model. The screen itself is unaffected: damage relative to the well is still damage. And the re-init-4
        "specificity control" on MAE is not neutral (E1).

    - **Remediation.** `[wording]` Carry P1.2.2's list into P1.1.1–P1.1.3 and the summary, as natural rewording with a
        pointer ("most likely", since nothing was re-run). Qualify the anchor claim: competent relative to the canary, not
        to its own frozen start. `[new substudy]` P1.1.4 re-validates the MAE readers on the repaired recipe, with drift
        anchored at the settled-head, large-decoder start.

    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

- **C2 — The well's competence stop watched kNN only.**

    - **Issue.** The stop reads selection-set kNN. On MAE, kNN held flat through the warm-up (0.53 → 0.53) while linear
        fell (0.63 → 0.55). So "settled" meant settled in geometry, not in information — the distinction P1.2 later made a
        rule ("read geometry and information separately"). A drive started mid-slide on linear would inherit the slide,
        which is the failure the stop exists to prevent.
    - **Remediation.** `[wording]` Note in P1.1.0 which read the stop watched, and what it missed on MAE.
        `[defer → Phase-1]` A competence stop in P1.3 judges on the linear and fine-tune rungs and holds kNN as a
        constraint, as P1.2.5's handover recommends for recipe choice.
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

- **C3 — "Benign specialization" rests partly on a canary P1.1.1 shows is weak on collapse.**

    - **Issue.** P1.1.1 calls JE's walk compression benign on three legs: it reverts within 10 IID steps, backbone RankMe
        stays flat, and the canary does not move. The first two hold. The third is weak evidence, because the same
        sub-study shows a 2.7× projector collapse costs this canary only 2–5 points. Later evidence also moved: over a
        full pass the walk costs JE's kNN against IID at night (P1.2.1), and kNN erodes late in every SimSiam-objective
        drive (P1.2.5). That is a longer horizon, so it does not contradict P1.1.1, but "benign" is a 119-step verdict.
        And "benign" was judged without a fine-tune read. P1.2.5's Part G later found a kNN loss that was not benign: the
        rate that cost kNN cost fine-tuning too.
    - **Remediation.** `[wording]` Scope the verdict to the horizon and to the reversion and backbone-rank evidence, with
        a pointer to P1.2.1 / P1.2.5's kNN erosion, and say no fine-tune rung was read. `[new substudy]` P1.1.4 splits the
        canary into information (linear) and geometry (kNN) panels, and counts a kNN fire as degradation only with a
        fine-tune drop beside it.
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

- **C4 — The backbone-RankMe discriminator now has two knobs it calls benign while the projector collapses.**

    - **Issue.** P1.1.1 proposes "projector RankMe with backbone RankMe" as the collapse reader, on 3 seeds of one knob.
        Its own stop-gradient knob (1 seed) collapsed the projector without lowering backbone RankMe. P1.2.5's Part E adds
        a second case: DINO with centring off collapses the projector (RankMe 23.4 against its unswitched twin's 53.4 at
        300 steps) while backbone RankMe stays put. Read P1.1.1's way, sharpening off fires and centring off does not. Bar
        E also asked the canary to fire on the collapse control, which P1.1.1 had already shown a 3-class canary will not.
        P1.1's handover did not say so outright.
    - **Remediation.** `[wording]` In P1.1.1's handover: the discriminator is untested on objectives other than SimSiam,
        has two non-firing knobs, and the canary is not a collapse arbiter. `[defer → Phase-1]` P1.4 tests it across knobs
        and objectives. If DINO-init DINO joins P1.3, P1.1.4's read of its natural collapse is the first such test (D5).
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

## D. Structural gaps

- **D1 — Nothing P1.1 calibrated transfers to P1.3's setup as is.**

    - **Issue.** The bands, panel thresholds, MDDs, restart-jolt size and the middle-block panel were all measured on one
        setup: BDD *val*, a 119-step drive, warm wells with fresh-at-well heads, MAE's 64-d × 2 decoder, no fine-tune
        protections, lr 3e-5 / 1e-4, MAE-init only. P1.3 runs BDD *train*, passes of ~1,750 steps or more, settled heads,
        the large decoder, the protections, and a DINO start. It will also retune its recipes (P1.2.5's handover), so even
        numbers fitted on today's recipes will not carry over unchanged. The summary's "the null and the single-drive
        rule, at about one false alarm in ten" reads as portable.
    - **Remediation.** `[wording]` State in the P1.1 summary and handovers that the *method* transfers and the *numbers*
        do not. `[new substudy]` P1.1.4 recalibrates the bands, thresholds and MDDs on P1.3's setup, per arm, on the
        handover recipes. A retuned recipe's bands are re-fitted by the same procedure on P1.3's own IID controls.
        P1.2.5's drives logged the full vector, so a first pass needs no new runs.
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

- **D2 — The healthy bands are untested on a DINO start.**

    - **Issue.** DINO-init SimSiam is one of P1.3's three equal arms, and one of the two on which it reads forgetting
        (P1.2.5). P1.1's bands and its JE readers were all calibrated on the MAE-started hybrid. A DINO start has a
        different geometry and objective history, so the bands cannot be assumed. P1.2.5 lists this as a P1.3 read.
    - **Remediation.** `[new substudy]` P1.1.4, with D1: the bands read on the DINO-init SimSiam arm, and a JE damage of
        known size built on that start (P1.1.1's surgical branches and the specificity control).
    - **Conclusion.** *Pending.*

- **D3 — The canary resolves coarser losses than the forgetting P1.3 needs to catch.**

    - **Issue.** On one drive the canary calls a 6–8-point dent and misses ~3. Across a 5-seed ensemble it resolves ~2–4.5
        points pooled, for sustained gaps (B2), and 3.6–7.5 per time-of-day stratum. Two quantities need keeping apart
        here.

        - *The size of the gain.* P1.2's gains so far are ~2–4.4 points of linear accuracy, but from one pass over a fixed
            domain, read pooled. P1.3's longer, shifting horizon may well grow them; the fine-tune ceilings leave ~10 points
            of headroom. The one-pass curve does not yet show growth on scene (it peaks mid-drive), so growth is an open
            P1.3 question, not an established lower bound.
        - *The size of the loss the monitor must catch.* This does not scale with the gain. Forgetting starts small and
            shows first on a past regime, so it must be caught early and per stratum, which is where the canary is weakest.
            And where P1.2's gains do grow (traffic light, small objects), the 3-class scene canary does not read them at
            all; its own headroom is capped near the fine-tune ceiling (A3).

        A first sign of this on P1.3's setup: P1.2.5's val referee finds the walk costing DINO-init SimSiam scene kNN
        against its IID control, a cost the in-drive canary barely registers on the same drives. Whether that is the
