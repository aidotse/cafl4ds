## P1.1.1

From [the reading-protocol sub-study](phase1/P1.1.1.md) — the second P1.1 piece. P1.1.0 measured how much a healthy
drive moves the health instruments. P1.1.1 turns that into a rule a monitor can apply to one drive: read each instrument
against bands drawn from healthy drives, and call an excursion only when it leaves them in the harmful direction. The
labelled canary decides whether an excursion lost information. The question it inherits is whether the joint-embedding
(JE) backbone's correlation-driven compression is early collapse or harmless reorganization.

**The approach.** The rule is validated in both directions. It must stay quiet on fresh healthy drives, and it must fire
on damage of known size. The damage is surgical — noise or re-initialization in the encoder's blocks, at graded doses,
injected mid-drive into both the correlated walk and the shuffled control — on both backbones. A second control dials in
real collapse on the same pretrained JE backbone. A third runs a short pass of mixed data after each drive, to see
whether the walk's compression undoes.

**What it found.**

- **The rule works, with a modest false-alarm rate.** Its threshold is read off the healthy drives themselves. On drives
    it never saw, it raises a false alarm on roughly one drive in ten per panel. That is about the best a handful of
    healthy seeds can certify.
- **The canary has a measured power curve on both backbones.** It catches a mid-sized accuracy dent at once and misses a
    small one. Damage reads the same on the walk as on the control, and it does not heal within the drive. MAE now has a
    degradation of known size — the screen P1.1.2's label-free MAE reader search needs.
- **JE's compression is benign specialization.** It reverts on its own when the diet mixes. Under a short mixed pass it
    undoes almost at once, with no accuracy cost, while a healthy model given the same pass barely moves.
- **Real collapse looks different, but only one level down.** Forced collapse compresses the projector about as much as
    the walk does, and costs the canary surprisingly little. What separates the two is the backbone's rank: it falls
    under collapse and stays flat under the walk. That is a candidate rule for reading JE collapse, not yet a proven
    one.
- **An unplanned finding.** At a higher learning rate, the walk itself starts losing canary accuracy, about twice what
    the control loses on average. Per seed, that excess rests mostly on one of three ([P1.1.3](phase1/P1.1.3.md)). That
    may be the natural JE degradation P1.4 is looking for, so it is handed there.

**Status: ✅ complete.** The false-alarm level of about one in ten per panel is accepted. The higher-lr walk degradation
is handed to P1.4, and the damage branches feed P1.1.2's MAE reader search.
