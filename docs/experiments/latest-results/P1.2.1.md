## P1.2.1

From [the gate read](../phase1/P1.2.1.md) — the second P1.2 piece, and the one that carries leg (a) of the Phase-1 exit
gate. The project assumes a backbone living on a stream should keep learning from it. P1.2.1 tests that directly: a
pretrained encoder trains on one full pass of driving frames it has never seen, and is compared with the same encoder
left frozen (**B5**).

**The approach.** Both backbones start from the identical off-the-shelf MAE-pretrained encoder, so the joint-embedding
backbone (JE) is a hybrid: MAE's weights, continued with a joint-embedding objective. Each backbone's training heads
were settled beforehand with the encoder frozen, so at step 0 each *is* B5, and any difference at the end comes from
adaptation alone. Each backbone drives the correlated walk with 5 seeds, beside a shuffled control. The adapted encoders
are then scored on the untouched BDD val split, on two tasks (the scene, and whether a traffic light is present among
frames matched for scene and time of day) and at every label budget. The pass bars were fixed before any data was read.

**What it found.**

- **The joint-embedding backbone passes.** Its features carry more linearly decodable information than the frozen
    encoder's on both tasks, by margins well outside the noise. The gain builds over the first half of the drive and
    holds.
- **The pass is real but not uniform.** It holds on every seed. But it rests on the linear probe, the read a switch to a
    joint-embedding objective favours most. Under fine-tuning, the adapted encoder is a better start on the
    traffic-light task and a slightly worse one on the scene task. The nearest-neighbour read is flat overall.
    [P1.2.4](P1.2.4.md) later found the gain specific to the driving data, not the objective switch alone.
- **MAE fails, with this recipe.** It reads below the frozen encoder on every measure of both tasks. Most of the loss
    arrives in the first hundred steps, which points at the training recipe more than at the data.
- **Correlation reshapes the joint-embedding backbone's geometry, not its information.** The correlated walk costs its
    nearest-neighbour accuracy at night, against the shuffled control, while the linear gain holds. That matters for the
    project's selection filter, which works on embedding distances. It is also a forgetting lead for P1.3. A gentler
    recipe ([P1.2.4](P1.2.4.md)) removes the night-time loss against the frozen encoder. It does not remove the walk's
    cost against the shuffled control, which reappears in [P1.2.5](P1.2.5.md)'s repeat. P1.2.5 also finds the
    nearest-neighbour read eroding late in every such drive, in daytime and night frames alike, so the lead looks more
    like general drift than forgetting of one regime.
- **What it means.** Leg (a) passes, on the joint-embedding backbone only. One limit carries over: the evaluation frames
    share the training frames' distribution, so this tests adapting to a new but fixed domain, not a stream that keeps
    shifting.
- **What followed.** MAE's loss traced to a too-small decoder; repaired, MAE matches the frozen encoder without beating
    it ([P1.2.2](P1.2.2.md)). JE's gain proved specific to driving data and grew under a gentler recipe
    ([P1.2.4](P1.2.4.md)). A repeat under changed conditions kept the linear gain, but not its larger size or the
    nearest-neighbour gain ([P1.2.5](P1.2.5.md)). P1.3 carries several backbones as equal arms — DINO's weights
    continued with joint-embedding training, this MAE-started hybrid, and MAE — and chooses among them only after a
    longer horizon.
