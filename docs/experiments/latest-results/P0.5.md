## The value proposition

Phase 0's job is to build thermometers for a self-supervised model's health that work without labels — because in
deployment (Phase 1+) there's no answer key to check the model against. This study chases the subtlest failure: **quiet
degradation**, where the training loss looks perfectly healthy while the representation has actually gone bad. It is the
direct test of the project's nightmare — *"the loss lies."*

The setting is **MAE** (learns by filling in masked-out patches of an image). The worry: an MAE can find a **shortcut**
— copy neighbouring pixels, interpolate — that drives the reconstruction loss down beautifully while learning nothing
semantically useful. The two arms differ by one shortcut lever (too little masking, or an over-powered decoder that does
the semantic work so the encoder does not have to); the shortcut arm's frozen probe must crater while its loss stays
healthy-looking.

The deeper question this study puts on trial: the geometry suite from the collapse study (P0.2). Does it double as a
*quality* reader on MAE, or does it have a blind spot — a representation that is "full-rank but useless", using all its
dimensions yet carrying no useful signal?

## Results

**Two robust negatives — which is what the ✅ actually rests on.** First, *MAE resists the shortcut*: a strong MAE barely
craters its probe, and the loss *decouples* from quality (the degraded arm's loss is even *lower*) — confirming "the
loss lies", but the damage is shallow. Second, and more useful as a warning: *the P0.2 geometry suite is a trap on MAE*.
RankMe is blind by construction, and uniformity / clusterability actively *mis-signal* — they say the degraded
representation looks *healthier*. A first-class "do not trust geometry readers on MAE" result.

**The one candidate reader — and why it fails.** The single label-free signal that fired two-sided (5/5) was alignment
under a stronger augmentation, on a from-scratch over-powered-decoder degradation (P0.5.2). But a follow-up dose-down
(P0.5.3) — weakening the decoder step by step — showed it tracks the *decoder machinery*, not quality: it fires even
when the shortcut arm's probe *matches or beats* healthy (no quality lost at all), and it stays silent on a genuine
crater made by the *other* lever. It is a decoder-regime detector, a confounder — not a quality reader.

**One structural fact, confirmed twice.** MAE optimizes pixel reconstruction, so representation quality is an *emergent
byproduct* it never directly trains for — and *no internal MAE signal reads quality label-free*: the loss decouples, the
geometry mis-signals, and invariance (alignment) is not trained, so under a strong augment it reads mechanism, not
quality. This is the same wall the forgetting arc (P0.3) hit from the other side. One finding, twice.

The upshot for Phase 1. Label-free *quality* monitoring on an MAE backbone is not available from its internals — it
needs a labelled canary probe, or a backbone whose objective ties geometry to quality (a joint-embedding projector,
which is exactly the P0.6 test). MAE's *loud* failures stay readable (divergence via the grad norm, collapse via
RankMe); it is *quality specifically* that its internals cannot see.

## The one-line takeaway

On an MAE backbone, representation *quality* cannot be read label-free from any internal signal — the loss lies, the
collapse-geometry suite is a trap (it mis-signals), and the one candidate reader turns out to track the degrade-lever's
machinery rather than quality — so the ✅ rests on those two robust negatives, and real label-free quality monitoring
needs a labelled canary or a joint-embedding backbone (P0.5 ✅\*).
