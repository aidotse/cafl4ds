# P1.1 — learning to read degradation

Study-level summary of the P1.1 group (earn the right to read the loop): [P1.1.0](P1.1.0.md) the healthy-movement null,
[P1.1.1](P1.1.1.md) the reading protocol, [P1.1.2](P1.1.2.md) the label-free lens screen, [P1.1.3](P1.1.3.md) the CKA
drift deep dive, and [P1.1.4](P1.1.4.md) the same tools rebuilt on P1.3's setup. Detail lives in the substudy
distillates linked here and, one level down, in [`phase1/P1.1.{0,1,2,3,4}.md`](../phase1/index.md).

## The value proposition

Phase 1 has to show that a self-supervised backbone, trained on a correlated stream of driving images, gets *worse*. The
catch is that every training run moves the health instruments, healthy or not. A needle that moved is not evidence. So
before P1.3 and P1.4 can claim degradation, three things have to exist.

- **A null.** How much does a healthy run move each instrument? Without it, any reading is uninterpretable.
- **A rule for one run, with a referee.** A deployed monitor sees one drive, not a seed ensemble. It needs a rule to
    call an excursion, and a labelled accuracy channel (the **canary**) to say whether information was really lost —
    with a known power, so "the canary saw nothing" means something.
- **A label-free reader for MAE.** MAE was then Phase 1's primary backbone, and Phase 0 left it with no internal signal
    that reads its quality. A deployment has no labels, so without such a reader MAE degradation can only be seen
    afterwards, never as it happens.

Both backbones start from the same MAE-pretrained ViT-B. MAE continues it with its own objective; the joint-embedding
backbone (JE) continues it with SimSiam, which makes JE a hybrid. The start was a practical choice: it keeps the
pipeline label-free and gives both one shared start. A pure joint-embedding start (DINO) is tested later, in P1.2.5.

Why this matters: P1.1 is what turns P1.3's and P1.4's runs into evidence. It is the difference between "the instruments
moved" and "the backbone degraded".

## Results

- **P1.1.0 — the null: a healthy drive is quiet, once it starts from a settled model.** The first attempt started from a
    small model that turned out no better than random weights, and its "healthy movement" was the model still settling.
    From a pretrained ViT-B warmed until its accuracy stops moving, a healthy drive barely moves anything. Against that
    null, correlation compresses the joint-embedding (JE) backbone's representation with no accuracy cost, and leaves
    MAE essentially untouched at this horizon. The canary would have caught a loss of a few points held over the drive;
    one that shows only at the end must be larger.
- **P1.1.1 — the rule works, and JE's compression is benign over a short drive.** Read against healthy bands, a drive
    raises a false alarm about one time in ten per panel, a loose figure from fifteen healthy drives. Surgical damage of
    known size gives the canary a power curve: it catches a dent of ~6–8 points at once and misses ~3, on both
    backbones, on the correlated walk and the shuffled control alike. JE's compression undoes within ten mixed-data
    steps at no accuracy cost, so it is specialization, not collapse, at this horizon; over a full pass, later studies
    found the walk does cost JE's nearest-neighbour read. Real collapse, dialled in on the same model, also lowers the
    *backbone's* rank — a candidate way to tell the two apart, though two other ways of collapsing the projector leave
    that rank in place. An unplanned lead: at a higher learning rate the walk itself costs JE accuracy, though the
    walk-specific part rests on one of three seeds.
- **P1.1.2 — one label-free MAE signal survives: CKA drift.** Every candidate was screened against the damage, plus a
    control that changes the weights a lot and costs the canary nothing. Phase 0's MAE candidates fail again, now for a
    named reason: they miss the damage, or they fire on the control and so read the weights, not the information. Only
    CKA drift — how far the representation's content has moved from the certified starting model — passes both. It is a
    change detector, not a quality reader. And at the encoder's output it also fires on healthy correlated drives.
- **P1.1.3 — natural forgetting is visible, in the middle of the network.** Three ways of making the output read smarter
    all fall short on BDD: reading only the conditions the stream has left behind, calibrating on healthy correlated
    drives, and reading JE's natural higher-lr loss. Re-running Phase 0's one reliable MAE forgetting event with saved
    models, the forgetting run drifts clearly more than its healthy twin in the middle blocks, on every seed. At the
    output, where Phase 0 looked, the two cannot be told apart. Read alone, without a twin, those blocks pass the BDD
    damage screen with a wider margin than the output, and catch most forgetting runs that the output misses entirely (a
    count scored on the runs the blocks were chosen on; an unselected panel catches fewer). The optimizer-restart jolt
    that troubled the output read is brief and maskable.
- **P1.1.4 — the tools, rebuilt where P1.3 runs.** P1.2 moved P1.3 to another setup: longer passes, new recipes, a DINO
    start, and forgetting read on the joint-embedding backbones rather than MAE. So P1.1's method was re-run there, on
    four arms. Every arm's label-side readers hold their false-alarm bars, and a held-out referee, P1.2's readout ladder
    read per driving regime, resolves the past-regime losses P1.3 must catch, given a large enough pool. The in-drive
    canary stays coarser than that referee. A label-free reader passes on MAE and, provisionally, on DINO continued with
    its own objective; on the joint-embedding arms none does, because a healthy walk moves them as far as real damage.

**What P1.1 hands to P1.3 and P1.4.**

- The method: a matched shuffled control, a single-drive rule validated on healthy drives it never saw, and damage of
    known size beside an undamaged twin. P1.1.0–P1.1.3's calibrated numbers do not transfer; P1.1.4 re-fitted them on
    P1.3's setup, and P1.3 re-fits them again on its retuned recipes.
- The referee: P1.2's readout ladder, read per regime at every checkpoint, with the pool sizes P1.1.4 worked out. The
    canary stays as a cheap, live, coarse alarm, not the judge. P1.3 sharpens it before its stream, with richer tasks
    and a continuous score, and keeps the current one beside it as an ablation.
- For MAE, now P1.3's plasticity arm: CKA drift read in the middle blocks, as a comparison against a matched control,
    and as the candidate single-drive alarm. P1.3's runs need per-block features for it, and must mask the two readings
    after any optimizer reset.
- For the joint-embedding arms, where P1.3 reads forgetting: labels only, for now. Collapse is read on the projector's
    rank together with the backbone's, as a candidate; the higher-lr walk is P1.4's lead, to be read per seed.

**The limits.** P1.1.0–P1.1.3's drives are short — about one pass over a few thousand frames — and every MAE reading
there carries the undersized decoder P1.2.2 later found was damaging MAE's top blocks. The readers were screened on
surgical damage, on one shallow, recoverable forgetting event from Phase 0, and in P1.1.4 on one natural MAE failure on
BDD that a recipe change confounds. No label-free reader yet has a specificity check that survived.

## The one-line takeaway

We can now read a drive, on the setup P1.3 runs: a quiet null, a single-drive rule, a held-out referee sized to the
losses that matter, and a label-free MAE reader that sees natural forgetting once read in the middle blocks — but for
the joint-embedding backbones, where P1.3 reads forgetting, only labels can call it yet.
