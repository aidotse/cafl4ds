# P1.1 — learning to read degradation

Study-level summary of the P1.1 group (earn the right to read the loop): [P1.1.0](P1.1.0.md) the healthy-movement null,
[P1.1.1](P1.1.1.md) the reading protocol, [P1.1.2](P1.1.2.md) the label-free lens screen, [P1.1.3](P1.1.3.md) the CKA
drift deep dive, and [P1.1.4](P1.1.4.md) the same tools rebuilt on P1.3's setup. Detail lives in the substudy
distillates linked here and, one level down, in [`phase1/P1.1.{0,1,2,3,4}.md`](../phase1/index.md).

## The value proposition

Phase 1 has to show that a self-supervised backbone, trained on a correlated stream of driving images, gets *worse*. The
catch is that every training run moves the health instruments, healthy or not. A needle that moved is not evidence. So
before P1.3 and P1.4 can claim degradation, three things have to exist.

- **A null.** How much does a healthy run move each instrument? Without it, any reading is uninterpretable.
- **A rule for one run, with a referee.** A deployed monitor sees one drive, not a seed ensemble. It needs a rule to
    call an excursion, and a labelled accuracy channel to say whether information was really lost, with a known power.
- **A label-free reader.** A deployment has no labels, so without one, degradation is seen only afterwards.

Both backbones start from the same MAE-pretrained ViT-B. MAE continues it with its own objective; the joint-embedding
backbone (JE) continues it with SimSiam, which makes JE a hybrid. A pure joint-embedding start (DINO) came later, in
P1.2.5. Why this matters: P1.1 is the difference between "the instruments moved" and "the backbone degraded".

## Results

- **P1.1.0 — the null: a healthy drive is quiet, once it starts from a settled model.** A first, small model was still
    settling, and its "healthy movement" was that. From a pretrained ViT-B warmed until its accuracy stops moving, a
    healthy drive barely moves anything. Against that null, correlation compresses JE's representation at no accuracy
    cost and leaves MAE essentially untouched at this horizon.
- **P1.1.1 — the rule works, and JE's compression is benign over a short drive.** A drive raises a false alarm about one
    time in ten per panel, a loose figure from fifteen healthy drives. The labelled canary catches a dent of ~6–8 points
    at once and mostly misses ~3; sizes in between were not tested. JE's compression undoes within ten mixed-data steps,
    so it is specialization, not collapse, at this horizon; over a full pass, later studies found the walk does cost
    JE's nearest-neighbour read. Forced collapse also lowered the *backbone's* rank, but two other ways of collapsing
    the projector leave it in place, so projector and backbone are read as separate questions. A lead: at a higher
    learning rate the walk itself costs JE accuracy, though the walk-specific part rests on one of three seeds.
- **P1.1.2 — one label-free MAE signal survives: CKA drift.** Every candidate was screened against damage of known size
    and against a control that moves the weights at no accuracy cost. Only CKA drift — how far the representation has
    moved from a certified start — passes both. It detects change, not quality, and at the output it also fires on
    healthy correlated drives.
- **P1.1.3 — natural forgetting is visible, in the middle of the network.** Three ways of reading the output smarter
    fall short. But re-running Phase 0's one reliable MAE forgetting event with saved models, the forgetting run drifts
    more than its healthy twin in the middle blocks, on every seed, where the output cannot tell them apart. Read alone,
    those blocks catch most forgetting runs, also when chosen without the run being scored, though on only five pairs.
- **P1.1.4 — the tools, rebuilt where P1.3 runs.** P1.2 moved P1.3 to longer passes, new recipes, a DINO start, and
    forgetting read on the JE backbones. So P1.1's method was re-run there, on four arms. Every arm's label-side readers
    hold their false-alarm bars. A label-free reader passes on MAE and, provisionally, on DINO continued with its own
    objective. On the JE arms none does, because a healthy walk moves them as far as real damage.

**Two label-side instruments, two roles.** The *referee* is P1.2's readout ladder (nearest-neighbour, linear probe and
short fine-tunes on held-out frames), read per driving regime on every saved checkpoint. It is expensive, and it judges
every degradation claim. The *canary* is a small labelled probe read live inside the drive: cheap, coarse, an alarm.
Because the referee judges, the canary needs only a modest upgrade: P1.3 adds a continuous score and the traffic-light
task before its stream, and logs the current canary beside it as an ablation.

**The gate P1.3 inherits.** P1.3 reads degradation on an arm, any of its four, only once both conditions hold:

1. The arm's label-side panels pass P1.1.4's false-alarm bars on the recipe P1.3 actually reads, re-fitted after
    retuning on its own shuffled controls (10 calibration seeds, 5 fresh).
1. The arm's per-regime and breadth held-out pools are sized from P1.1.4's power reads, and the 2-point past-regime
    target is pre-registered.

A label-free reader is not part of the gate, so forgetting on the JE arms is read through labels only.

**Where this leaves Phase 1: everything sits at the edge of resolution.** Adapting gains about 2–4 points over the
frozen start (P1.2). The first natural forgetting the referee confirms on P1.3's setup is smaller: once daytime is
behind the stream, the MAE-started hybrid's walk sits 1.4–1.8 points below its shuffled control on daytime scenes. And
the referee resolves about 2 points, on its best rung and largest slices. So after one pass, gain, loss and resolution
are the same size, and one pass cannot separate a real loss from noise. Pulling them apart is P1.3's job, through a
longer horizon, dense checkpoints, and held-out pools sized to the target in advance.

**What else P1.3, P1.4 and P1.6 inherit.**

- *Calibrations bind their setup.* P1.1.0–P1.1.3's numbers did not survive P1.2's change of recipe, start and horizon.
    Whenever a later study changes the setup, what it leans on is re-run there, or carried as provisional.
- *MAE (P1.3's plasticity arm):* CKA drift in the middle blocks, read against a matched control and as the candidate
    single-drive alarm. P1.3 scores blocks 4–11 out of sample on its drives, from per-block features, masking two
    readings after any optimizer reset.
- *JE collapse (P1.4):* the projector suite calls collapse; backbone rank is read beside it (has it reached the
    encoder?). P1.4 runs the collapse knobs as an ablation on P1.3's JE arms, against the referee, and re-tests the
    higher-lr walk per seed.
- *The label-free channel (P1.6):* a JE reader scored against the walk's own healthy spread rather than the shuffled
    control, and a specificity anchor for MAE's, both prototyped on P1.3's checkpoints.

**The limits.** P1.1.0–P1.1.3's drives are short, and every MAE reading there carries the undersized decoder P1.2.2
later found. The readers were screened on surgical damage, one shallow Phase-0 forgetting event, and one natural MAE
failure on BDD that a recipe change confounds. No label-free reader yet has a specificity check that survived.

## The one-line takeaway

We can now read a drive, on the setup P1.3 runs: a quiet null, a single-drive rule, a held-out referee sized to the
losses that matter, and a label-free MAE reader that sees natural forgetting in the middle blocks — but for the
joint-embedding backbones, where P1.3 reads forgetting, only labels can call it yet.
