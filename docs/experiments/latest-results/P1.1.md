# P1.1 — learning to read degradation

Study-level summary of the P1.1 group (earn the right to read the loop): [P1.1.0](P1.1.0.md) the healthy-movement null,
[P1.1.1](P1.1.1.md) the reading protocol, [P1.1.2](P1.1.2.md) the label-free lens screen, [P1.1.3](P1.1.3.md) the CKA
drift deep dive. Detail lives in the substudy distillates linked here and, one level down, in
[`phase1/P1.1.{0,1,2,3}.md`](../phase1/index.md).

## The value proposition

Phase 1 has to show that a self-supervised backbone, trained on a correlated stream of driving images, gets *worse*. The
catch is that every training run moves the health instruments, healthy or not. A needle that moved is not evidence. So
before P1.3 and P1.4 can claim degradation, three things have to exist.

- **A null.** How much does a healthy run move each instrument? Without it, any reading is uninterpretable.
- **A rule for one run, with a referee.** A deployed monitor sees one drive, not a seed ensemble. It needs a rule to
    call an excursion, and a labelled accuracy channel (the **canary**) to say whether information was really lost —
    with a known power, so "the canary saw nothing" means something.
- **A label-free reader for MAE.** MAE is Phase 1's primary backbone, and Phase 0 left it with no internal signal that
    reads its quality. A deployment has no labels, so without such a reader MAE degradation can only be seen afterwards,
    never as it happens.

Why this matters: P1.1 is what turns P1.3's and P1.4's runs into evidence. It is the difference between "the instruments
moved" and "the backbone degraded".

## Results

- **P1.1.0 — the null: a healthy drive is quiet, once it starts from a settled model.** The first attempt started from a
    small model that turned out no better than random weights, and its "healthy movement" was the model still settling.
    From a pretrained ViT-B warmed until its accuracy stops moving, a healthy drive barely moves anything. Against that
    null, correlation compresses the joint-embedding (JE) backbone's representation with no accuracy cost, and leaves
    MAE essentially untouched at this horizon. The canary would have caught a loss of a few points.
- **P1.1.1 — the rule works, and JE's compression is benign.** Read against healthy bands, a drive raises a false alarm
    about one time in ten per panel. Surgical damage of known size gives the canary a power curve: it catches a dent of
    ~6–8 points at once and misses ~3, on both backbones, on the correlated walk and the shuffled control alike. JE's
    compression undoes within ten mixed-data steps at no accuracy cost, so it is specialization, not collapse. Real
    collapse, dialled in on the same model, also lowers the *backbone's* rank — a candidate way to tell the two apart.
    An unplanned lead: at a higher learning rate the walk itself costs JE accuracy, though the walk-specific part rests
    on one of three seeds.
- **P1.1.2 — one label-free MAE signal survives: CKA drift.** Every candidate was screened against the damage, plus a
    control that changes the weights a lot and costs the canary nothing. Phase 0's MAE candidates fail again, now for a
    named reason: they miss the damage, or they fire on the control and so read the weights, not the information. Only
    CKA drift — how far the representation's content has moved from the certified starting model — passes both. It is a
    change detector, not a quality reader. And at the encoder's output it also fires on healthy correlated drives.
- **P1.1.3 — natural forgetting is visible, in the middle of the network.** Three ways of making the output read smarter
    all fall short on BDD: reading only the conditions the stream has left behind, calibrating on healthy correlated
    drives, and reading JE's natural higher-lr loss. Re-running Phase 0's one reliable MAE forgetting event with saved
    models, the forgetting run drifts clearly more than its healthy twin in the middle blocks, on every seed. At the
    output, where Phase 0 looked, the two cannot be told apart. Read alone, without a twin, those blocks pass the BDD
    damage screen with a wider margin than the output, and catch most forgetting runs that the output misses entirely.
    The optimizer-restart jolt that troubled the output read is brief and maskable.

**What P1.1 hands to P1.3 and P1.4.**

- The null and the single-drive rule, at about one false alarm in ten per panel.
- The canary as the referee, with its measured power.
- For MAE: CKA drift read in the middle blocks, as a comparison against a matched control now, and as the candidate
    single-drive alarm. P1.3's runs need per-block features for it, and must mask the two readings after any optimizer
    reset.
- For JE: read the projector's rank together with the backbone's; the higher-lr walk is P1.4's lead, to be read per
    seed.

**The limits.** The drives are short — about one pass over a few thousand frames. Every reader has been screened on
surgical damage and on one shallow, recoverable forgetting event from Phase 0. None has yet met a natural failure on
BDD. Producing one is P1.3's research question, and validating the readers on it is P1.3's first obligation.

## The one-line takeaway

We can now read a drive: a quiet null, a single-drive rule at about one false alarm in ten, a canary that sees a ~6–8
point loss, and — for the first time — a label-free MAE reader that sees natural forgetting, once read in the middle
blocks rather than at the output (P1.1 ✅; every reader still awaits its first natural failure on BDD, in P1.3).
