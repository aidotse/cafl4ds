## P1.2.5

From [strong starts, the payoff curve and a wider panel](../phase1/P1.2.5.md) — the last P1.2 piece. Until here, the
evidence that a backbone gains by learning on the stream rested on one start: MAE's pretrained weights, continued with a
joint-embedding objective. That start is weak under the readout P1.2 relies on, which flatters any adaptation. P1.2.5
asks whether adapting the best available start still beats keeping it frozen, and after how much of the stream.

**The approach.** Two backbones start from DINO, the strongest frozen encoder tried: one continues with DINO's own
objective, the other with the joint-embedding objective P1.2 already used. The earlier MAE-started backbone is repeated
beside them. Each is compared with its own starting point, frozen, on the untouched evaluation frames, at checkpoints
along one pass. Four supporting reads: a wider panel of tasks; controls trained on non-driving images; a check that the
health instruments see DINO's known failure; and an exploratory read of the gain by object size and by the stream's
regimes (time of day, weather).

**What it found.**

- **Adapting a strong start pays, but narrowly.** DINO continued with the joint-embedding objective beats frozen DINO by
    a few points on the two main tasks. Non-driving images do not reproduce the gain, so it comes from the driving data.
    But it shows on almost no other task. On the nearest-neighbour read the selection filter uses, it appears early in
    the drive and is gone by its end. By the pre-registered table the claim is "Qualified".
- **DINO's own objective does not suit this data.** Driving frames look so alike that DINO's centring step flattens its
    own training target. Its continued training adds nothing a linear probe reads and costs fine-tuning, so it is not
    carried forward. It is the objective that fails, not the features: frozen DINO stays the best frozen start.
- **The MAE-started backbone passes again, with a smaller gain.** Repeated under slightly changed conditions, its linear
    gain holds but shrinks, and its nearest-neighbour gain repeats only mid-drive. Road-free scenes now reproduce part
    of its gain, so its link to the driving data is weaker than first found ([P1.2.4](P1.2.4.md)). Its gain is the
    broadest of the three, though, and it sits on small objects — pedestrians, trucks, buses smaller than one image
    patch.
- **Within one pass, the gain carries and holds.** Night frames gain before the stream reaches night, and daytime frames
    keep their gain after it leaves. What does erode is the nearest-neighbour read: in every drive with the
    joint-embedding objective it gains in the first half of the pass and gives that back in the second. How often an
    object appears in the stream does not decide whether it gains.
- **What it means.** One pass is too short to pick a backbone. P1.3 runs DINO's joint-embedding backbone, the
    MAE-started one and MAE as equal arms over a longer horizon, and chooses only after; recipe tuning moves there too.
    The one exception is the fine-tuning protections, adopted here without being re-tested: both joint-embedding starts
    are also run without them. It reads every readout at every checkpoint, not only at the drive's end, on this wider
    panel fixed in advance. The scope is still one pass over a fixed domain: the evaluation frames share the training
    frames' distribution.
