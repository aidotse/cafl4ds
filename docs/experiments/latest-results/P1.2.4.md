## P1.2.4

From [attribution and widening](phase1/P1.2.4.md) — the P1.2 piece that tests JE's pass in P1.2.1. There, the
joint-embedding backbone (JE) ended a pass over new driving frames better than its frozen starting encoder. Note that
"JE" here is a hybrid: it starts from the same MAE-pretrained weights as the MAE backbone and continues them with a
joint-embedding objective. Two doubts remained. Could a deployment get the same gain more cheaply, by adapting once on
generic images and then freezing, instead of learning on its stream? And was the gain large enough to be worth
protecting?

**The approach.** Attribution: the same training, matched in start point, recipe, length and image shape, was run on
non-driving images instead. One control used everyday photos with all street content removed (COCO). A stricter one used
outdoor and indoor scenes with roads and driving scenes removed (Places365). If either recovered the whole gain, the
gain could be had offline, once, without the stream. Widening: a handful of recipe changes were tried, the best chosen
on reserved training frames, then confirmed once on the untouched evaluation set. The confirmed recipe was also compared
with two stronger frozen backbones (DINO and an ImageNet-supervised model), not just the original one.

**What it found.**

- **General image understanding is not enough.** A one-off adaptation on generic images cannot replace the domain's own
    data. Everyday photos recover only a small part of the gain. Scenes without roads recover about half, so part of
    what JE learns is scene structure in general. The remainder is significant against both controls by the
    pre-registered test. Against the stricter one it is narrow: it rests on the many evaluation images more than on the
    few training seeds.
- **Part of the edge may be novelty.** The encoder was pretrained on ImageNet, which the two controls resemble far more
    than dashcam frames do. So BDD may help more partly because there is more in it left to learn. That leaves one
    question open: would any data equally new to the model, but unrelated to driving, help as much? It was not tested.
- **A better recipe makes the gain larger and broader.** Keeping the early layers close to their pretrained state lifts
    the linear gain and turns the nearest-neighbour and few-shot readings positive too. It also removes the night-time
    nearest-neighbour loss P1.2.1 found.
- **It beats the strongest frozen backbone on the scene task, not on traffic lights.** Frozen DINO is the best of the
    off-the-shelf encoders. Adapted JE clears it on scene; on traffic lights it lands above DINO, but within noise. On
    nearest-neighbour reads, the stronger frozen backbones remain ahead.
- **What it means.** By the pre-registered table this is the "Strong" claim: adapting on in-domain driving data beats
    the best off-the-shelf backbone tried. In practice the claim is narrower: one readout, one task, one pass over a
    fixed domain, a modest margin, and two recipes stitched together (the controls ran on the older one). Whether
    learning *continuously* beats adapting once can only show when the stream shifts; that is P1.3's question.
