## P1.2.4

From [attribution and widening](phase1/P1.2.4.md) — the P1.2 piece that tests JE's pass in P1.2.1. There, the
joint-embedding backbone (JE) ended a pass over new driving frames better than its frozen starting encoder. Two doubts
remained. JE's training teaches any encoder to ignore image augmentations, which might help on any data, so was the gain
really from the driving stream? And was it large enough to be worth protecting?

**The approach.** Attribution: the same training, matched in start point, recipe, length and image shape, was run on
non-driving images instead. One control used everyday photos with all street content removed (COCO). A stricter one used
outdoor and indoor scenes with roads and driving scenes removed (Places365). If either recovered the whole gain, the
gain would be generic. Widening: a handful of recipe changes were tried, the best chosen on reserved training frames,
then confirmed once on the untouched evaluation set. The confirmed recipe was also compared with two stronger frozen
backbones (DINO and an ImageNet-supervised model), not just the original one.

**What it found.**

- **The gain is from the stream.** Everyday photos recover almost none of it. Scenes without roads recover about half,
    so part of what JE learns is outdoor-scene structure in general. The driving-specific remainder is significant
    against both controls, but narrow against the stricter one.
- **A better recipe makes the gain larger and broader.** Keeping the early layers close to their pretrained state lifts
    the linear gain and turns the nearest-neighbour and few-shot readings positive too. It also removes the night-time
    nearest-neighbour loss P1.2.1 found.
- **It beats the strongest frozen backbone on the scene task, not on traffic lights.** Frozen DINO is the best of the
    off-the-shelf encoders. Adapted JE clears it on scene; on traffic lights it lands above DINO, but within noise. On
    nearest-neighbour reads, the stronger frozen backbones remain ahead.
- **What it means.** By the pre-registered table this is the "Strong" claim: continuous learning on the stream beats the
    best off-the-shelf backbone tried. In practice the claim is narrower: one task, one pass, a modest margin.
