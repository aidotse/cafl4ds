## P1.1.4

From [the reading foundation for P1.3's setup](../phase1/P1.1.4.md) — the fifth P1.1 piece, opened by the
[P1.1 audit](../audits/P1.1.md). P1.1 earned the right to read degradation on a short drive, from MAE-started wells,
with a recipe P1.2 later replaced. P1.3 now runs elsewhere: longer passes, three different starting models including a
DINO one, a repaired MAE recipe, and forgetting read on the joint-embedding backbones rather than on MAE. P1.1's method
carries over to that setup; its calibrated numbers do not.

**The approach.** Rebuild the reading tools on P1.3's own three arms, with P1.1's method unchanged. A fourth is
calibrated beside them: DINO continued with its own objective, which P1.2 set aside on its linear read but which
fine-tunes best of all. It joins P1.3 as a fourth explored arm, and its objective's drift toward collapse needs its own
watch. Four parts, each held to a bar fixed in advance:

- the healthy null and the single-drive bands, per arm, first on existing drives and then on fresh healthy seeds. The
    labelled canary is split in two, following P1.2's lesson: the linear read judges information, and the
    nearest-neighbour read counts as damage only when a fine-tuning read drops beside it;
- damage of known size on the two joint-embedding arms and the DINO one, and a search for a label-free reader that sees
    it there;
- the same on the repaired MAE recipe, plus a natural MAE failure (the old recipe's) to test the middle-block reader
    P1.1.3 left;
- how small a loss on a past driving regime the labelled referee can resolve, and how large a held-out pool that needs.

**Why it matters.** P1.3 reads degradation on an arm only once that arm's readers pass these bars. Without them, a
healthy run could read as damaged and a damaged one as healthy.

**First reads, on existing drives.** The labelled canary inside the drive tracks the held-out referee drive by drive,
and pooled over the day it reads the correlated stream's cost as the referee does, once both weight the times of day
alike. On a driving regime the stream has already left, though, it can read a gain where the referee reads a loss. So
forgetting is read on the referee, per time of day. There, the fine-tuning read resolves the smallest losses. The
healthy spread of the readings depends more on the learning rate than on the starting model, so a retuned recipe has its
bands re-fitted.

**The healthy bands, with fresh drives.** With three times the healthy drives, every arm's readers keep their false
alarms within the bars on drives they never saw. One check had to change on the way. A geometry change counts as damage
only when a fine-tuning read drops beside it, and the first form of that check fired by chance on most healthy drives.
Calibrated like the other panels, it holds its error rate and still sees a natural loss. Two readings for P1.3. Over one
pass, learning online beats the frozen start on some arms and reads, not all. And where the canary saw a walk dip on the
regime still ahead, the referee instead found a loss on the regime behind: the forgetting P1.3 is built to read.

**Damage of known size, and the reader search.** Each arm's healthy drive was branched mid-pass, damaged at four sizes,
and run on, beside an undamaged twin. The referee confirms the damage lands at the sizes designed, and that restarting a
branch costs little. The planned "harmless" control, re-initializing the top blocks, failed on every arm: the referee
found small fine-tuning losses the canary had missed. So those branches are reported but not scored. Of the label-free
signals logged during the drive, none passes on any arm. Read block by block from saved checkpoints, two do better. On
MAE, drift from the start state passes, and the middle-block reader P1.1.3 left also fires on the old recipe's natural
failure, though partly because the recipe changed, and with no surviving control to show that a fire means lost
information. On DINO-init DINO the middle blocks pass too, provisionally: helped by an encoder that goes nearly still
late in the pass, and by a rule settled after the results. On both joint-embedding arms nothing passes: a healthy walk
moves the representation as far from the shuffled control as real damage does.

**What the referee can resolve.** The fine-tuning read sees the smallest losses on a regime the stream has left, within
the target on the largest slices and with a larger pool per weather regime. Breadth at 1 point needs a held-out pool
several times BDD val's, which only a reservation taken from training data before the stream can supply.

**The insight in one line.** P1.3 can read degradation on every arm, through labels judged by the held-out referee; a
label-free alarm exists for MAE and DINO-init DINO, and finding one for the joint-embedding arms passes to the monitor
track, P1.6, which measures them against the walk's own healthy spread instead of the shuffled control.

**Status: ✅\* complete, a qualified success.** P1.3's gate is delivered on all four arms: label-side reads that hold
their false-alarm bars, and a referee whose power sizes the held-out pools. The label-free reader is delivered only in
part. It exists on MAE and DINO-init DINO, each with a caveat, and not on the joint-embedding arms, a question for the
monitor track (P1.6).
