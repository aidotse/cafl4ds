## The value proposition

Phase 0's job is to build thermometers for a self-supervised model's health that work without labels — because in
deployment (Phase 1+) there's no answer key to check the model against. Different failure modes need different
thermometers; this study calibrates the ones for **collapse**.

Collapse is the classic way a self-supervised model can cheat. Some of these models learn by pulling two augmented views
of the same image to the same spot in an internal space. The trivial way to win that game is to send *every* image to
the same spot — the representation "collapses" to a constant and stops carrying information, even though the training
loss looks fine. There are two flavours: **point** collapse (everything piles onto one point) and **redundancy**
collapse (the dimensions become copies of each other, so the space is really lower-dimensional than it looks).

The question: can a small suite of geometry metrics reliably tell a collapsed representation from a healthy one? The
candidates are RankMe (how many dimensions the representation actually uses), off-diagonal covariance (how correlated
the dimensions are), and per-dimension variance. And a second question that turns out to matter more: *where* do we read
them — at the backbone (the main network) or at the projector (a small head bolted on only during training)?

The test rig is the usual two-arm setup: one arm is forced to collapse (a "positive control" — e.g. SimSiam with its
anti-collapse trick switched off), a twin healthy arm is left intact. A good thermometer must fire on the first and stay
quiet on the second.

## Results

**Surface is everything.** The load-bearing finding is *where* you read, not *which* metric you pick. Read at the
**projector**, not the backbone. At the projector the suite works cleanly; at the backbone it works only under narrow
conditions (long training, IID data) and throws false alarms otherwise — so the backbone survives only as a
downstream-relevant reference, never the gate.

**The calibrated suite (P0.2.0–P0.2.4).** At the projector: RankMe is the reliable core — it fires in *every* collapse
corner. Off-diagonal covariance is the *redundancy*-specific detector (loud on redundancy collapse, correctly silent on
point collapse). Per-dimension variance separates the two flavours (craters under point collapse, flat under redundancy)
but is the noisiest, so never a standalone gate. The healthy reference is fixed as "IID data × 40 epochs" — the cheapest
setting that certifies a genuinely healthy arm. One documented false-alarm boundary: RankMe fires spuriously when the
*evaluation* set has very few classes — an eval-set artifact, not an instrument fault, and fixed by matching classes.

**Catching it early (P0.2.5).** Everything above forces collapse *maximally* — but the deployment worry is catching
degradation while it is still mild. A controlled partial-collapse dial shows the projector detectors track intermediate
severity smoothly and monotonically (RankMe slides ~28 → ~21 → ~10 → ~6 → ~4 as the dial turns), so they read the
incipient band, not just the extremes.

One honest caveat. Every threshold here is tuned at toy scale, so the *numbers* won't port to a real backbone — only the
*shape* of the response does. And whether real-scale collapse creeps in as gradually as this toy dial suggests is a
Phase-1 question.

## The one-line takeaway

Collapse is calibrated two-sided against both flavours, and the decisive lesson is to **read the geometry suite at the
projector, not the backbone** — where RankMe is the reliable core and the detectors track even mild, incipient collapse
(P0.2 ✅).
