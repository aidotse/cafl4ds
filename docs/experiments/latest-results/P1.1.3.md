## P1.1.3

From [the CKA drift deep dive](phase1/P1.1.3.md) — the fourth P1.1 piece. P1.1.2 left one label-free signal standing for
the MAE backbone: CKA drift, which measures how far the representation's content has moved from a starting point we
certified as good. It sees surgical damage. But healthy adaptation moves the model too, so it also fired on healthy
correlated drives and on optimizer restarts. And in Phase 0 a natural forgetting event barely moved it. P1.1.3 asks
whether it can tell real information loss from benign movement.

**The approach.** Four reads of existing BDD runs, each held to a pass bar fixed in advance, plus one re-run. The re-run
repeats Phase 0's one reliable natural MAE forgetting event, this time saving the model, so that CKA drift could be read
after every encoder block instead of only at the output.

**What it found.**

- **At the output, the single-drive fixes fail.** Reading drift only on the driving conditions the stream has left
    behind is no better than the ordinary read. Calibrating on healthy correlated drives widens the healthy range until
    real damage falls inside it. And on the joint-embedding backbone's natural, label-confirmed degradation, drift moves
    no more than a harmless change of learning rate moves it.
- **The optimizer-restart jolt is manageable.** On MAE it lasts a reading or two and then fades; on the joint-embedding
    backbone there is none, even at MAE's learning rate. It is large on MAE because MAE's healthy drift is so small. A
    deployment knows when it resets its optimizer, so it can mask the jolt.
- **Natural forgetting does show — in the middle of the network.** The re-run reproduces Phase 0's result exactly. Read
    block by block, the forgetting run drifts clearly more than its healthy twin in the middle blocks, on every seed. At
    the output, where Phase 0 looked, the two are indistinguishable: the top block's shared movement swamps the
    difference. Phase 0's faint signal was a matter of where it read, not of the estimator.
- **The middle blocks also work without a twin.** Read as a single-drive alarm, they pass the BDD damage screen with a
    wider margin than the output, and, unlike it, keep firing on damage until the drive's end. On Phase 0's forgetting
    they catch most forgetting runs without their healthy twin; the output catches none.
- **What stays open.** That forgetting was shallow and quickly recovered, so whether deeper forgetting moves the middle
    blocks more is not settled. And the middle-block alarm has not yet met a natural failure on BDD.

**The insight in one line.** Every attempt to make the output read smarter — better rows, a better null, a natural
target — fell short. Changing *where* in the network it reads is what made natural forgetting visible.

**Status: ✅ complete.** CKA drift, read in the middle blocks, is usable now as a comparative reader and is the candidate
single-drive alarm. P1.3 must validate it on its first natural MAE degradation on BDD, which needs per-block features
from its runs.
