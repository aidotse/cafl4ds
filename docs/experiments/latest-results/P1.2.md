## The value proposition

The project assumes that a backbone living on a data stream should keep learning from it. Everything else it builds —
the selection filter, the health monitor, the controller that re-aims selection — exists to make that continued learning
safe. So the first existential question is blunt. Does continued self-supervised training on the stream actually beat
simply freezing a good off-the-shelf encoder?

Why this matters: if the frozen encoder is as good, a deployment should freeze it and skip the whole loop. The project
would then reframe to *selection for efficiency* rather than continual learning. This is the first of the Phase-1 exit
gate's three tests: does adaptation beat the frozen floor?

The test had to be fair in three ways. The adapted and frozen encoders start from the identical weights, so only
adaptation differs. The comparison reads untouched evaluation frames, at several label budgets, after a correlated drive
— the deployment's own setting. And the gain must not be available more cheaply: not from a one-off adaptation on
generic images, and not from simply freezing a stronger off-the-shelf encoder.

What "gain" means here: always a difference against a frozen encoder, read on the same images, never an improvement over
where training began. The main comparison is the adapted model against its own starting point, frozen; that is what
continued learning adds. A second comparison is against the best frozen encoder available, so that a weak start cannot
flatter the result. The first comparison only works because every start here is a full pretrained model: a random
network gains hugely against itself and can still be far worse than a good frozen one.

One thing to keep in mind when reading the results: both backbones start from the same MAE-pretrained encoder. The
masked-autoencoder backbone (MAE) continues it with its own objective. The "joint-embedding" backbone (JE) continues it
with a joint-embedding objective — a hybrid, not an independent model. That start was a practical choice made in P1.1:
it keeps the pipeline label-free and gives both backbones one shared start. P1.2.5 then adapted an independent, stronger
start: DINO.

## Results

- **P1.2.0 — the tools, and a hidden artifact.** A readout ladder scores an encoder at three label budgets
    (nearest-neighbour, linear probe, short fine-tune), with intervals over seeds and images. Building it exposed an
    artifact: the early dip seen in every earlier continued-training run was mostly caused by randomly initialized
    training heads. From then on, heads are trained first with the encoder frozen. For MAE, half the dip remained.
    P1.2.2 later found a second setup problem, an undersized decoder, which most likely explains that remainder too. A
    second readout task — traffic-light presence among frames matched for scene and time of day — joined the scene
    label, because it has three times the room to improve.
- **P1.2.1 — the gate: JE passes, MAE fails.** After one pass over ~56,000 new driving frames, the joint-embedding
    backbone (JE) carries more linearly decodable information than its frozen start, on both tasks. The
    masked-autoencoder backbone (MAE) reads worse on everything. JE's correlated stream also reshaped its
    nearest-neighbour geometry at night, while the information held.
- **P1.2.2 — MAE's failure was the recipe, but fixing it is not enough.** The decoder used in training was far smaller
    than MAE's own design, and it damaged the encoder's last layers. With the proper decoder and fine-tuning
    protections, MAE stops losing to the frozen encoder on the primary measure, but it does not gain either. Under the
    pre-registered bars that is a FAIL: a No-Go for adapting MAE from its pretrained start, on one pass over a fixed
    domain.
- **P1.2.4 — JE's gain needs the domain's data, and it grows.** General image understanding is not enough: a one-off
    adaptation on matched non-driving images recovers little of the gain (everyday photos) or about half of it (scenes
    without roads). Part of BDD's edge may be that dashcam frames are far from the ImageNet pretraining data; whether
    any equally novel data would do is open. Fine-tuning protections lifted the gain and broadened it to most readouts,
    in one comparison. The adapted encoder beats the best frozen backbone tried (DINO) on the scene task, though not on
    traffic lights.
- **P1.2.5 — a strong start still gains, narrowly; one pass is too short to choose.** DINO's weights, continued with the
    joint-embedding objective, beat frozen DINO by about 2 points on both main tasks, and generic images cannot
    reproduce it. But the gain shows on almost no other task, and on the nearest-neighbour read only in the first half
    of the pass. DINO's own objective fails on this data: the frames are so alike that its centring flattens its
    training target. Repeated, P1.2.4's recipe passes again with a smaller gain (+2.4 to +3.8 points on scene across the
    two runs), and its nearest-neighbour gain replicates only mid-drive; in that repeat, road-free scenes reproduce part
    of its gain. An exploratory read finds its linear gain on small objects, carried across the stream's regimes and not
    given back within the pass, while the nearest-neighbour read gains early and erodes late in every such drive.

**The exit gate's adaptation test passes. By P1.2.5's pre-registered table the claim is "Qualified":** adapting on
in-domain driving data beats a strong frozen start, on some tasks. P1.2.4's "Strong" label rested on a weak start, and
on attribution read under a different recipe than its floor test. Re-read with the confirmed recipe, in P1.2.5's
conditions, that attribution weakens. The honest scope: the linear probe, two tasks plus small objects, about 2 points,
one pass at 224 px, on a fixed domain (the evaluation frames share the training frames' distribution).

**What P1.2 hands to P1.3.**

- **A reframed P1.3, with three equal arms.** The loop exists to protect what adaptation adds, so P1.3 studies the
    backbones that adapt. One pass is too short to pick among them. P1.3 runs DINO's weights continued with SimSiam (the
    pre-registered vehicle), the MAE-started hybrid (the broadest linear gain) and MAE (does it adapt with a longer
    horizon, or regime by regime?) as peers, and chooses after. DINO's own objective is not carried forward. Recipe
    tuning moves to P1.3 too, at a horizon where the trade it makes can show. The fine-tuning protections are the
    exception: P1.2.5 tests them on both JE starts. A second MAE pass, from P1.2.2's repaired recipe, opens MAE's arm.
- **A forgetting lead that holds.** The correlated walk costs nearest-neighbour accuracy against its shuffled control,
    under both recipes. And the nearest-neighbour read erodes late in every SimSiam-objective drive, in every slice of
    the evaluation set. So P1.3 reads it through each drive, not only at its end, and the other readouts with it.
- **Read both geometry and information.** Linear probes say whether information survives; nearest-neighbour reads say
    whether the distances the selection filter uses survive. They disagreed throughout P1.2, and by objective.
- **A panel fixed in advance.** P1.2.5's wider, size-binned task panel and regime windows, so the small-object finding
    is tested rather than rediscovered; with dense checkpoints and a larger held-out pool for the rare objects.
- **Continuous learning still has to beat adapting once.** On a fixed domain, learning on the stream amounts to one
    offline adaptation on in-domain data. Adapting once on part of the stream, then freezing and running into the rest,
    is the control that tells them apart. It is a candidate P1.3 sub-study.
- **Readers P1.3 must build first.** A JE vehicle needs a label-free forgetting reader. The hybrid has none for losing
    its MAE-pretrained information, and P1.1's healthy bands are untested on a DINO start.

## The one-line takeaway

Continued learning on driving data beats a frozen encoder, even a strong one — for joint-embedding training, because of
the driving data, by about 2 points and on few tasks within one pass — while MAE, even with a repaired recipe, only
matches its frozen start; which backbone carries the project waits on P1.3's longer horizon.
