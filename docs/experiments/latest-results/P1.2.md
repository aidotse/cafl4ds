# P1.2 — does a backbone that keeps learning beat one left frozen?

Study-level summary of the P1.2 group (earn the right to *continue*): [P1.2.0](P1.2.0.md) the readout tools,
[P1.2.1](P1.2.1.md) the gate read, [P1.2.2](P1.2.2.md) the MAE recipe diagnosis, [P1.2.4](P1.2.4.md) attribution and
widening. [P1.2.3](../phase1/index.md) (the same question from other starting points) has not run yet. Detail lives in
the substudy distillates linked here and, one level down, in [`phase1/P1.2.{0,1,2,4}.md`](../phase1/index.md).

## The value proposition

The project assumes that a backbone living on a data stream should keep learning from it. Everything else it builds —
the selection filter, the health monitor, the controller that re-aims selection — exists to make that continued learning
safe. So the first existential question is blunt. Does continued self-supervised training on the stream actually beat
simply freezing a good off-the-shelf encoder?

Why this matters: if the frozen encoder is as good, a deployment should freeze it and skip the whole loop. The project
would then reframe to *selection for efficiency* rather than continual learning. This is leg (a) of the Phase-1 exit
gate.

The test had to be fair in three ways. The adapted and frozen encoders start from the identical weights, so only
adaptation differs. The comparison reads untouched evaluation frames, at several label budgets. And any gain must come
from the stream itself, not just from the training objective, and hold against stronger frozen encoders than the
starting one.

## Results

- **P1.2.0 — the tools, and a hidden artifact.** A readout ladder scores an encoder at three label budgets
    (nearest-neighbour, linear probe, short fine-tune), with intervals over seeds and images. Building it exposed an
    artifact: the early dip seen in every earlier continued-training run was mostly caused by randomly initialized
    training heads. From then on, heads are trained first with the encoder frozen. A second readout task — traffic-light
    presence among frames matched for scene and time of day — joined the scene label, because it has three times the
    room to improve.
- **P1.2.1 — the gate: JE passes, MAE fails.** After one pass over ~56,000 new driving frames, the joint-embedding
    backbone (JE) carries more linearly decodable information than its frozen start, on both tasks. The
    masked-autoencoder backbone (MAE) reads worse on everything. JE's correlated stream also reshaped its
    nearest-neighbour geometry at night, while the information held.
- **P1.2.2 — MAE's failure was the recipe, but fixing it is not enough.** The decoder used in training was far smaller
    than MAE's own design, and it damaged the encoder's last layers. With the proper decoder and fine-tuning
    protections, MAE stops losing to the frozen encoder, but it does not gain either. Under the pre-registered bars that
    is a FAIL: a No-Go for adapting MAE from its pretrained start, on this horizon.
- **P1.2.4 — JE's gain is from the stream, and it grows.** The same training on matched non-driving images recovers
    little of the gain (everyday photos) or about half of it (scenes without roads). The driving-specific remainder is
    significant against both. Fine-tuning protections lift the gain and broaden it to every kind of readout. The adapted
    encoder beats the best frozen backbone tried (DINO) on the scene task, though not on traffic lights.

**Leg (a) of the exit gate passes, on JE.** By P1.2.4's pre-registered table, the claim is "Strong": continuous learning
on the stream beats the best off-the-shelf backbone tried. Its honest scope is narrower than the words: the linear
probe, the scene task, a few points, one pass at 224 px.

**What P1.2 hands to P1.3.**

- **A decision on P1.3's vehicle.** P1.3 was planned around MAE. MAE's No-Go means that adapting it adds nothing to
    protect, so P1.2.2's design raises the choice. The recommendation is JE as P1.3's primary vehicle, with the
    protections recipe.
- **A weaker forgetting lead than P1.2.1 suggested.** JE's night-time nearest-neighbour loss appeared under the
    unprotected recipe. The protections recipe removes it (measured against the frozen encoder; the shuffled-order
    comparison was not re-run).
- **Read both geometry and information.** Linear probes say whether information survives; nearest-neighbour reads say
    whether the distances the selection filter uses survive. They disagreed more than once in P1.2.
- **Stronger frozen backbones exist.** DINO and a supervised ViT-B beat the starting encoder, most of all on
    nearest-neighbour reads. Whether adapting them would beat them in turn is open.

## The one-line takeaway

Continued learning on the driving stream does beat a frozen encoder — for the joint-embedding backbone, from the stream
itself, by a few points — while MAE, even with a repaired recipe, only matches its frozen start.
