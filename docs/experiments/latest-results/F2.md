## F2

From the [non-IID label-skew sub-study](../federated/F2.md) — the first FL study to ask a scientific rather than a
harness question: does Dirichlet label skew degrade the global representation of a warm-started MAE over a single
federated pass? Built on CIFAR-100 with a standard ViT-Tiny, four clients, FedAvg, and the averaging frequency bracketed
at its two extremes (average every batch vs. average once at the end).

**The headline is a design finding, not a result about heterogeneity.** The federated clients turn out to train on
images the warm-start pre-pass had already trained on several times, because both phases draw their splits from the same
full CIFAR-100 train set with the same seeding. The federated phase is therefore one more epoch over old data — worth
about as much as the noise floor — so label skew has almost nothing to degrade. As measured, IID and sharply skewed
partitions are indistinguishable, and the federated pass barely moves the probe at all; both follow from the same cause.
The question stays open pending a design where clients see data the warm start never did.

Run at two warm-start strengths, which function as an informal replication and **disagree on the sign of every apparent
effect** — both the label-skew direction and the averaging-frequency direction reverse between them. So the small
consistent-looking differences an earlier single base seemed to show were noise, and no claim about non-IID survives.
The one reproducible movement is that RankMe expands in every arm, by well over the probe noise and independently of
partition or averaging schedule — the federated pass demonstrably changes the representation's geometry without reaching
downstream accuracy.

Two findings that do stand. First, a **methodological fix now applied to the harness**: warm starts restored only the
encoder, leaving MAE's decoder random, and the resulting warm-up transient — audit P0.3's confound, reappearing in the
federated setting — cratered and then repaired the probe across most of the run, dominating whatever the partition did.
Checkpointing the objective's heads alongside the encoder removes it, and `pretrain.py` now does so by default. Second,
**reconstruction loss is anti-correlated with skew** and must not be read as global health here: less diverse shards
make the local MAE task easier, so loss *improves* under skew for reasons unrelated to representation quality.

Also established along the way: a centralized IID reference showing MAE does learn usefully at this scale, the measured
sharpness of `Dirichlet(0.1)` over 100 classes, and a negative result that single-pass from-scratch SSL reaches
essentially the frozen random-init floor — which is why the `I=pretrained` warm start is load-bearing for every
streaming and federated arm. SimSiam is not covered: it point-collapses at this encoder scale without the warmup+cosine
schedule, which is its own open thread.
