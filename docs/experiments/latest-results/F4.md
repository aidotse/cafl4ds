## F4

From the [BDD100K condition-split sub-study](../federated/F4.md). F3 found that label skew is too weak to matter for a
label-free objective. F4 tries a stronger kind of skew: BDD100K driving images, with each client holding different
conditions (clear daytime, clear night, snow and rain). The probes predict the scene, an axis the split does not sort
by. Results are averaged over three seeds and scored on BDD's test split.

**Federated MAE improves a little, reliably.** Plain FedAvg, starting from a warm-started MAE, gains a small amount on
the test split in every seed, with little variation between seeds.

**Splitting clients by condition costs nothing here.** FedAvg ends at the same test score whether each client holds one
kind of weather or a fair mix of everything. The clients do drift further apart under the condition split, but averaging
removes the difference. The likely reason is that the setup is gentle: the starting model already knows every condition,
clients train only a few steps between averages, and the global model barely moves. More local training per round is the
direct test.

**Plain FedAvg again beats the alternatives.** FedProx and FedAdam fall behind FedAvg in every seed. With no non-IID
cost to correct, FedProx only slows the clients. FedAdam's server step is either too large (the model jumps away from
the starting point and gains nothing) or too small (it learns too little).

**The measurement needed fixing first.** City street is most of BDD's test images, so plain accuracy rewards guessing
it. F4 adds balanced accuracy, which caught one method (FedYogi) whose plain-accuracy gain was really a shift toward the
majority scene. The per-round probes needed a larger hold-out to be readable, and even then only the end-of-run
evaluation resolves gains this small.

The starting model is weak, which may cap what any federated pass can add. Within-client order was sorted by scene, a
setting shared by every arm.
