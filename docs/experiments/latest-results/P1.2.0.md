## P1.2.0

From [the readout ladder and the frozen floor](phase1/P1.2.0.md) — the first P1.2 piece. P1.2 asks whether a backbone
that keeps learning on the driving stream ends up better than the same backbone frozen. That frozen floor (**B5**) is
the off-the-shelf pretrained encoder, untouched by any driving data. P1.2.0 builds the measuring tools that question
needs, tests them on P1.1's saved models, and settles three design questions before the real test, P1.2.1, runs.

**The approach.** A "readout ladder" scores any encoder at three label budgets: a nearest-neighbour read and a linear
probe on frozen features, and a short fine-tune on a few labelled images per class. Each comparison with the frozen
floor carries an interval that accounts for both seed-to-seed spread and the particular images scored. Around that,
three checks: does a short fine-tune change the picture; was the familiar early dip in continued training caused by
randomly initialized training heads; and is there a readout task with more room to improve than the scene label?

**What it found.**

- **The early dip was largely the training heads.** Every warm-up so far bolted fresh, random heads onto the pretrained
    encoder. Training the heads first, with the encoder frozen, removes the joint-embedding backbone's dip entirely, and
    about half of MAE's. So part of what looked like the cost of continued training was an artifact, and P1.2.1 starts
    with settled heads.
- **The scene task has little room, so a second task joins it.** A frozen encoder already gets close to what full
    fine-tuning reaches on the scene label. Full-frame object tasks turned out to be the scene in disguise: at the image
    size used, traffic lights and pedestrians are only a few pixels wide. A task balanced so that scene and time of day
    say nothing about it — is there a traffic light, among otherwise-matched frames — has about three times the room. It
    joins the scene as a co-primary readout.
- **Squashing the wide frames into squares does not hurt the frozen floor.** Cropping instead, to keep the shape, did
    not help it. The squash stays, noted as a possible way for adaptation to look better than it is.
- **On P1.1's models, the frozen floor already wins for MAE.** Models trained offline on driving data lose to the
    untouched encoder on every MAE reading, and the short fine-tune widens the gap rather than closing it. The
    joint-embedding backbone gains a little on frozen reads and loses under fine-tuning. P1.1's short drive changes
    nothing on top. These models carry the head artifact above, so this is a warning for P1.2.1, not its answer.
