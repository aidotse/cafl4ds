## P1.2.2

From [the MAE recipe diagnosis](phase1/P1.2.2.md) — the P1.2 piece triggered by MAE's failure in P1.2.1. There, the
masked-autoencoder backbone (MAE) ended a full pass of new driving frames worse than the same encoder left frozen. MAE
was then P1.3's planned primary vehicle, so the question had to be settled first: is continued MAE training simply bad
on this data, or was the training recipe at fault? This is pure MAE throughout, its own weights trained with its own
objective, not the hybrid that P1.2.1 calls the joint-embedding backbone.

**The approach.** As the learning rate goes to zero, the adapted encoder *is* the frozen one. So a clear loss means the
recipe is moving the encoder somewhere harmful. Each suspect got one training run: a zero-rate run to certify the
pipeline, a lower and a higher learning rate, a gentle start, fine-tuning protections that keep the early layers near
their pretrained state, and a larger decoder. The decoder is the small network that turns the encoder's features back
into pixels during training. Every run was compared on frames reserved from the training split. The best recipe was then
run five times and read once on the untouched evaluation set, against P1.2.1's bars as written.

**What it found.**

- **The pipeline is clean.** A full run at zero learning rate returns the starting model bit for bit, and every readout
    matches the frozen encoder exactly.
- **The decoder was the culprit.** The original decoder is far smaller than the one MAE was designed with. Every recipe
    that kept it lost to the frozen encoder, whatever the learning rate or protections. The full-size decoder alone
    removed most of the loss. The damage sat in the encoder's last layers, most likely because a decoder too weak to
    rebuild pixels pushes the encoder itself toward pixel detail. Phase 0 met the same lever from the other side: there,
    an over-powerful decoder let the encoder learn too little. The decoder has to fit the encoder.
- **Fixed, MAE matches the frozen encoder but does not beat it.** The confirmed recipe's loss is gone on the primary
    measure, but there is no gain either. On the scene task's cruder measures it still reads slightly below. Under the
    pre-registered bars that is a FAIL: every measure rules out a meaningful gain.
- **What it means.** By the pre-registered bars, adapting MAE from its pretrained start does not pay on this horizon.
    The verdict covers one pass over a fixed domain: the evaluation frames share the training frames' distribution. But
    one setting turned out to matter a lot, which suggests others might too. MAE stays in the programme in a new role.
    P1.3 now studies the backbones that adapt: the joint-embedding backbone, which gains, is its forgetting vehicle, and
    MAE is its plasticity arm. The stream did shift, but one read at the end of the pass could not see adaptation to a
    shift; and the repaired MAE was still recovering from an early dip when the pass ended. So P1.3 asks whether MAE
    adapts with a longer horizon, and regime by regime as the stream moves. A second MAE pass, starting from the
    repaired recipe, opens that arm.
